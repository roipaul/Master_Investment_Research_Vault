**Provisional baseline (Claude's framing, not Paul's view).** At 16.9 times guided 2026 adjusted earnings, on an operating margin of about 6% where 2019-2023 ran at 8.1-8.8%, the return depends on five things: (1) margins in Medicaid, commercial insurance and Optum Health recovering to the company's own target ranges by about 2028, through repricing and exits, at the cost of members; (2) the Medicare Advantage margin holding inside 2-4% against what the government does to funding: the rate, the risk-adjustment rules, Star Ratings and audits; (3) Optum Rx and Optum Insight holding their earnings while the pharmacy-benefit model moves to full rebate pass-through; (4) earnings turning into cash, with reserves kept whole, debt-to-capital near 40% and the surplus returned; (5) no legal or regulatory outcome (Justice Department inquiries, retroactive audit recoveries) that takes years of earnings or forces a change in how the insurer and Optum work together.

What to track: the medical care ratio against the guide; cost trend against pricing by line; Medicare Advantage margin, membership and Star Ratings; the Medicaid margin and state rate updates; the commercial margin; the Optum Health margin and patient count; Optum Rx scripts, retention and margin; Optum Insight backlog; days claims payable and reserve development; operating cash flow against net income; debt-to-capital and buybacks; the audit rule; the Justice Department matters.

## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**A. Medicare Advantage: a smaller, better-priced book facing a harder regulator.**
- What changed: membership down 840 thousand this year by design, and the 2026 margin near 4%. Against that: Star Ratings for 2027 put "at least 69%" of members in four-star plans (press: 78-81% the year before); the 2027 rate notice gives +2.48% on average but stops counting diagnoses from unlinked chart reviews (-1.53% for the industry); and after the print the agency proposed extrapolated audit recoveries from payment year 2018 with no adjustment for errors in traditional Medicare. UnitedHealthcare sued over the ratings on 19 November.
- Puts: the book has been repriced once and can be again; the footprint for 2027 widens to 94% of eligible people while rivals also cut benefits; an earlier version of the audit rule was struck down in September 2025 and the appeal court seemed sceptical in October (press); costs known in advance go into bids.
- Takes: lower ratings cut 2028 bonus payments; retroactive recoveries cannot be priced; the largest plan with the most home-visit and chart-review coding has the most at stake (inference); a further draft would reportedly cut the ratings to 15 measures and take US$107bn from insurers over ten years.
- Net effect: not sized by the company. Each point of margin on about US$169bn of Medicare & Retirement revenue is about US$1.7bn, about US$1.50 a share. Going from the top of the range to the middle costs about US$1.2 a share (e). Retroactive recoveries would be a balance-sheet matter: at a hypothetical fifth to a quarter of the reported US$9bn industry figure, about US$2bn per payment year, or about US$2 a share before tax for each year reached. UnitedHealth's share of industry enrolment is not on file, so this is an illustration and not an estimate.
- Updates: legs 2 and 5. Confidence: low. Settles it: the 2027 outlook; the company's comment letter; the Fifth Circuit ruling; the final rule.

**B. Optum Health: smaller and partly repaired.**
- What changed: revenue -10% on fewer value-based patients; margin 3.2% (2.6% adjusted) from 0.2%; a date, 2028, now attached to the 6-8% target; the fourth quarter planned at a loss.
- Puts: about 4 points of margin to recover on about US$93bn of revenue, about US$4bn (e); loss contracts are running off.
- Takes: the business depends on the same Medicare funding as A; the patient count is no longer disclosed; the target was 8-10% in 2024.
- Net effect: about US$3.5 a share at the middle of the target (e), over 2027-2029.
- Updates: leg 1. Confidence: medium on direction, low on timing. Confirms: a 2027 margin guide above 4%.

**C. Optum Rx: more external, less rebate.**
- What changed: more than half of members are now outside UnitedHealthcare; retention in the high 90s; 100% rebate pass-through promised by 1 January 2028; the February 2026 law delinks Part D fees from list prices from 2028; scripts -5% as UnitedHealthcare shrinks.
- Puts: margin 4.3%, inside the 3-5% target, with specialty volumes growing.
- Takes: Cigna's pharmacy-benefit income fell 22% in Q3 on contract renewals and it guides down again for 2027; CVS calls 2027 the trough for its health-services arm. The pool that funded the old model is shrinking for everyone.
- Net effect: the guide of above US$6.25bn for 2026 has not moved all year. Treated as flat in the base case.
- Updates: leg 3. Confidence: medium.

**D. Capital and portfolio.**
- What changed: US$5.0bn of buybacks in nine months and a new 50m-share authorisation; debt-to-capital from 43.9% to 41.7%; South America and Optum UK sold or being sold; a US$3.0bn acquisition.
- Puts: 6.4m shares were bought at US$313 through forwards; the diluted share count fell 0.7% in the last year and can fall faster.
- Takes: the open-market purchases in Q3 were at US$410, 18% above today's price; all three rating agencies have the company on negative outlook; almost all the cash sits in regulated subsidiaries.
- Net effect: buybacks of US$5-8bn a year retire 1.5-2.5% of the shares at today's price.
- Updates: leg 4. Confidence: high.

**E. Less disclosure under the returning chief executive.** The outlook table, the backlog, the patient count and return on equity have all gone from the release in 2026, while "adjusted" operating earnings have arrived. This lowers the quality of the evidence on legs 1 and 3 and is a finding in itself.

**Unresolved: structural or cyclical.** Commercial cost trend above 11%. Management's case is that arbitration awards under the No Surprises Act ("nearly 6% of total medical spend in some regions") are a new cost; if so, margins of 7-9% need either a change in the law or permanently higher premiums. The reading that settles it: the 2027 commercial margin guide, and whether Elevance and Cigna, which say trend is in line with their pricing, report the same thing.
