# 1. Company Overview

*Source limitations and dating.* This primer is current to November 24, 2026. Meituan's Q3 2026 results are scheduled for November 27, 2026 [32], so the latest reported period here is Q2 2026 (interim results of August 28, 2026). All company financials come from Meituan's HKEX filings: the FY2025 results announcement [1], the 2025 Annual Report [2], the Q1 2026 announcement [3] (the HKEX document as mirrored by AASTOCKS), the interim 2026 announcement [4], the Q3 2025 announcement [5] and the FY2024 announcement [6]. Two gaps matter. First, Meituan does not disclose order volumes, GTV, transacting users, food-delivery market share or per-order unit economics in any of these filings; every such figure in this document comes from broker estimates or press reports and is labelled secondary. Second, the earnings-call material used for management commentary [7][8] is a third-party transcription of the call, not a company document. The full 2025 Annual Report was read in parts; its Note 7 (expenses by nature), Note 5 (segments), the cash flow statement and the Report of Directors were reviewed, but some notes were not.

## 1.1 Meituan (3690.HK) — Company Primer

### Company Overview

Meituan is China's largest platform for local services: it connects consumers with nearby restaurants, shops, hotels and service businesses, and in the case of food delivery and on-demand retail it also employs (through contractors) the courier network that physically moves the goods. Its stated mission is to help people "eat better, live better" [2]. The company is incorporated in the Cayman Islands and listed on the Main Board of the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (stock code 3690, with an RMB counter) under a weighted-voting-rights (WVR) structure [2]. Co-founder Wang Xing is Chairman and CEO; Mu Rongjun, the other WVR holder, is an executive director; Chen Shaohui is CFO; Wang Puzhong runs Core Local Commerce [2]. No succession has been announced.

Scale, as of the end of 2025: revenue of RMB364.9bn, up 8.1% [1]; approximately 111,000–112,000 full-time employees (the MD&A gives 111,298, the sustainability table 112,123) [2]; more than 8 million merchants across 200+ in-store categories, per management on the Q2 2026 call [7]; and, per press reports of the Q3 2025 results, more than 800 million annual transacting users [35] (secondary; Meituan's filings describe user counts only as "record highs" [5]). The group swung from a RMB35.8bn profit in 2024 to a RMB23.4bn loss in 2025 because of the food-delivery subsidy war with Alibaba and JD.com [1]. It returned to a small profit in Q2 2026 (net profit RMB2.2bn; adjusted net profit RMB2.5bn) [4].

The core model is a three-sided local marketplace: consumers, merchants and couriers. Meituan earns money in four ways — delivery fees, merchant commissions, merchant advertising, and (increasingly) the sale of goods it owns — and its competitive position rests on density: the more orders in a neighbourhood, the cheaper each delivery, the more merchants want to list, and the more consumers find what they want. Management's current framing is "Retail + Technology": deepen supply-chain penetration, expand Meituan Membership, invest with discipline in grocery and overseas, and build AI assistants for local services [1][2]. In 2026 it added a further line — that it will not chase subsidy-driven volume, and that AI spending will be judged on return on investment [7].

### Segments

Meituan reports two operating segments plus an unallocated line [2]:

- **Core Local Commerce (CLC).** Food delivery (Meituan Waimai), Meituan Instashopping (on-demand non-food and grocery delivered from third-party stores and Meituan's own "Instant Marts"), and In-store, Hotel and Travel (the Meituan and Dianping apps: vouchers, group-buy deals, reservations, hotel and attraction bookings, merchant advertising). Revenue in 2025: RMB260.8bn, 71.5% of the group; operating loss RMB6.9bn (–2.6% margin) after a RMB52.4bn profit (20.9% margin) in 2024 [1][2].
- **New Initiatives (NI).** Everything else: Keeta (food delivery outside the Mainland — Hong Kong, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Kuwait, the UAE, Bahrain and Brazil), Xiaoxiang Supermarket (self-operated grocery with front warehouses and, since December 2025, a handful of offline stores), Kuailv (B2B supply for restaurants), Happy Monkey (快乐猴, a discount-store format with 40 stores at Q2 2026), bike-sharing and power-bank rental, the restaurant SaaS, and until 2025 Meituan Select (community group-buy, now discontinued) [1][2][7]. Revenue in 2025: RMB104.0bn, 28.5% of the group; operating loss RMB10.1bn (–9.7% margin) [1].
- **Unallocated items.** Share-based compensation, amortisation of acquired intangibles, other gains, and since 2025 corporate-level AI investment and regulatory fines (a RMB745.7m SAMR fine was booked here in Q1 2026) [1][3].

The table hides the most important fact about the segment structure: CLC combines two businesses with very different economics. Food delivery is a logistics business with thin per-order profits even in good years. In-store, Hotel and Travel is an advertising-and-commission business with no logistics cost, which press estimates put at 20–30% operating margins in Q1 2026 [9]. Meituan does not report the two separately, so the CLC margin is a blend whose movements can reflect mix as much as performance. Management has said in-store margins improved sequentially in Q2 2026 but will decline in H2 2026 as it raises investment against Douyin [7].

**Segment and revenue-line changes that break comparisons.** Two reporting changes matter for anyone using historical numbers:

1. *2023 reorganisation.* The current two-segment structure (CLC versus NI) dates from the 2023 restructuring; the earlier three-segment structure (Food Delivery; In-store, Hotel & Travel; New Initiatives) gave a cleaner split of food delivery and in-store profit that is no longer available [6].
2. *Q1 2026 revenue-type reclassification.* From Q1 2026 Meituan reports revenue by type as delivery services, merchant services (commission plus online marketing combined), product sales (grocery retail — Xiaoxiang, Kuailv — and self-operated medicine and alcohol, recognised gross on delivery) and others (including interest) [3]. Prior periods were restated. Because commission and online marketing are now combined, the ability to track advertising separately ends with the FY2025 report. The restatement also slightly changed 2025 quarterly totals (Q1 2025 revenue is shown as RMB86.2bn in the Q1 2026 announcement, against RMB86.6bn originally) [3].

### How the Business Model Works

#### 1. Where revenue comes from

Meituan's revenue is recognised on four bases, and the mix determines both growth and margin [1][3]:

- **Delivery services (RMB96.1bn in 2025, 26% of group revenue).** The fee for delivering a food-delivery order when Meituan's own courier network does the delivery. Meituan recognises the delivery fee as revenue, net of consumer incentives that are treated as reductions of revenue. This is why the subsidy war showed up first as falling delivery revenue: delivery services revenue fell 2.0% in 2025 even as order volumes rose, and fell 17.1% year on year in Q3 2025 at the peak of subsidies [1][5]. When subsidies were cut in 2026, delivery revenue recovered to +17.5% in Q2 2026 [4].
- **Commission (RMB105.5bn in 2025) and online marketing (RMB51.9bn) — combined from 2026 as "merchant services" (RMB157.4bn).** Commission is a percentage of the order value that merchants pay on food delivery, Instashopping and in-store transactions. Online marketing is advertising bought by merchants: ranking placement in search and feeds, promoted listings on Dianping, and performance-based ads [1]. Merchant services grew 6.6% in Q2 2026 [4].
- **Product sales.** Goods Meituan buys and resells — Xiaoxiang Supermarket and Kuailv, plus self-operated pharmacy and alcohol. Recognised gross, so revenue is large relative to profit. Product sales grew 46.6% in Q1 2026 and 48.8% in Q2 2026 to RMB26.7bn, making them the fastest-growing revenue line [3][4].
- **Others (including interest).** Bike-sharing and power-bank fees, SaaS, interest income on user funds and the micro-credit business, and other services. This line fell 25.3% in Q1 2026 and 23.5% in Q2 2026, mostly reflecting the closure of Meituan Select [3][4].

The 1P/3P distinction matters here. In food delivery, Meituan is an agent: it does not record the meal's value as revenue, only its commission, advertising and delivery fee. A RMB40 meal might generate a few yuan of commission and a delivery fee of similar size, so revenue is a small fraction of gross transaction value. In grocery (Xiaoxiang, Kuailv, Dingdong once closed), Meituan is principal and books the whole basket. The rising share of product sales therefore inflates revenue growth relative to the platform's underlying take, and lowers reported margins mechanically. Readers comparing 2026 growth with 2024 should keep this mix effect in mind.

Meituan's "expenses by nature" note gives the clearest view of the cost base [2]:

| Employee benefits | 47.2 | 45.2 | +4.5% |

| Depreciation of PP&E | 9.9 | 8.2 | +21.4% |

*Source: [2], Note 7. User incentives deducted from revenue are not included in the promotion line; they reduce revenue directly.*

Three costs dominate:

- **Logistics** (the courier network) is the largest cost and is almost entirely variable with orders. It is paid per delivery to riders engaged through third-party labour-service providers, plus peak-hour and weather incentives, and, from 2025–26, platform-funded social insurance: a pension-insurance subsidy rolled out nationwide in 2025, and occupational-injury insurance that covered more than 16 million couriers by end-2025 and the whole platform from July 1, 2026 [1][4][7]. Logistics cost rose 25% in 2025 — faster than revenue — because order volumes rose (many of them low-ticket subsidised orders), courier incentives rose during the war, and the new insurance layer was added [1][2].
- **Promotion and incentives** nearly doubled in 2025, to RMB74.5bn [2]. Together with incentives booked as revenue reductions, this is where the subsidy war lives in the accounts.
- **Transaction costs** include the cost of goods sold in grocery, so they scale with product sales.

**Per-order economics of food delivery.** Meituan does not publish them, but the shape is well understood and was the central variable of 2025–26. Revenue per order is the delivery fee plus commission plus advertising, net of user incentives. Cost per order is mainly the rider cost, plus payment processing, customer service and allocated overhead. J.P. Morgan estimates Meituan's fully loaded fulfilment cost at about RMB6.3–6.5 per order, with rivals RMB0.3–0.5 higher because of lower order density [11] (secondary). Press estimates put Meituan's per-order loss at a peak of RMB3–4 in 2025, narrowing to RMB1.0–1.1 in Q1 2026 [9][30] and to a small positive figure (around RMB0.16) in Q2 2026 [11] (all secondary). Management confirmed that food-delivery unit economics turned positive in Q2 2026 and guided that Q3 2026 would improve year on year but decline sequentially while staying positive [7]. Before the war, the industry rule of thumb was that Meituan earned roughly RMB1 of operating profit per order; Huxiu observed in 2026 that Wang Xing no longer cites that figure [9][7] (secondary).

What determines unit economics:
- **Order density.** More orders per rider-hour within a zone means lower cost per delivery. This is why Wang Huiwen, a Meituan co-founder, described food delivery as a business of city-by-city "cells" in which scale only works inside each cell — a competitor can fight one city at a time.
- **Average order value (AOV).** Commission scales with AOV; rider cost does not. The war pushed the mix toward low-ticket drinks and snacks, which cost as much to deliver but earn less. Meituan says it kept over 70% of orders with an actual payment of RMB30 or more [9] (secondary, citing management), and at its October 2026 conference said the share of restaurant orders priced below RMB15 had halved year on year [13].
- **Subsidy intensity.** Direct incentives per order — the swing factor of 2025.
- **Courier welfare costs.** Social insurance and heat/peak subsidies, now structural.

**In-store, Hotel and Travel economics** are simpler. Merchants pay commission on vouchers redeemed and buy advertising to rank higher on Dianping and Meituan's in-store pages. There is no delivery cost, so incremental revenue drops largely to profit. The risk is price: Douyin pays merchant subsidies that per Leifeng Network reach 6–7.5% in contested cities and twice Meituan's rate in some core cities [22] (secondary), and the competitive response is to spend on user incentives and merchant support rather than to cut commission.

#### 3. The demand engine

Demand for Meituan's services is consumption-driven but less cyclical than discretionary retail:
- **Food delivery** is habitual and high-frequency. China's delivery market reached RMB1.42 trillion in 2025 (up 11.7%), 24.5% of all catering revenue, with 630 million users, per Red Meal data [13] (secondary). Structural drivers are urban single households, office lunch demand, and the shift of meals from dine-in to delivery; cyclical drivers are consumer confidence and restaurant pricing. Q2 and Q3 are seasonally strongest (summer drinks, heat).
- **Instashopping and grocery** ride the instant-retail trend: consumers buying groceries, electronics, pharmacy and daily goods for delivery within 30–60 minutes. The Ministry of Commerce's research institute sized instant retail at RMB781bn in 2024 (up 20.2%) and expects it to exceed RMB1 trillion in 2026 and reach about RMB2 trillion by 2030 [14].
- **In-store, Hotel and Travel** is more discretionary: dining out, leisure services (beauty, KTV, fitness, massage), domestic hotels and attractions. It is tied to consumer confidence and holiday patterns.

Meituan does not split revenue into recurring and discretionary. Practically, food delivery, grocery and pharmacy behave like staples; in-store, hotel and travel behave like discretionary consumption.

#### 4. The reinforcing structure

The Meituan ecosystem works through shared assets rather than a single flywheel:
- **One app, one wallet, many categories.** The Meituan app carries food delivery, Instashopping, in-store deals, hotels, bikes and grocery. High-frequency food delivery acquires and retains users cheaply; lower-frequency, higher-margin categories (hotels, in-store) are cross-sold to them. Meituan Membership, upgraded in 2025, binds the categories together [1][2].
- **Shared courier network.** The same riders deliver meals, groceries, pharmacy items and Instashopping orders, so non-food orders raise density and lower cost per order for food. This is the strategic logic of Instashopping and the Dingdong acquisition: more categories through the same network.
- **Merchant digitisation.** Meituan's merchant SaaS, POS systems, Kuailv supply and AI tools (Smart Manager for over 700,000 restaurant merchants; Digital Staff for over 300,000 in-store merchants as of Q1 2026) embed Meituan in merchant operations beyond order-taking [3].
- **Dianping's reviews.** A decade of user-generated reviews is the discovery layer for in-store spending, and the reason Alibaba's Amap and Douyin target it [25].

The vulnerability of this structure is that each layer is replicable in isolation by a rich competitor — Alibaba can subsidise food delivery, Douyin can subsidise in-store vouchers, Amap can host rankings — and 2025 showed that rivals are willing to try all three at once.

#### 5. Go-to-market and transaction model

Meituan reaches consumers directly through its apps and through mini-programs inside WeChat; merchants sign up through Meituan's local sales teams (historically one of China's largest ground forces) and, increasingly, self-onboard. Food delivery runs two fulfilment modes: Meituan's own courier network (Meituan Delivery) and merchant self-delivery, where the merchant uses its own or a third-party courier and Meituan earns commission only. Meituan also proposed buying Maiyatian, a delivery-aggregation service that dispatches orders across delivery providers; SAMR said in October 2026 the combined share would exceed 50% and that the deal could restrict competition [20] (secondary).

#### 6. Key operating metrics

Meituan has steadily reduced KPI disclosure. Filings now describe users, merchants, transactions and GTV only qualitatively ("record high", "grew steadily") [3][4][5]. Metrics the company discussed in 2025–26 include:
- **Core local commerce operating margin** — the headline profitability measure.
- **Food delivery unit economics (UE)** — discussed directionally on calls, never quantified [7].
- **Annual active advertising merchants** — growth mentioned in Q1 2026 [3].
- **Keeta UE by market** — e.g. Hong Kong UE profitable from October 2025, Saudi Arabia from July 2026 [7].

For volume and share, readers must rely on third-party estimates (Part 3).

#### 7. Funding and capital model

Meituan funds itself from operating cash flow, treasury investments and the bond market. At June 30, 2026 it held RMB104.7bn of cash, RMB63.6bn of short-term treasury investments and RMB21.9bn of restricted cash, against RMB42.1bn of borrowings and RMB46.6bn of notes payable [4]. It redeemed US$1.48bn of 2028 zero-coupon convertible bonds in April 2026 [3]. It also holds a portfolio of strategic stakes — Li Auto (12.66%, equity-accounted), Z.AI/Zhipu (3.86%) and Unitree Robotics (7.61%) — that management valued at about RMB77.3bn at June 30, 2026 and said it is "very open" to monetising [4][7].

### Corporate History

Meituan's history is a sequence of land-grabs, consolidations and retrenchments. The consequential events:

- **2010 — founding in group-buying.** Wang Xing founded Meituan in Beijing in 2010 during China's "thousand group-buy war"; it emerged as the survivor [29].
- **2013 — food delivery.** Meituan launched food delivery in 2013 [29], entering a market led by Ele.me and Baidu Waimai. It became the leader through a multi-year subsidy war, and Ele.me was acquired by Alibaba in 2018.
- **October 2015 — merger with Dianping.** The merger with Dianping, China's leading restaurant review site, created the in-store and reviews franchise that underpins today's highest-margin business. The merged company raised more than US$3.3bn in January 2016 [29].
- **2018 — Mobike and IPO.** Meituan acquired Mobike for US$2.7bn and listed in Hong Kong on September 20, 2018 at HK$69 per share [29]. Mobike was a loss-making asset that was later written down and folded into Meituan Bike.
- **2020–2025 — Meituan Select.** Launched in 2020, the community group-buy business absorbed heavy investment and was wound down in 2025 [2][9][29]. Wang Xing later said the direction was right but the model flawed, because non-standard goods turned competition into price-only [9] (secondary). The Happy Monkey format is described as applying that lesson [9].
- **2021 — antitrust.** SAMR opened an antitrust investigation in April 2021 and later fined Meituan for "choose one of two" exclusivity practices; the episode wiped tens of billions of dollars off the market value [29].
- **2023 — Keeta.** Keeta launched in Hong Kong in May 2023, Meituan's first service outside the Mainland; Saudi Arabia followed in September 2024, Qatar, Kuwait and the UAE in H2 2025, Brazil in October 2025 and Bahrain in January 2026 [1][27][29].
- **2025–26 — the delivery war.** JD.com entered food delivery in February 2025 and Alibaba escalated through Taobao Instant Commerce. Meituan's CLC swung from a RMB52.4bn profit in 2024 to a RMB6.9bn loss in 2025 [1]. Regulators intervened from January 2026 (Part 3).
- **February 2026 — Dingdong.** Meituan agreed to acquire Dingdong Fresh's China business for an initial US$717m, excluding Dingdong's overseas operations, with a twelve-month long-stop date [1][26]. The Q1 and Q2 2026 announcements do not mention completion [3][4].

# 2. Company Logistics

## 2.1 Customers and Distribution

### Who Are the Customers

Meituan has three customer groups, and each pays in a different way.

**Consumers.** Consumers pay delivery fees on food-delivery orders (net of incentives, which Meituan records as revenue reductions) and pay for goods Meituan sells as principal (Xiaoxiang, pharmacy, alcohol) [1][3]. Meituan does not disclose the number of transacting users in its 2025–26 filings, describing them only as at record highs [1][5]; press reports of the Q3 2025 results put annual transacting users above 800 million [35] (secondary). Meituan says the core user base "grew steadily", with more low- and medium-frequency users moving to high frequency, and that food-delivery DAU and MTU reached all-time highs in Q3 2025 [5]. In Q2 2026 management said it extended its lead in both order volume and GTV, especially in mid-to-high AOV segments, and credited enhanced Meituan Membership benefits for premium-user engagement [7]. Younger consumers are cited as the strongest new-user cohort in Instashopping [4]. There is no consumer concentration.

**Merchants.** Restaurants, retailers, pharmacies and service businesses pay commission and buy advertising (together "merchant services", RMB80.4bn in H1 2026, 41% of revenue) [4]. Management cited more than 8 million merchants across over 200 in-store categories on the Q2 2026 call [7]. The merchant base is fragmented: per a service provider quoted by Huxiu, mid- and tail-tier merchants account for over 90% of Meituan's in-store revenue, a more long-tail structure than Douyin's [22] (secondary). Merchants increasingly multi-home: QuestMobile counted 7.44 million merchants operating on more than one delivery platform in July–September 2025, up 26.8% year on year [10] (secondary). Large chains (tea and coffee brands, fast-food chains) carry more negotiating power and run their own membership programmes.

**Couriers** are not customers but are a constituency that shapes the business model and the regulatory relationship: Meituan funds pension subsidies and occupational-injury insurance and pays peak and heat incentives [1][4][7].

**Concentration.** The five largest customers accounted for less than 30% of revenue in 2025 [2]. In practice no customer is material; the threshold disclosure is a listing-rule formality.

### Distribution Channels

Meituan distributes its services almost entirely through its own channels:

- **The Meituan app** — the primary channel for food delivery, Instashopping, in-store deals, hotels and grocery.
- **The Dianping app** — discovery and reviews for in-store, with its own deal and reservation functions; in September 2025 Dianping relaunched a "Premium Delivery" service offering 30-minute delivery from restaurants on its "Must Eat" and "Black Pearl" lists [25] (secondary).
- **WeChat mini-programs** — access inside Tencent's ecosystem.
- **Keeta apps** — separate apps in each overseas market.
- **Offline** — Xiaoxiang's first offline stores (Beijing December 2025, Ningbo April 2026, Hangzhou July 2026, a second Ningbo and a first Shenzhen store in August 2026) and Happy Monkey's 40 stores [7].

On the merchant side, Meituan's sales force signs and services merchants, supplemented by city-level service providers and self-onboarding. In October 2026 Meituan said it plans to build about 1,000 "super kitchens" with restaurant brands [13] (secondary), and in Q2 2026 it was extending digital-transformation programmes to 400 county-level regions covering more than 500,000 merchants [8].

Channel shifts under way:
1. **Discounting retreat.** After regulators' intervention, Meituan cut broad subsidies and shifted to targeted incentives, membership benefits and higher-AOV orders [4][7][13].
2. **Merchant self-delivery versus platform delivery.** Aggregators such as Maiyatian let merchants route orders across delivery providers; Meituan's proposed acquisition of Maiyatian was flagged by SAMR in October 2026 as potentially restricting competition [20] (secondary).
3. **AI front-ends.** Meituan's consumer assistant (Xiaotuan) and merchant agents (CatPaw platform) aim to keep the user relationship inside Meituan as AI assistants become a new entry point [4][7].

Meituan discloses no channel-level KPIs.

## 2.2 Suppliers and Procurement

Meituan's largest "suppliers" are not goods vendors but service providers:

- **Courier labour.** Logistics expenses of RMB155.1bn in 2025 (+24.9%) were the single largest cost, over 40% of revenue [2]. Riders are engaged mostly through third-party labour-service and logistics partners; Meituan sets pay rates, incentives and dispatch through its algorithm. Since 2025 Meituan has funded rider pension subsidies nationwide and occupational-injury insurance, extended platform-wide from July 1, 2026 [1][4][7].
- **Goods vendors (grocery and pharmacy).** Transaction costs, which include the cost of inventories sold, were RMB69.2bn in 2025 (+36.5%) [2]. Xiaoxiang and Kuailv buy fresh produce, packaged goods and restaurant supplies; Xiaoxiang is raising its private-label share [4][7].
- **Outsourcing** (customer service, operations, sales support): RMB12.7bn in 2025 [2].
- **Technology and cloud.** Meituan runs largely on its own infrastructure; R&D was RMB26.0bn in 2025 (7.1% of revenue), rising in 2026 on corporate AI investment [1][4]. LongCat 2.0 was described by management as trained entirely on Chinese infrastructure [7].
- **Payments.** Consumers pay through Meituan Pay, WeChat Pay and Alipay; processing fees sit in transaction costs, but Meituan does not disclose them separately [2].

Concentration: the five largest suppliers accounted for less than 30% of purchases in 2025 [2]. No commodity-level procurement detail, long-term supply contract or hedging programme is disclosed.

Tariff and trade exposure is minimal because nearly all revenue is domestic service revenue [2]. Keeta's overseas operations carry foreign-exchange exposure (HKD, SAR, AED, BRL), which Meituan does not quantify.

# 3. Industry Overview

## 3.1 Industry Overview

### Introduction

China's "local services" industry covers three overlapping markets: **on-demand food delivery** (restaurant meals delivered in about 30 minutes), **instant retail** (groceries, pharmacy, alcohol, electronics and daily goods delivered from nearby stores or warehouses within about an hour), and **in-store services** (discovery, booking and discounted vouchers for restaurants, leisure, beauty, hotels and attractions consumed on site). The first two are logistics businesses layered on top of a marketplace; the third is a pure advertising-and-commission marketplace.

The industry exists because of China's urban density and labour costs. Tens of millions of consumers live within a few kilometres of thousands of restaurants and shops, and a large pool of riders on e-bikes makes delivery cheap — typically a few yuan per order. Platforms aggregate demand (consumers), supply (merchants) and fulfilment (riders) and take a cut of each transaction. For in-store services, platforms play the role that Yelp, Google Maps and Groupon play in the West, combined in one app.

The industry's economics depend on local density, not national scale: a platform must win neighbourhood by neighbourhood, and a well-funded rival can attack one city at a time. That is why the industry has repeatedly gone through subsidy wars — Meituan versus Ele.me in 2015–2018, Meituan versus Douyin in in-store from 2022, and Meituan versus JD.com and Alibaba in delivery in 2025–2026.

### How the Industry Works

**The value chain.** A food-delivery order passes through four parties: the consumer (who pays the menu price plus a delivery fee, minus any platform or merchant discount), the merchant (who receives the menu price minus commission and any merchant-funded discount), the platform (which keeps commission, any advertising the merchant has bought, and the delivery fee, and pays incentives) and the rider (paid per order by the platform or its labour contractors). Instant retail adds a further party when the platform holds inventory itself (front warehouses such as Xiaoxiang or Hema) — then the platform earns retail margin instead of commission. In-store has no rider: the consumer buys a voucher on the app and redeems it at the merchant; the platform earns commission on the voucher and advertising fees.

**Business models and margin implications.**

| Model | Platform's revenue | Main cost | Margin profile |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3P food delivery, platform-delivered | Commission + ads + delivery fee | Rider cost per order | Thin; a few RMB of revenue per order against ~RMB6–7 of fulfilment cost [11] (secondary) |
| 3P food delivery, merchant-delivered | Commission + ads | Minimal | High, but small share of orders |
| Instant retail, 3P stores | Commission + ads + delivery fee | Rider cost | Similar to food delivery; higher AOV helps |
| Instant retail, 1P front warehouses | Retail gross margin | Inventory, warehouse rent, shrinkage, delivery | Low; scale- and private-label-dependent |
| In-store vouchers and ads | Commission + ads | Sales force, marketing | High |
| Online travel (hotels) | Commission | Marketing, service | High; Trip.com dominant |

**Pricing.** Commission rates vary by merchant tier and city; delivery fees are dynamic by distance, time and weather; advertising is sold by auction and performance. Platforms subsidise consumers through coupons, membership discounts and "free delivery"; in subsidy wars they also co-fund merchant discounts. Regulation since 2025 has focused on exactly these practices (below).

**Network effects** are local and partial. More consumers in a zone attract merchants and lower delivery cost per order; but merchants and consumers can multi-home at little cost. In late 2025, 82.3% of fast-food consumers had ordered from two or more platforms in the past month and 91.3% compared platforms when ordering, per Hongcan Industry Research Institute [10] (secondary). This is why share can move quickly when one platform outspends another, and why it moves back slowly.

### Demand Drivers and Cyclicality

- **Urbanisation and household structure** — small households and office workers in dense cities drive habitual delivery.
- **Catering spending** — national catering revenue grew 3.2% in 2025 [15], slower than food delivery (+11.7%) [13], so delivery is taking share from dine-in.
- **Consumer confidence** — matters most for in-store, hotel and travel; less for delivery.
- **Platform subsidies** — the 2025 war temporarily inflated orders: combined daily orders for the three platforms reached about 250 million at the peak between Q2 2025 and Q1 2026, against roughly 120 million in UBS's March 2026 estimate once subsidies receded [11] (secondary).
- **Seasonality** — food delivery peaks in summer (drinks) and during extreme weather; in-store and travel peak around holidays (Spring Festival, May Day, National Day).

The rules protect merchants and riders from being squeezed, which in practice limits how hard any platform — including Meituan — can push take rates or cut rider pay.

### Key Industry Trends and Developments

**From subsidies to unit economics.** All three delivery platforms now talk about profit per order rather than volume. Meituan's UE turned positive in Q2 2026 [7]; Alibaba said Taobao Instant Commerce unit economics improved quarter on quarter while share was maintained, and attributed a large rise in its e-commerce group's profit to reduced losses there [16]; JD's food-delivery losses fell by more than half year on year in Q3 2026, and JD said it would cut food-delivery investment significantly in 2027 [34]. At its October 2026 conference Meituan said the share of restaurant orders priced under RMB15 had halved year on year [13].

**Instant retail as the next battleground.** Alibaba's Quick Commerce revenue (Freshippo plus Taobao Instant Commerce) was RMB58.8bn in the September 2026 quarter, up 22% [16]; Alibaba's targets, as reported by 36Kr, include monthly positive unit economics in FY2027 and instant-retail scale above RMB1 trillion in FY2028, and it expects non-food transaction volume to overtake food within FY2028 [10][11] (secondary). Hema front warehouses exceeded 500 by June 2026, with a target of over 1,000 by year-end [11] (secondary). Meituan responded with the Dingdong acquisition, Xiaoxiang's expansion to over 2,000 front warehouses in 100 cities, a 2027 target of 100,000 flash warehouses and RMB200bn GMV, and a RMB30bn "Commodity NEXT" programme [11][26] (secondary).

**Supply-side differentiation.** With subsidies capped, platforms compete by controlling supply: Meituan's Raccoon Canteen shared kitchens and satellite stores; JD's Qixian Kitchen (JD pays rent, ingredients and staff; partners supply recipes and share profits) and Qixian Supermarket; Alibaba's Jiwa Canteen (budget) and Jiayan (premium dining) [11] (secondary).

**Douyin and Amap in in-store.** Douyin's local-services payment GMV exceeded RMB850bn in 2025 (+59%), with a 2026 target of +50% [22] (secondary). Dolphin Research cites industry expectations that Douyin's in-store, hotel and travel GTV would rise from just over 70% of Meituan's in 2025 to nearly 100% in 2026 [23][30] (secondary; pre-redemption). Alibaba's Amap launched "Street Stars" AI-ranked lists in September 2025 with billions of RMB in merchant incentives, positioned against Dianping, and says the lists will not run merchant ad bidding [25] (secondary). Amap reached a peak of about 370 million daily active users on October 1, 2026 [16].

**Rider welfare as a permanent cost.** Pension subsidies and occupational-injury insurance funded by platforms became national in 2025–26 [1][13]. JD's use of full-time riders raised its per-order cost relative to Meituan's, per J.P. Morgan [11] (secondary).

**AI agents as a new entry point.** Meituan (Xiaotuan, LongCat), Alibaba (Qwen app) and JD (Dongdong assistant) are building AI assistants that could take orders on the user's behalf. Whether an AI assistant becomes the user's entry point — and whose — could change who owns the consumer relationship [4][7][34].

**Overseas expansion.** Chinese platforms are exporting the dense-courier model: Meituan's Keeta in Hong Kong, the Gulf and Brazil; Didi's 99Food in Brazil; JD's Joybuy in Europe [7][28][34].

### Competitive Landscape

**Food delivery (Mainland China).** Three national platforms. Share estimates differ by source, date and method, and none comes from a company:

Read together, the estimates say: Meituan lost roughly 15–20 points of order share from 2024 to 2026, mostly to Alibaba; its GTV share is higher than its order share because it kept more higher-value orders; JD peaked in 2025 and has fallen back to single digits or low double digits; and relative shares have been roughly stable since September 2025 per Goldman [10]. Order share overstates the importance of subsidised low-ticket orders, so GTV share is the better measure of economic position — but only Meituan has given a GTV figure, and it is self-reported.

**In-store, hotel and travel.**

| Player | Positioning | Scale / share |
|---|---|---|
| Meituan / Dianping | Search-driven: reviews, rankings, vouchers redeemed post-purchase; 8m+ merchants [7] | In-store GTV "a little more than" RMB1 trn in 2024 [31] (secondary) |

| Kuaishou | Short video; lower-tier cities | Not quantified in sources reviewed |

Online hotel booking is more concentrated: per LatePost, Trip.com group (with Qunar and Tongcheng) holds about 70% of online hotel GMV, Meituan about 20%, Fliggy 5–7% and Douyin about 3%, much of Douyin's supply coming from Trip.com and Tongcheng [24] (secondary).

**Overseas (Keeta).** Hong Kong: Keeta reported over 50% share against Foodpanda [27] (secondary). Saudi Arabia: Keeta targets 40% by mid-2027 against HungerStation and Jahez [27] (secondary). Brazil: iFood is the incumbent; Keeta and 99Food are entrants [28] (secondary).

### Level of Competitive Intensity

**Food delivery: very high in 2025, moderating but still elevated in 2026.** The 2025 war showed that two deep-pocketed entrants could take a combined ~40–50% of orders within a year by outspending the incumbent. Management said in August 2026 that industry subsidies remain "well above" 2024 levels and that normalisation will take "a few quarters" [7]. Intensity is now constrained less by the players' willingness than by regulation (the draft subsidy rules) and by shareholders (Alibaba and JD both committed to narrowing losses) [16][18][34]. Barriers to entry are low for a rich entrant — consumers and merchants multi-home — but barriers to *profitable scale* are high: density determines cost per order, and Meituan's estimated cost advantage of RMB0.3–0.5 per order [11] (secondary) compounds across tens of millions of daily orders.

**In-store: high and rising in 2026.** Douyin is growing faster than Meituan with heavier merchant subsidies, and management guided lower in-store margins for H2 2026, citing a competitor using a "dedicated shelf-based app" with heavy subsidies [7]. But in-store competition costs less than delivery competition because there is no logistics cost to subsidise; Dolphin Research expects the profit impact to be less severe than the delivery war even if the share impact is larger [23] (secondary).

**Instant retail: high and structural.** Alibaba treats it as core to Taobao; Meituan treats it as the extension of its delivery network; JD treats it as an extension of its supply chain.

**Hotels: moderate, under regulatory review.** Trip.com's dominance and the 2026 antitrust actions limit how aggressively anyone can compete through exclusive deals [21][24].

### Basis of Competition

| Dimension | What it means in this industry | Who it favours |
|---|---|---|
| Order density / fulfilment cost | Cost per delivery falls with orders per rider-hour per zone | Meituan (largest network; lowest estimated cost per order [11]) |
| Price and subsidies | Coupons, free delivery, merchant co-funded discounts | Deepest pockets (Alibaba); now capped by regulation [18] |
| Supply breadth | Restaurants, stores, categories, own inventory | Meituan (restaurants, in-store merchants); Alibaba (Taobao brands, Hema); JD (electronics, 1P goods) |
| Traffic and user frequency | High-frequency entry point that can be cross-sold | Meituan (delivery); Alibaba (Taobao, Alipay, Amap); Douyin (content time) |
| Discovery and content | Reviews, rankings, short video | Dianping (search intent); Douyin (impulse) |
| Merchant tools | SaaS, POS, supply (Kuailv), AI agents | Meituan |
| Membership | Paid or free memberships bundling discounts | Alibaba (88VIP, ~66m members [16]); Meituan Membership |
| Regulatory execution | Food safety, rider welfare, pricing compliance | Incumbents with mature compliance; all fined in 2026 [17] |

### Structural Dynamics and Outlook

Three structural shifts are visible in the sources.

First, **food delivery has moved from a near-monopoly to a duopoly with a tail**. Before 2025 Meituan held about two-thirds of orders with a passive number two; by 2026 Alibaba holds about 40% and treats instant commerce as strategic to Taobao [10][11][16]. The rational-duopoly case is that both players now prioritise unit economics and regulators prohibit predatory subsidies; the risk case is that Alibaba's non-food push and AI-assistant integration reopen the fight in forms the rules do not cover.

Second, **the contest is shifting from orders to supply**: front warehouses, private label, shared kitchens and own-brand restaurant formats. This raises capital intensity for all three, and blurs the line between platform and retailer — visible in Meituan's rising product-sales revenue [3][4].

Third, **the in-store business, the industry's profit pool, is under its most serious challenge**, from Douyin on price and Amap on discovery [22][23][25]. Because in-store carries most of Meituan's profit at normal delivery margins, its trajectory matters more to Meituan than to any competitor.

Summary: China's local-services industry is large, still growing faster than overall consumption, and dense-network-driven. 2025–26 broke Meituan's monopoly economics in food delivery and drew regulators deep into pricing, subsidies and mergers. The industry is now settling into a regulated duopoly in delivery, an intensifying three-way contest in in-store, and a capital-hungry race in instant retail.

Drivers:
- **The war's cost showed up in two lines.** Selling and marketing rose from about 18–19% of revenue before the war to 34–36% in Q3–Q4 2025 (promotion, advertising and user incentives of RMB74.5bn for 2025 vs RMB39.1bn in 2024) [1][2][5]. Cost of revenues rose from about 61% to 74% because courier incentives went up, low-ticket orders cost as much to deliver as high-ticket ones, and incentives deducted from revenue shrank the denominator [1][5].
- **The 2026 recovery is mostly marketing.** S&M fell back to 23.6% in Q2 2026, still about five points above Q3 2024; cost of revenues fell to 66.5%, still about six points above [4][5]. Part of the remaining gap is structural — product sales carry a high cost of revenues by nature, and rider social insurance is permanent — and part is competitive intensity that management says will take "a few quarters" to normalise [7].

- **New Initiatives** improved in 2026 as Meituan Select's losses ended and Keeta Hong Kong turned profitable, offset by Brazil, the Gulf and grocery expansion; grocery losses widened quarter on quarter in Q2 2026 as the business scaled [4][7].
- **CLC margin at 7.9% in Q2 2026** compares with 20.9% for 2024 [1][4]. The gap is the single most important number in the Meituan debate.

### Working Capital and Balance Sheet KPIs

Meituan's business runs on negative working capital in food delivery: consumers pay at order and merchants are settled on a short cycle, so payables to merchants and couriers rise with volume. Restricted cash (RMB21.9bn at June 30, 2026) is largely customer and merchant funds held for settlement [4]. Grocery and Dingdong will add inventory. Meituan does not disclose working-capital days.

The three things the reader must understand about Meituan's financials:

1. **The war cost about RMB60bn of operating profit in CLC.** CLC went from RMB52.4bn profit (2024) to RMB6.9bn loss (2025) on slightly higher revenue [1]. Operating cash flow swung by about RMB71bn [1]. Meituan funded this from its cash pile and new borrowing, not equity.
2. **Segment reporting hides the profit pool.** The return to RMB5.7bn of CLC profit in Q2 2026 combines a small food-delivery profit with an in-store, hotel and travel business that press estimates put at 20–30% margins [4][9]. If in-store margins fall in H2 2026 as guided [7], CLC margin can stall even as food delivery improves.
3. **Revenue growth is increasingly 1P.** Product sales were 25.5% of Q2 2026 revenue [4]; Dingdong would add more. This lifts revenue, depresses margin percentages and raises inventory and capital needs.

### Key Risks to the Outlook

**Re-escalation in delivery or instant retail.** Alibaba's Taobao Instant Commerce is targeting positive monthly unit economics in FY2027 and RMB1 trillion of instant-retail scale in FY2028 [11] (secondary); management says industry subsidies are still well above 2024 levels [7]. A push into non-food categories, or subsidies routed through AI assistants or membership programmes, could fall outside the draft subsidy rules [18].

**In-store share and margin.** Douyin's local services grew 59% in 2025 and target 50% growth in 2026, with merchant subsidies up to twice Meituan's in contested cities [22] (secondary); Amap is building map-based discovery [25]. Management has guided lower H2 2026 in-store margins [7]. In-store is the highest-margin part of CLC, so share loss here affects profit more than share loss in delivery.

**Regulatory scope.** Regulation now reaches pricing (draft subsidy rules), labour (nationwide injury insurance), food safety (RMB745.7m fine), hotel booking (September 2026 investigation) and M&A (Maiyatian review) [3][13][17][18][20][21]. The same rules that cap rivals' subsidies also cap Meituan's ability to raise commissions or lower rider pay.

**Mix toward 1P.** Grocery (Xiaoxiang, Kuailv, Dingdong if closed) is gross-revenue, low-margin and capital-intensive; Meituan Select's closure shows the downside of this category [2][9]. Grocery losses widened quarter on quarter in Q2 2026 [7].

**Overseas investment.** Keeta's Brazil entry faces an entrenched iFood and litigation with 99Food [28] (secondary). New Initiatives losses are guided flat for 2026, not down [7].

**Disclosure.** With no order, user, GTV or segment-level profit disclosure, investors depend on management commentary and broker estimates to track the variables that matter most (UE, share, in-store margins).

## 4.5 Questions Before Forming a View

The questions below are the ones this fact base cannot settle. Materiality reflects how much the answer would change an assessment of the business.

| # | Question | Materiality | Why it matters / where the evidence would come from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is Core Local Commerce's sustainable operating margin in a regulated duopoly, against 20.9% in 2024 and 7.9% in Q2 2026? | High | The single largest driver of earnings; needs food-delivery UE and in-store margin separately (calls, broker models, Q3 2026 results on Nov 27) [1][4] |
| 2 | Is Meituan's food-delivery GTV share stable at the current level, and does order share keep moving toward Alibaba? | High | Share estimates diverge by 10+ points across sources [10][11][12]; GTV vs order share matters for economics |
| 3 | How much in-store, hotel and travel share and margin does Douyin (and Amap) take in 2026–27, on a redeemed-GTV basis? | High | In-store carries most CLC profit; management guided lower H2 2026 margins [7][22][23] |
| 4 | Will the June 2026 draft subsidy rules be finalised as drafted, and do they cover subsidies routed through membership, AI agents or non-food categories? | High | Determines whether the price war can recur [18] |
| 5 | Does instant retail and grocery (Xiaoxiang, Dingdong, Commodity NEXT) earn a return, or repeat Meituan Select? | Medium | Rising capital intensity and 1P mix [4][11][26] |
| 6 | What do Keeta's Gulf and Brazil losses look like, and when do they turn? | Medium | New Initiatives losses guided flat for 2026 [7][27] |
| 7 | Will Meituan monetise its ~RMB77bn strategic-stake portfolio and resume buybacks at scale? | Medium | Capital returns were near zero in 2025–26 [4][7] |
| 8 | What is the outcome of the hotel-booking investigation and the Maiyatian review? | Low–Medium | Regulatory reach into structure and M&A [20][21] |

**Further research.** Meituan Q3 2026 results and call (November 27, 2026); broker models for split CLC economics (Bernstein, CLSA, Capital IQ estimates); Alibaba's December-quarter instant-commerce disclosures; SAMR's final subsidy rules; Douyin local-services data (LatePost, Leifeng); merchant and rider channel checks; Dingdong closing terms.

**Companies to study alongside.** Alibaba (Taobao Instant Commerce, Amap); JD.com (New Businesses); Trip.com (hotel booking structure); Dingdong (acquisition target); DoorDash and Delivery Hero/Talabat (mature-market delivery margins and Gulf competition); iFood/Prosus (Brazil).
