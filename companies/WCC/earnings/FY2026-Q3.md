# Wesco International (WCC) - FY2026 Q3 earnings update (reported 2026-10-29)

**Backfill.** Results came out on 2026-10-29, before the watch started; run on 2026-11-24 at Paul's request. Valuation uses the 2026-11-23 close of US$303.06. That is 12.7% below the last close before the release (US$347.22) and 17.4% below the results-day close (US$366.71). The shares rose 5.6% on the day, then fell with the data-centre supply chain after the US midterm elections (4-5 November) and again on 20 November.

**Provisional baseline (Claude's framing, not Paul's view).** At 16.6 times guided 2026 adjusted earnings and 10.9 times trailing adjusted EBITDA, the return depends on five things: (1) data-centre sales, now about a quarter of the company and the source of about 60% of its growth, continuing to grow through 2027-28 and staying "margin accretive"; (2) the other three-quarters of the business (construction, industrial, OEM, utility) growing in the mid-to-high single digits; (3) the adjusted EBITDA margin moving from 6.5% in 2025 and about 7.2% in 2026 towards 8% and beyond, helped by the digital programme; (4) profit turning into cash, which it did not in 2025 (free cash flow US$54m) and does only in part now; (5) leverage falling from 2.8 times through cash, not only through a larger EBITDA, so that buybacks can resume.

What to track: organic growth in total and outside data centres; data-centre sales and growth; backlog growth by segment; gross margin and adjusted EBITDA margin by segment; the add-backs inside adjusted EBITDA; free cash flow and working capital against sales; net debt and leverage; price contribution; UBS margin.

What would break it (Claude's proposals): data-centre sales growth under 10% for two quarters with total organic growth under 3%; full-year adjusted EBITDA margin back under 6.5%; free cash flow under US$300m in 2026 or under half of adjusted net income in 2027; leverage back above 3.5 times; digital-transformation costs still above US$75m a year after 2027 without the promised 100bp, or an acquisition above US$1bn while leverage is above 3 times.

Missing: Paul's view, primer, debate with probabilities, variant view, model.

## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**A. Data centres have gone from a growth line to the centre of the company.**
- What changed: about 23% of trailing sales against about 18% for 2025 (US$4.3bn of US$23.5bn); 48% of CSS; data-centre customers now about 10% of EES, where sales to them more than doubled (decks, 10 November deck).
- Puts: CSS margin 10.7%, a record, with management crediting services added over a project's life; multi-year customer commitments; advances from customers.
- Takes: one end market with a small number of very large buyers; permitting and power as outside constraints; a book that can stop growing on a customer's decision. The company does not disclose how much is hyperscale.
- Net effect: at CSS's margin, the US$1.7bn of data-centre sales added since 2025 is worth about US$180m of EBITDA a year, 10% of the trailing total (e). It lasts as long as the build does: management puts construction at one to two years and time-to-power at three to five.
- Updates: leg 1. Confidence: medium on 2027 (backlog), low beyond. Confirms: backlog growth still above sales growth in February; the hyperscale share disclosed. Reverses: CSS backlog growth falling below sales growth.

**B. A rising share of "adjusted" profit is cost the company asks to have set aside.**
- What changed: add-backs 10.3% of adjusted EBITDA in Q3 against 6.7%; trailing digital-transformation cost US$77m against 35m for 2025; trailing stock compensation US$67m against 41m (release).
- Puts: the digital programme is the source of the promised 100bp or more of margin, about US$270m a year on 2026 sales (e); the first full conversions were done in the first half.
- Takes: the programme has run five to six years by the CEO's account, its cost is rising, and no date is given for the benefit. Stock compensation has nearly doubled in a year.
- Net effect: on a basis that treats all three items as costs, the margin is 7.0%, not 7.8%, and EV to trailing EBITDA is about 12.2 times, not 10.9 (computed).
- Updates: leg 3. Confidence: high on the arithmetic. Confirms the concern: add-backs above 10% of adjusted EBITDA again in 2027. Reverses: digital costs falling in 2027 with the SG&A ratio still improving.

**C. Customers are funding part of the build.**
- What changed: deferred revenue up US$294m in nine months, with a non-current part for the first time (10-Q).
- Puts: lower capital need per dollar of project sales; commitment from the customer.
- Takes: an obligation to deliver at agreed terms; it reverses if projects are cancelled.
- Net effect: about two-thirds of nine-month free cash flow (294 of 430; computed). Without it, conversion would be about 20% of adjusted net income.
- Updates: leg 4. Confidence: medium (the line also includes ordinary advances).

**D. Utility: services are growing inside a flat-margin segment.**
- What changed: grid services up double digits each quarter; more than US$1bn of multi-year awards won in 2026 (10 November deck); UBS backlog up about 90%; public-power pricing "stabilized"; broadband flat.
- Puts: a long-dated, contracted line that starts in the first half of 2027; AEP raised its capital plan to US$100bn from US$70bn, and Oncor expects about 20% more in its next plan.
- Takes: UBS margin 10.4% is flat on the year and below the 11% the CEO says it once earned.
- Net effect: about US$400m of trailing sales, 7% of UBS; too small to move the group before 2028 (e).
- Updates: leg 2. Confidence: medium.

**Expected and not seen:** net debt falling; the benefit of the digital programme in a number.

**Read-across from the vault.** [[ai-infrastructure-cycle]] carries the phase as Late cycle after [[NVDA]]'s FY27 Q3: demand still supply-bound, with the strain in funding (Alphabet's and Amazon's free cash flow negative; financing-contingent commitments at the smaller cloud companies). Three readings since the results point the other way at the margin: the Dodge Momentum Index fell 9% in October on weaker data-centre and warehouse planning; the Texas governor-elect campaigned on pausing new data centres (Texas is put at about 27% of planned US data-centre power; press); and the power-equipment group fell 7-14% on 4 November.

**Phase: Late cycle.** Read on the same indicators, Q2 was Late cycle too; the pace since is faster than the July guide expected. By end market:
- Data centres (about 25% of Q3 sales): Late cycle. For: bookings and backlog still outrunning sales, hyperscalers' combined capex up 108% with Alphabet and Amazon pointing higher for 2027. Against: planning data and politics, above.
- Industrial, OEM and construction outside data centres: Early recovery. For: ISM at 55.5, stock-and-flow sales picking up. Against: architecture billings at 44.9 and private non-residential construction outside data centres and power down about 8%.
- Utility: Mid-cycle, improving.

**Against mid-cycle:** over-earning by roughly 10-15% (e). The Q3 margin is 130bp above 2025's; data-centre sales above their 2025 level are worth about 10% of EBITDA; price inflation of 3 points is helping gross profit on inventory. Against that, the rest of the business is still below a normal year.

**What the price assumes:** 16.6 times guided earnings and a 3% free cash flow yield assume that data-centre sales keep growing through 2028 and that the margin holds near 8%. No slowdown is paid for.

**Next period.** Confirms: organic growth of 12% or more in Q4; backlog growth still above 50%; sales outside data centres at 7% or better; 2027 guide of at least mid-single-digit organic growth with margin up. Phase wrong (later than thought): CSS backlog growth below sales growth; a 2027 guide that needs data centres to grow less than 15%.

What has to be true: the data-centre build runs for at least two more years at Wesco's customers; the other three-quarters of sales grow; the margin gain is real; profit turns into cash and debt comes down. The evidence on file supports the first through 2027 only, supports the second, supports about two-thirds of the third, and does not yet support the fourth. The vault's own read on the cycle that drives the first is Late. A distributor's working capital would release cash in a slowdown, which softens the harsh case for the balance sheet but not for the equity multiple.

**Call: Wait** (cycle and price). What would move it to Own: a price near US$245 with the baseline intact; or a February outlook that shows 2027 growth not resting on data centres, free cash flow above 80% of net income, and digital costs falling. What would move it to Don't own: a falsifier above, or evidence that Q3's margin was a mix peak.
