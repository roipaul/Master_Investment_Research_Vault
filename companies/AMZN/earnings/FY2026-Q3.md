# Amazon (AMZN) — Q3 2026 earnings review and follow-ups (2026-10-30)

> **Imported** on 2026-11-26 from the Global Tech Project chat "AMZN" (2026-10-30). Claude's replies are copied as written, with tool calls and closing offers of further work removed; Paul's questions are kept as the section headings. **Claude's framing, not Paul's view** — including the entry levels. Paul's only stated position here is in his own question in section 3.

## 1. "Can you evaluate the AMZN earning released last night and what are the key takeaways?"

**Bottom line: operationally one of Amazon's best quarters in years, but the market is now pricing the cost of growth rather than the growth itself.** The stock sold off despite the AWS print.

**The numbers**
- Revenue increased 12% to $201.9 billion, while operating profit reached $28.1 billion, a record 13.9% margin. AWS grew 39.3% to $46 billion, with a 38.7% operating margin. EPS $1.89 vs est $1.96 — a miss, but net income fell 2.37% year over year only because Q3 2025 included pre-tax gains from investments in Anthropic that did not repeat.
- The 61% op income growth is flattered: last year's quarter included $4.3 billion of FTC settlement and severance charges. Adding those back to the comparison period reduces growth to approximately 29%. Still excellent.
- Retail growth looks soft only on timing: excluding this shift, third quarter year-over-year growth would have been approximately 400 basis points higher.

**Key takeaways**

1. **AWS is the story and it's re-accelerating into scale.** The AWS growth rate was the highest since the fourth quarter of 2021. AWS revenue grew 39.3% year over year, with a strong backlog of $578 billion. Jassy guided that AWS can grow north of 40% in each of 2027 and in 2028, and be a trillion-dollar revenue business in the next five to 10 years. Note the whole hyperscaler cohort accelerated — Amazon Web Services accelerated revenue growth, a day after Google and Microsoft did the same. So this is an industry demand wave, not share gain per se.

2. **Profit mix is now overwhelmingly cloud.** AWS produced $17.8B of the $28.1B in operating income, about 63%, on 23% of revenue. Your SOTP work should treat retail as increasingly a funding vehicle for AWS, with Ads the swing factor — Amazon Ads generated $19.6 billion in Q3, with expected growth returning to high teens in Q4 despite some timing anomalies.

3. **Capex and cash flow are why the stock fell.** A selloff on a 39% AWS growth print says the market is no longer rewarding growth alone. It is pricing the cost of getting that growth: $77.3B of capex in a single quarter and a negative free cash flow trend. Going into the print, trailing free cash flow turned negative at −$7.6B and long-term debt already rose to $128.9B from $65.6B at year-end. Amazon is holding 2026 capital expenditures at roughly $220 billion — not raised again, which is the one small relief.

4. **Retail cost-to-serve keeps improving.** Units nevertheless rose 12%, while shipping costs excluding fuel and line-haul inflation increased 8%, and Amazon doubled its robotic-arm fleet during 2026 and remains on track for a fourth consecutive year of lower cost to serve per unit.

5. **Q4 guide is solid:** a Q4 sales range of $240.0B to $247.0B, i.e. 12–16% growth with Prime Day timing no longer a drag.

**How I'd frame it for your thesis**
The quality-value question is no longer "is AWS growing" — it's whether ~$220B/yr of capex earns its cost of capital. The $578B backlog and multi-year contracted AI capacity support a yes; the debt build and negative FCF mean the equity now carries real cycle risk if AI demand pauses in 2027–28. Things to track: AWS margin holding near 38–39% as depreciation ramps, backlog growth vs. capex growth, and whether 2027 capex guidance (likely given in February) steps up again.

## 2. "What are the open questions now and what is your stand on the questions?"

**Open questions after Q3, with my stand on each**

1. **Does ~$220B/yr capex earn its cost of capital?** (High materiality — this is the whole stock now.)
*My stand: probably yes, but with a narrower margin of safety than the bulls assume.* AWS at 39% growth and a 38.7% margin on a $184B run-rate is already generating ~$70B of annualised operating income, and a $578B backlog (over 3 years of revenue) means much of the capacity is pre-sold. The risk isn't that the return is bad; it's that the return is good but concentrated — a handful of AI labs (Anthropic, OpenAI) make up a large share of backlog, and their own economics are unproven. I'd want to see backlog diversify.

2. **Can AWS hold a high-30s margin as depreciation catches up?**
*My stand: expect erosion to the low-to-mid 30s over 2027–28, and that's fine.* Capex of ~$76B a quarter versus depreciation that hasn't yet caught up means margins are flattered today. Custom silicon (Trainium, Graviton) is the real offset. I'd treat 35% as the base case, not 39%.

3. **Is the hyperscaler acceleration demand or capacity release?**
*My stand: mostly capacity release.* All three accelerated the same week, which points to supply constraints clearing rather than a step-up in end demand. That implies growth is capped by how fast capacity lands — supportive of Jassy's >40% guidance for 2027–28, but also meaning growth is a function of capex, which is the circularity the market dislikes.

4. **How should retail be valued now?**
*My stand: a cash cow with an improving cost curve, worth less than consensus SOTPs assume but more stable.* Four consecutive years of lower cost per unit and 12% unit growth are real. But retail ex-Ads earns a thin margin, and Ads (~$80B run-rate, high-teens growth) is doing most of the work. I'd value retail + Ads together on earnings, not revenue.

5. **Balance sheet: is debt-funded AI capex a problem?**
*My stand: not yet, but it removes optionality.* Debt doubled in nine months and FCF is negative. Operating cash flow of ~$160B TTM covers it, but Amazon has moved from a self-funding compounder to a leveraged build. If AI demand pauses in 2027, buybacks and flexibility are gone.

6. **What's the right entry point?**
*My stand: not a clean quality-value setup at today's price.* The thesis has become "AWS trillion-dollar business" — a growth story, not a valuation-gap story. The pullback helps but doesn't create a gap; the asymmetry is in 2027 capex guidance in February. If that number steps up again and the stock sells off further, that's the better entry.

**What would make me more positive:** backlog growing faster than capex; AWS margin holding ≥36% through 2027; Ads growth back to 20%+.
**What would falsify:** backlog growth stalling while capex keeps rising; a major AI-lab customer cutting commitments; 2027 capex guided >$280B without matching backlog.

## 3. "I sold my AMZN position at around the current price a year ago. At what price do you think I should be interested again at initiating?"

**Short answer: I'd get interested around $210, and build a full position toward $190–195.** Stock is ~$227 premarket today, after a ~10% drop from $254.

**Why the number**

First, the context your sale gives you: the stock is flat over the year you've been out, while operating income has grown roughly 30%+ underlying. So the derating has already largely happened — a year ago you were paying ~35x forward earnings; today it's about 21x. FY 2027 EPS estimate $10.56 (it trails FY2026 because this year's figure carries those one-time investment gains). My own build gets a similar place: AWS ~$245B revenue in 2027 at a 35% margin (~$86B) plus ~$48B from retail and Ads, ~$130–135B of operating income, ~$9.70–10.50 EPS depending on tax and interest.

Then the multiple. Amazon is now an earnings story with no free cash flow — trailing free cash flow fell to minus $42.4 billion and full-year 2026 cash capex is expected at about $220 billion, with 2027 significantly higher. For a business growing earnings 20%+ but funding it with debt, I'd want to pay roughly a market multiple, not a premium: 18–20x 2027 EPS = $190–210. Below ~$195 you're getting AWS's 2028 growth more or less free if the 40%+ guide holds, and you have a cushion for the margin compression I expect.

**What would move the target**

- *Lower it:* February's 2027 capex guide comes in above ~$280B without backlog growing at least as fast; AWS margin prints below 35%; a major AI-lab customer trims commitments.
- *Raise it:* backlog accelerates past capex growth, or Amazon signals a 2028 capex plateau — that's the point where FCF inflects and the stock rerates fast, and you don't want to be waiting for $190 when it happens.

One correction to my earlier note: ad growth slowed to 12% from 26% this quarter (management blamed timing and expects high-teens in Q4). That's worth watching, since Ads is what makes the retail segment worth owning. Also the Q4 operating income guidance of $31 billion at the midpoint is below the $33.2 billion analysts expected — the sell-off is as much about that guide as about capex.

Standard caveat: I'm not a financial advisor and these are my own estimates, not a recommendation — but that's where I'd set the alert.

## 4. "How is the current backlog growth comparing to the capex growth?"

**Headline: backlog is still growing faster than capex year on year (~190% vs ~120%), but the quarter-on-quarter picture turned this quarter — backlog additions slowed while capex stepped up.**

| | Q1'26 | Q2'26 | Q3'26 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Backlog (RPO) | $364B | $496B | $578B |
| Sequential add | +$120B | +$132B | +$82B |
| Cash capex | $43B | $53B | $76B |
| Backlog ÷ capex that quarter | 2.8x | 2.5x | 1.1x |

Sources for the backlog path: backlog at AWS climbed to $496 billion at the end of the quarter, up dramatically from $364 billion exiting March, having been $195 billion a year earlier; Q3 is $578B. Capex: during the June quarter, Amazon spent $53.1 billion in capex after $43.2 billion spent in the March quarter; Q3 was $75.9B.

**How to read it**

- *The H1 backlog surge was two deals.* In Q1 2026 AWS and OpenAI expanded an existing $38 billion commitment by $100 billion over eight years. In Q2 2026 AWS and Anthropic expanded theirs by more than $100 billion over 10 years. Strip those out and the underlying run-rate of new commitments is ~$60–80B a quarter — which is roughly what Q3 delivered without a mega-deal. So Q3 isn't a demand slowdown so much as the absence of a lumpy signing.
- *But on that underlying basis, backlog adds now only match capex, not exceed it.* ~$80B of new multi-year commitments against $76B of cash spend in the quarter. That's the ratio that should worry a value investor: the contracted book is no longer growing faster than the spend.
- *Coverage is thinner than the headline suggests.* Backlog has a 6.4-year weighted-average remaining term, so $578B is only ~$90B a year of contracted revenue against $220B of annual capex. The other ~$100B of AWS revenue is on-demand and uncontracted — fine while demand outstrips supply, but it's the part that evaporates first in a downturn.
- *Concentration is the unanswered question.* Backlog concentration stays undisclosed: Amazon reports one number for $496 billion of contracted revenue. It names no customer and gives no concentration figure. Plausibly ~40% of the backlog is two AI labs whose own cash flows depend on raising capital.

**What I'd track from here:** sequential backlog adds in Q4 and Q1 versus the ~$80B underlying rate. If adds fall below capex for two quarters running while 2027 capex guides "significantly higher," that's the falsification signal I flagged earlier — and the reason I'd still wait for the lower entry.

## 5. "Did the management say anything about the return on investment on the capex in this earnings?"

**Yes, but only qualitatively — no numbers this time, which is itself a step back from Q2.**

What they said:

- Jassy's key line: We continue to accelerate our CapEx payback and have even more conviction than we already had that the investment we're making in AWS will yield a much larger revenue and free cash flow generating business than we would have imagined a couple years ago with strong return on invested capital.
- On AI margins specifically: Our operating margins have long been strong on our core offering, but rapidly improving on the AI side too, well ahead of where they were on core at a similar time of evolution.
- When an analyst asked directly about capex needed for 40% growth and the duration of returns on servers, networking and data centers, the answer stayed vague: management called investment meaningful and said infrastructure break-even timing continues to move earlier. One transcript summary flags it plainly: Amazon views current AI investment as a major long-term opportunity for revenue and free cash flow, but did not provide a specific payback period.
- Olsavsky also declined to guide AWS margins, and both acknowledged the mix headwind: rapid AI growth creates an unfavorable mix effect for AWS because AI margins remain below core cloud margins, despite improving faster than core margins did at a comparable stage.

**Why this matters for your question**

In Q2, CEO Andy Jassy gave one number Amazon had never published: the payback period on servers — under three years. This quarter he says payback is "accelerating" but doesn't restate or update the figure, in the quarter where capex jumped 43% sequentially and 2027 was flagged as "meaningfully more." Three readings:

1. *Benign:* they said it once, don't want to make it a quarterly KPI.
2. *Mixed:* payback is improving on servers, but the mix is shifting toward data centers and power (15–20 year assets with much longer paybacks), so a blended number would look worse — easier not to give one.
3. *Concerning:* memory and depreciation costs are rising fast enough that the sub-three-year figure wouldn't hold.

I lean toward (2). The useful disclosure would be payback split by asset class, or AI gross margin vs. core. Neither was offered, and that gap — combined with no concentration disclosure on the $578B backlog — is why I'd treat "strong ROIC" as a claim to verify rather than accept. It's the open question I'd put at the top of your list for the Q4 call in February.
