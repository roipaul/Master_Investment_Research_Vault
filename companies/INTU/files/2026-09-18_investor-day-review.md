# Intuit (INTU) — Investor Day 2026 review (2026-09-18)

> **Imported** on 2026-11-26 from the Global Tech Project chat "Intuit" (2026-09-18). Claude's reply is copied as written, with tool calls removed. **Claude's framing, not Paul's view.** Built from the slide deck, press release and a webcast write-up; no transcript or Q&A had been posted.

## "Intuit had an investor day yesterday. Can you pull the transcript and evaluate what is new in the conference? Anything we can learn to know something new about the company?"

No transcript is posted yet. Intuit has put up only video replays, a fact sheet and the slide deck, so this is built from the deck, the press release and a webcast write-up, checked against the Aug 25 earnings call. I haven't seen the Q&A.

The headlines aren't new: FY27 guidance was simply reiterated, and the 10–15% business and 4–8% consumer three-year targets, SBC inside non-GAAP, and the Mailchimp split were all given on Aug 25. What's new is the disclosure underneath:

- Growth has been price and mix, now quantified. Business customers paying under $200 shrank 8% a year over three years while the $1,000+ band grew 19%. Consumer platform customers went from 35.5M to 37.4M while revenue per customer rose from $148 to $198. TurboTax units fell from 45.9M in FY21 to 39.0M, and DIY e-file share dropped 3 points in a year to 56%.
- The small-business core looks saturated, not just neglected. Intuit says 55% of the 8M employer small businesses are already on its platform; the open space is 39M non-employers (90%+ use no software) and 1.8M mid-market firms. That sits awkwardly with the "we took our eye off new customers" story. My read, not theirs.
- Credit Karma Tax: $0 federal, $15 state, no upsells, built into Credit Karma; the pilot was ~80% incremental and over 60% of TurboTax defectors already have a Credit Karma account. Ironically, Intuit had to sell the original Credit Karma Tax to Square in 2020 to get the deal cleared. Management expects customer growth to outpace revenue per customer near term.
- "Assisted" is mostly DIY-with-help. 13M Live customers, but TurboTax's share of assisted e-files is 0.8%, roughly 0.7M returns on my arithmetic. Live revenue per unit slipped from $224 to $221; a flat $150 full-service offer drove about a quarter of full-service growth, and locations expand 30%+.
- AI costs are small so far: about $110M of token spend in FY26 (~0.5% of revenue), with gross margin expected up over time. Paid hooks are coming: Agent Studio is in private pilot with possible extra fees, and agentic books-close turns paid in January 2027.
- Earnings-quality details. Desktop revenue per customer doubled in three years ($1,594 to $3,154) on a base down to 0.8M, and desktop is now guided to decline. Mailchimp earns $604M at a 47.5% margin on flat $1.3B revenue. FY26 free cash flow of $8.6B includes a $1.2B one-off tax benefit. $5.5B of buybacks retired 13M shares, about $423 each versus roughly $313 now.

What to track: a fixed scorecard — quarterly mid-market customers and revenue, Enterprise Suite annualized revenue ($146M) and payments volume; annually paid customers, revenue per customer and e-file share (24.9%). The first hard test is e-file share this coming tax season.

Net: a more candid diagnosis than a new plan. At about 14x adjusted earnings, the debate is whether cheaper front doors add customers without handing back the pricing that drove the last three years.
