**Provisional baseline (Claude's framing, not Paul's view).** At about 0.84 times book and 7.8 times earnings, with about 7% of the market value coming back each year as dividends and cancelled shares, the return depends on five things: (1) return on equity holding near 13% with the CET1 ratio above 13.5%, so that the capital-return formula keeps paying out a little over half of profit; (2) credit costs staying under about 50bp through a rate-hiking cycle, with household debt above KRW 2,000tn and corporate delinquencies rising; (3) the bank's margin recovering as loans reprice to the higher base rate; (4) the regulatory take (fines, levies, education tax, risk weights, directed lending) not growing faster than earnings; (5) the non-bank businesses, above all KB Securities, adding earnings and not volatility.

What to track: bank and group NIM; credit cost, NPL ratio and coverage; CET1 ratio and RWA growth; the total return in won and the share count; net fee income; penalty provisions and new sanctions; KB Securities and insurance profit; the share of low-cost deposits.

What would break it (Claude's proposals): CET1 below 13.0% at a year-end (the formula then gives no first-round return); group credit cost above 70bp for a full year; bank NIM below 1.60%; the annual return cut below KRW 3.0tn or the formula changed; new fines above KRW 500bn in a year, or a business suspension at a main subsidiary.

Missing: Paul's view, primer, debate with probabilities, variant view, model.

## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**A. The regulatory take is now a standing line, not an event.**
- What changed in 2026: education tax doubled (about KRW 200bn a year more for the group, e, from the nine-month figure); the Banking Act now bars passing levies into loan rates (Hana put it at 0.7bp of bank NIM); mortgage risk-weight floor raised to 20% with 25% under review; household-loan growth capped (1.5%, then 3%); a KRW 110tn "productive finance" pledge to 2030; LTV fine KRW 69.7bn (January, in court); ELS penalty KRW 173.6bn (final); Treasury-bond fines KRW 336bn (not final); FSS sanctions pending on the bank, KB Life and KB Securities (statements, Korean press, peer releases).
- Puts: the ELS matter closed at about half the provision; separate taxation of dividends from 2026 favours high-payout companies, and KB has declared that it qualifies.
- Takes: recurring levies of about KRW 200-250bn a year before tax, 2-3% of pre-tax profit (e); penalties of about KRW 406bn announced in 2026 against KB entities, 6% of a year's profit; and each one reduces the capital the return formula works from.
- Net effect: about 3% off sustainable earnings (e), plus an irregular charge that has averaged several percent of profit for three years running (ELS in 2024-26, LTV and Treasury bonds in 2026). Plays out continuously.
- Updates: leg 4. Confidence: medium. Confirms: another sanction above KRW 100bn in 2027. Reverses: a court win on the Treasury-bond or LTV case; two clean years.

**B. More of the group's capital and earnings now sit in KB Securities.**
- What changed: KRW 1.7tn injected in 2026 (February and July; 1H26 deck); equity KRW 9.4tn; nine-month profit KRW 1,068bn before the fine against 497bn a year earlier; then a fine equal to 31% of that nine-month profit.
- Puts: fee income that does not depend on the capped household loan book; non-bank share of profit 40% (pre-amendment).
- Takes: earnings now move with stock-market turnover (fees halved in one quarter); a conduct record in the business that received the capital; the criminal referral, if it leads anywhere, is a licence and reputation risk that cannot be sized from here.
- Net effect: unresolved until a normal brokerage year is seen. If the CFO's KRW 1tn-a-quarter fee base holds, 2026 fee income is about KRW 1.1tn above base (e), most of it at KB Securities.
- Updates: legs 1 and 5. Confidence: low.

**C. Loan growth has moved from households to large companies.**
- What changed: large-corporate loans +15.2% year to date against household +1.4% (Fact Book). The CFO named competition for large-corporate loans as one cause of the margin fall.
- Puts: growth where regulators want it; lower loss rates than small business.
- Takes: thinner spreads, higher risk weights than mortgages, and a corporate loan-loss charge that rose 74% on the quarter.
- Net effect: small on earnings so far; a drag on margin of a basis point or two (e, from management's ordering of causes). Plays out through 2030 with the pledge.
- Updates: legs 2 and 3. Confidence: low.

**D. The capital-return formula has now run two full rounds as written.**
- What changed: nothing, which is the finding. Amounts tie to the published thresholds (section 2).
- Puts: a return that can be worked out in advance from two disclosed numbers; shares bought below book add to book value per share.
- Takes: the formula pays out of capital above a threshold, so fines, RWA growth and bond losses come straight off the next round; and management declined to add anything to it.
- Net effect: at about 13.7% CET1 (e) and RWA of KRW 369tn, headroom over 13.0% is about KRW 2.6tn (e), against KRW 2.8tn a year earlier, before Q4 earnings, the Q4 dividend and RWA growth.
- Updates: leg 1. Confidence: high on the mechanism; medium on the year-end figure.

**Expected and not seen:** a wider margin after 50bp of base-rate rises.

- About 7.8 times 2026E earnings (e); 0.84 times book and 0.86 times tangible book (company per-share figures less the amendment, e); dividend yield 3.6%; buybacks a further 3.8%.
- Last published consensus (MarketScreener, 8 October, before the results and the amendment): net profit KRW 6,686bn for 2026 and 6,954bn for 2027. Context only.

Scenario table (first statement; three years to end-2029; all (e); won per share):

| Case | Net profit | Shares | 2029 EPS | Exit multiple | Value | Dividends, three years | Return a year |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative | Flat at KRW 6.6tn (fees back to base, credit cost up, margin recovers) | -3% a year | 20,280 | 7 times | 141,980 | 16,720 | about 3% |
| Base | +3% a year; return on equity about 12.5% | -3.5% a year | 22,510 | 8 times | 180,070 | 18,060 | about 11% |
| Harsh | -28% (credit cost doubles to about 90bp); buybacks stop | flat | 13,180 | 6 times | 79,080 | 14,000 | about -14%; a third of the capital lost |

The base case meets the 15% hurdle at about KRW 130,000. At today's price it needs an exit multiple of about 9 times, or roughly book value, to get there; at 10 times it returns about 19%. The price sits between the conservative and base cases.

Since the print the shares are down 6.4%, all of it in one market-wide session. The amendment and the two penalty decisions moved the price by about 1%. The market appears to have treated the fines as already expected.

**Fresh money.** Someone meeting KB today gets Korea's largest bank group at a 16% discount to book, earning 13-14% on equity in a good year for brokerage, and returning 7% of its market value a year by a formula that has held for two rounds. What has to be true: return on equity stays near 13% once fees normalise; credit costs stay under 50bp while rates rise; the margin widens; the regulator's take stops growing. The evidence on file supports the first only with help from the margin, is turning against the second, has not yet shown the third, and went against the fourth this month. The yield does most of the work: 7% from returns plus 3% growth is about 10-11% with no change in the multiple, and that is short of the hurdle. A higher multiple would close the gap, but the readings that would justify one are the ones still open.

**Call: Wait** (price, with evidence due in February). What would move it to Own: a price near KRW 130,000 with the baseline intact; or, at today's price, FY2026 results showing year-end CET1 at or above 13.7%, a first 2027 round of at least KRW 2.6tn, Q4 NIM up and credit cost on guide, which would support a base case with faster per-share growth. What would move it to Don't own: a falsifier above, most likely the fines line.

**Model:** none on file.
