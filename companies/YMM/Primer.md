Company Overview
Full Truck Alliance (FTA; 满帮集团, Manbang Group) runs China’s largest app-based
marketplace for road freight. A factory, wholesaler or logistics company with a load to move
posts it on FTA’s apps (Yunmanman 运满满 and Huochebang 货车帮), and an independent

truck driver, usually an owner-operator, finds and accepts the load on the same app. The
company describes its platform as “a digital, standardized and smart freight platform that
connects shippers and truckers” [1]. Most of the activity is intercity full-truckload (FTL)
freight: one shipper’s goods fill one truck for a trip that averages more than 500 km [2]. The
platform also carries less-than-truckload (LTL), intra-city, cold-chain and “entrusted”
(managed) freight [2][1]. FTA’s American Depositary Shares trade on the New York Stock
Exchange under the ticker YMM; each ADS represents 20 Class A ordinary shares [1]. The
holding company is a Cayman Islands company. Its principal executive office is registered in
Guiyang, Guizhou, while operations and investor relations are run from Nanjing, and a
Shanghai office handles shareholder requests [1]. The founder, Peter Hui Zhang (张晖), is
Chairman and Chief Executive Officer. Langbo Guo (President since May 2023, previously chief
strategy officer) is in charge of finance, tax, cash planning, strategic planning and operational
analysis, and signs the annual report’s financial certification. Simon Chong Cai was CFO from
2020 until May 2025. Since then he has been Chief Financing and Investment Officer, covering
financing, investment and investor relations, and he leads the financial discussion on earnings
calls [1][3]. No CEO succession has been announced.
The business is large in activity and small in reported revenue, and it makes a lot of cash. In
FY2025 (calendar 2025, reported in RMB) the platform handled 236.3 million fulfilled orders,
up 19.8%. Average shipper monthly active users (MAUs) were 3.14 million [4]. 4.6 million
truckers fulfilled at least one order during 2025, and 4.78 million did so in the twelve months to
June 2026. FTA had 1.32 million shippers with active paying memberships at year-end 2025, and
7.23 million users had used at least one value-added service [1][5]. Net revenues were RMB12.49
billion (+11.1%), income from operations RMB4.15 billion, and net income RMB4.46 billion [1].
The company had 8,251 employees at the end of 2025. More than half of them (4,670) sit in
sales and marketing, mostly the field operations team FTA calls its “ground force” [1]. It has no
borrowings. Cash, deposits and short-term investments stood at RMB31.5 billion at end-2025
and RMB33.4 billion (US$4.9 billion) at June 30, 2026 [4][6]. All revenue is earned in mainland
China [1]. In the latest reported quarter (Q2 2026, released August 19, 2026) revenue was
RMB3.38 billion (+4.4%), fulfilled orders were 68.5 million (+12.7%), and shipper MAUs were
3.57 million (+12.8%) [6]. Q3 2026 results are scheduled for November 19, 2026 [7].
The core model is an asset-light, two-sided marketplace. FTA owns no trucks and employs no
drivers. It earns money in three ways. It charges shippers for the right to post loads
(membership “freight listing” fees). It charges truckers a commission on each load they accept
(“transaction service” fees). And where a shipper wants FTA to be the contracting carrier of
record, mainly to obtain a VAT invoice, it earns a brokerage spread (“freight brokerage”).
Around these it sells value-added services: consumer-style loans to truckers and small
shippers, insurance agency, toll (ETC), fuel and vehicle-related services [1]. The mix has moved
sharply toward the trucker commission over the past two years. Transaction service was 34.2%
of revenue in FY2024, 42.6% in FY2025 and 52.2% in Q2 2026, while freight brokerage fell from
42.1% to 33.6% to 29.4% [5]. Management’s framing since early 2026 has three parts: a shift
“from scale-first growth to balancing quality and scale”; making the company “AI-driven
infrastructure for matching, credit, and dynamic pricing”; and building “new growth curves”
(overseas expansion under the Qmove brand, and autonomous trucking through its Giga.AI
subsidiary) while keeping the core steady [3]. The standing corporate vision, set out at the 2023
Investor Day, is “to become one-stop logistics platform for enterprises” [8].

Freight brokerage is the largest line by history but the least valuable per renminbi. FTA signs
the freight contract with the shipper, issues the VAT invoice, and pays the trucker. Under US
GAAP it is an agent, so revenue is the spread between what the shipper pays and what the
trucker receives. However, VAT on the full freight value is booked gross, inside revenue, and the
matching VAT cost sits in cost of revenues net of local-government grants [1]. The result is a
line whose reported revenue is mostly a tax pass-through. Management calls brokerage a “non-
core business” with “relatively low margins” that exists for customer stickiness [9][10]. It has
been shrinking by design since an August 2025 fee increase [11].
Freight listing is a subscription business. Shippers buy annual membership packages that
unlock order posting. It grows slowly (low double digits in 2025, +3.3% in Q2 2026) and carries

deferred revenue: contract liabilities, mainly prepaid memberships, were RMB649.8 million at
end-2025 [1][6].
Transaction service is now the core profit engine. It is a percentage commission charged to
the trucker on orders that originate in commission-charging cities, and these now include “all
eligible cities”. About 95% of fulfilled orders paid a commission in Q2 2026 [2][5]. The line grew
66.7% in FY2024 and 38.2% in FY2025, and was +33.1% in each of the first two quarters of 2026
[12][4][6]. Since January 1, 2024 it has also included intra-city revenue that was previously
booked in freight listing and value-added services. FY2023 was recast to match [1].
Credit solutions is a lending book, funded mainly from FTA’s own capital, extended to
truckers and small shippers [1]. It grew through 2025 but has been shrinking deliberately in
2026: the outstanding balance fell 21.9% in the first half to RMB4.3 billion, and value-added
services revenue fell 24.9% year on year in Q2 2026, “primarily due to a decrease in credit
solutions revenues” [6].
Other value-added services are agency fees from insurers, highway authorities, gas-station
operators and vehicle dealers. Since July 2025 they also include sales of advanced driver-
assistance (ADAS) kits and ADAS-enabled carrier services from Giga.AI [1].
Realignments that break historical comparisons. There are three. (1) From January 1, 2024,
“transaction commission” was renamed “transaction service” and absorbed intra-city revenue.
2023 was recast, and the original and recast FY2023 splits do not reconcile exactly, so this
primer uses the recast basis [12][1]. (2) Giga.AI has been consolidated since July 9, 2025, adding
revenue to value-added services and cost to R&D [13]. (3) From the FY2025 release (March 12,
2026), the loan balance and non-performing loan (NPL) ratio include off-balance-sheet loans
funded by partner institutions on which FTA bears credit risk. December 2024 was restated, but
earlier quarters remain on the old basis [4]. Separately, since Q3 2025 the company has given
revenue guidance both including and excluding brokerage [14].

How the Business Model Works

The problem the platform solves
China’s long-haul road freight is carried mostly by individual owner-operators, and the cargo
comes mostly from small and mid-sized shippers. Before apps, the two sides met through
roadside “logistics information departments” (信息部) and layers of brokers in freight parks,
often over several days (Part 3 covers this). FTA’s pitch is that direct matching cuts freight costs
by roughly 10–15% compared with traditional channels and cuts matching time from three to
five days to under ten minutes (company claim) [5]. The platform’s own data support the speed
claim. The median matching time was 14 minutes in Q2 2023 and about five minutes in Q2 2026,
and the overall fulfillment rate (the share of posted orders that end up matched) rose from
about 30% in Q2 2023 to 47.0% in Q2 2026 [8][2][6]. A single trip involves one shipper, one
trucker, one freight price agreed between them, and, where monetised, several fee streams for
FTA. Following one trip through the system shows where each renminbi of revenue comes
from.

Where revenue comes from, mechanically
1. The shipper’s membership (freight listing). A shipper downloads the app and, as a non-
paying user, can post up to five orders a month. Beyond that it buys a membership. The
company offers four main tiers. RMB288 a year allows up to 30 orders, and RMB688 up to 188.
RMB1,688 allows up to 2,576 orders and is aimed at high-frequency businesses. RMB3,888
allows up to 9,136 orders over two years and targets ultra-high-volume enterprises. Paying
members are sometimes allowed extra free posts as a promotion [1][5]. The RMB288 tier was
introduced in early 2024 to monetise low-frequency direct shippers. It passed 300,000 average
monthly active members by Q2 2025 and 370,000 by Q3 2025, up more than 300% year on year.
About 20% of RMB288 members who hit their order cap upgraded to RMB688 (secondary for
the Q3 2025 figures) [15][10]. Paying shipper members passed 1.2 million in Q2 2025, reached
1.27 million in September 2025 (secondary), and stood at 1.32 million with active memberships
at December 31, 2025 [15][10][1]. Twelve-month retention of paying shippers was about 80% in
2025. That is measured as the share of January 2025 paying members who were still active
shippers in December [1]. In Q1 2026 the posting fee was waived for orders within 200 km, a
choice to trade listing revenue for short-haul volume (secondary) [16]. Membership revenue is
recognised straight-line over the term, so this line lags the member count and moves slowly [1].
2. The trucker’s commission (transaction service). When a trucker accepts an order that
originates in a commission-charging city, FTA charges the trucker a fee equal to a percentage of
the order’s freight charge. It is recognised when the shipper and trucker agree. Where an order
has no stated price, FTA charges on a fair-market price estimated by its pricing models [1].
Truckers can instead buy short memberships, usually weekly or monthly, that waive or offset
commissions; FTA recognises these ratably [1]. Each online transaction generates an electronic
agreement. The trucker pays a freight deposit into an escrow account at a third-party
commercial bank, which FTA cannot use. The deposit is released to the shipper or refunded to
the trucker when the order completes or is cancelled. In return, truckers get cancellation-fee
protection if a shipper cancels while they are en route; most of that fee is recovered from the
shipper. FTA also helps truckers collect overdue freight [1]. The 20-F describes “significant
opportunities” to extend the fee to more cities and to “raise transaction service fees rate”, while
noting that this ability “remains untested” [1]. Commissions began in August 2020 in three
cities (Hangzhou, Huzhou, Shaoxing). They covered 60 cities and 36.3% of platform GTV by
March 2021, 234 cities by Q1 2024, 273 by Q4 2025, and “all eligible cities” by Q2 2026 [17][18][3]
[2].
Two numbers drive this line: penetration (the share of fulfilled orders that pay a commission)
and monetisation per order (average commission plus trucker-membership revenue per
monetised order). Penetration rose from 69.3% in Q1 2023 to 77.4% in Q1 2024, 85.2% in Q1
2025, 88.6% in Q4 2025 and 94.7% in Q2 2026. Average monetisation per order rose from
RMB20.4 to RMB22.7, RMB25.5, RMB26.3 (Q4 2025) and about RMB26.9 (Q1 2026) [18][19][3]
[16][2]. Management attributes part of the 2026 penetration jump to “governance removing
low-quality orders” from the denominator rather than to new charging. Newly commissioned
orders also start at lower rates, which dilutes the average (secondary) [16]. Pricing is set by city,
route, vehicle type and user segment [2]. At the 2023 Investor Day management set out an
aspiration, with no time horizon, for “transaction commissions to grow by 10x” through 3x

order volume, 2x take rate on high-value orders and 1.5x penetration [8]. The penetration leg is
now close to its ceiling.
3. The brokerage spread and the VAT gross-up (freight brokerage). Many shippers,
especially companies and state-owned enterprises, need a VAT invoice for their freight spend so
they can deduct input VAT. Individual truckers generally cannot issue one. FTA’s consolidated
affiliates are licensed “online freight” (网络货运) operators. They contract with the shipper as
carrier of record and invoice it for the full freight charge plus FTA’s fee. The shipper prepays
into its platform account, and after delivery FTA pays the trucker and deducts its service fee, set
as a percentage of the shipping fee. The 20-F gives several reasons shippers choose this over
contracting a trucker directly: cargo-damage cover of up to RMB20,000 per shipment (backed
by cargo insurance in some cases), protection against truckers demanding more money or
delivering late, and regulatory compliance, chiefly the VAT invoice [1]. FTA owes VAT, at the 9%
transportation rate, on the full selling price, but has little deductible input VAT because the
trucker cannot invoice it. Local governments have historically returned part of this VAT as
“financial incentives” under cooperation agreements with the operator [1]. The accounting
presentation matters. Net revenue is the spread plus the VAT collected, which is grossed up
because FTA is primarily responsible for paying it. VAT included in total net revenues was
RMB5.10 billion in FY2024 and RMB4.67 billion in FY2025, mostly from brokerage [1]. In cost of
revenues, brokerage-related gross VAT was RMB5.01 billion (FY2023), RMB5.61 billion
(FY2024) and RMB3.67 billion (FY2025). Tax surcharges, substantially all brokerage-related,
added RMB0.89 billion, RMB1.04 billion and RMB0.86 billion. Government grants offset
RMB2.47 billion, RMB2.45 billion and RMB1.08 billion; of these, the grants received by the
online freight affiliates were RMB2.15 billion, RMB2.10 billion and RMB0.90 billion [1]. Gross
brokerage VAT exceeded net brokerage revenue in 2023 and 2024. After the August 2025 fee
increase it did not exceed it in 2025 [1]. The company says it sets the brokerage fee rate with its
VAT obligation in mind [1].
The August 2025 change follows from this. Grants have been shrinking industry-wide. Logory, a
Hong Kong-listed online freight carrier, says local “excess tax burden” subsidies have been
disrupted since mid-2025 because no national tax regime for the model has been issued [20].
On January 23, 2026, the Ministry of Transport and the State Taxation Administration issued
permanent rules for online freight carrier platforms. The rules require lawful input-VAT
deduction, bar false invoicing, and link platform waybill data to the tax authorities [21]. At FTA,
a grant receivable of RMB735 million at end-2024 had fallen to nil by end-2025, and the State
Council’s Regulations on Fair Competition Review (effective August 1, 2024) allow selective
local subsidies without a legal basis to be suspended [1]. FTA therefore raised the brokerage
service-fee rate from August 2025 and warned that volume would “significantly decline” [11].
Management said the rate was moving to 10–11% as rebates were removed (secondary) [10].
Since Q1 2026 brokerage has run on two tracks. The self-operated track keeps a roughly 10%
take rate and mainly serves SME shippers that need invoices plus matching; its retained
customers are largely state-owned shippers. The aggregator track lets licensed third-party
partners handle invoicing and settlement while FTA takes a low single-digit channel fee
(roughly 1–2%) (secondary) [16][2]. Invoicing-only customers are now a single-digit percentage
of brokerage volume, and the model is meant to “reduce our exposure to VAT refund risks”
(secondary) [2]. The third-party transcripts disagree on whether the aggregator fee is booked in
value-added services (Q1 2026 call) or in brokerage (Q2 2026 call); the company’s filings do not

settle it yet [16][2]. For scale, the 2023 Investor Day cited brokerage GTV of about RMB5.4
billion in the month of June 2023, roughly RMB65 billion annualised (our arithmetic) [8].
4. Lending (credit solutions). FTA makes cash loans to truckers and working-capital loans to
shippers, typically for less than a year. Most are funded on balance sheet by Huochebang
Microfinance, a small-loan company approved to lend online. A small portion of trucker loans
is funded by an institutional partner and guaranteed by FTA [1]. Credit limits typically do not
exceed RMB50,000 for truckers and RMB100,000 for shippers. The company says it plans “a
conservative approach” in response to regulatory developments. Its maximum undiscounted
exposure under guarantees was RMB359.1 million at end-2025 [1]. Income is interest and fees
recognised over the loan’s life. The on-balance-sheet book (principal plus accrued interest) was
RMB5.23 billion gross at end-2025, with an allowance of RMB377 million (7.2% of gross). Loans
more than 90 days past due were 3.0% of gross, up from 2.1% a year earlier [1]. On the new
definition, which includes partner-funded loans, total outstanding was RMB5.5 billion at end-
2025 and RMB4.3 billion at June 30, 2026, and the NPL ratio rose from 2.0% (December 2024)
to 2.9% (December 2025) to 3.8% (June 2026) [4][6]. All loans were moved to annualised rates
of 26% or below by end-2025 “in line with regulatory guidance”. The book is shifting toward an
asset-light model, with guarantee-backed and facilitated lending funded by partner institutions
and less from FTA’s own capital (secondary) [3][2].
5. Agency and other services. FTA earns referral and agency fees from insurers (through its
licensed broker, Shan’en Insurance Brokerage), from highway authorities (ETC toll cards), from
gas-station operators (about 12,000 partner stations, plus a Sinopec agreement covering more
than 3,000 stations in Jiangsu, Zhejiang and Anhui), and from automakers and dealers [1][16].
Since July 2025 this line also includes Giga.AI’s ADAS kit sales and ADAS-enabled carrier
services [1]. In energy, FTA recommends partner gas stations to truckers by location, processes
payment in-app, and earns a fee from the station operator as a percentage of the fuel bill. In
ETC, it earns account-opening fees from highway authorities or truckers and top-up fees based
on transaction value [1]. Exhibit 8.1 lists several “Manbang Energy Technology” entities [22].

Below gross profit, most costs are semi-fixed. People are the main cost. Of 8,251 employees at
end-2025, 4,670 (56.6%) were in sales and marketing, 1,801 (21.8%) in customer service and
operations, 1,264 (15.3%) in R&D and 516 (6.3%) in G&A [1]. Sales and marketing grew 9.5% in
FY2025 against 11.1% revenue growth, G&A fell 22.3%, and R&D was flat despite absorbing

customer-service and operations payroll, and, in lending, credit provisions. Incentives paid to
users are recorded in sales and marketing where they would otherwise push a customer’s

cumulative revenue negative (RMB201.1 million in FY2025) [1]. Operating margin went from
11.8% (FY2023) to 22.0% (FY2024), 33.2% (FY2025) and 43.9% in Q2 2026 [1][6].
Below operating income, interest income on the cash pile is material: RMB954 million in
FY2025, about 18% of pre-tax income (our arithmetic). The effective tax rate rose from 7.1%
(FY2024) to 17.2% (FY2025) as preferential-rate savings shrank and withholding tax was
accrued on a PRC-to-Hong Kong distribution [1].

The demand engine
Platform volume depends on three things: the amount of road freight moving in China, the
share of that freight that is spot-matched rather than contracted, and the share of spot
matching that happens on FTA rather than offline. The first is cyclical. It follows industrial
production, construction and consumption, and has a strong seasonal dip around Chinese New
Year in Q1 (Part 3 covers this). The second and third are structural. Orders on FTA grew about
20–24% a year in 2024–2025 while national road freight volume grew in the low single digits
(Part 3). Management describes online penetration of road freight as “extremely low”, so
platform share gains rather than the freight cycle have been the main driver (secondary) [16].
The orders are not homogeneous. The fastest-growing group, and the one management targets,
is direct shippers: manufacturers, traders and other cargo owners posting their own loads, as
opposed to professional brokers and logistics companies re-posting loads. Direct shippers’ share
of fulfilled orders rose from 47% in Q1 2024 to 55% in Q4 2025 [18][3]. Direct-shipper orders
also match more reliably: their fulfillment rate exceeded 65% in Q2 2026 against 47% for the
platform as a whole (secondary) [2]. The 2025–2026 slowdown in order growth, from +22% to
+12–14%, was attributed by management mainly to “governance” rather than weaker freight
demand. FTA removed fake accounts, loads reposted by middlemen (“freight reselling”), FTL
loads misposted as LTL carpooling, and unverified users. Trucker vehicle verification is now
close to 100% (secondary) [3]. This is a self-reported explanation. The order data are consistent
with it, since fulfillment rates rose throughout, but they cannot prove it.
On the supply side, monthly active truckers responding to orders were roughly 3 million in Q1
2026, and next-month retention of responding truckers has been above 85% in every quarter
since Q2 2024 [16][24][5]. The prospectus put the 2020 base at more than 2.8 million truckers
fulfilling orders, about 20% of China’s 13.7 million heavy- and medium-duty truck drivers at
the time [17].
Recurring versus transactional. FTA does not split revenue into recurring and cyclical
buckets. In effect, freight listing (7.8% of FY2025 revenue) and trucker memberships inside
transaction service (RMB82.7 million) are subscription-like. Credit income runs off a book. The
rest, commissions and brokerage, is transactional and moves with order volume [1].

The reinforcing structure
The platform’s economics depend on liquidity, and liquidity feeds itself. More shippers posting
loads give each trucker more choice of lanes and backhauls, which cuts empty running and
waiting time. That attracts more truckers. More truckers raise the probability that a posted
load is accepted quickly (fulfillment rate up, matching time down), which brings more

shippers, especially low-frequency direct shippers who have no broker relationship to fall back
on. Three features of FTA’s design build on this loop.
  •    Data, pricing and trust. Every matched trip adds to a closed-loop record of lanes,
       prices, trucker behaviour and disputes. FTA uses it for “tap and go” fixed-price orders,
       assigned first come, first served at prices recommended by its pricing algorithms.
       Standard orders are priced off the average of recent comparable transactions; others are
       priced by a machine-learning model (clustering plus LightGBM). The data also feed a
       knowledge graph of shippers, truckers, trucks, cargo, routes and gas stations; for
       algorithms that score the probability that a given trucker accepts a given load; for real-
       time blocking of abnormally low prices and price-reasonableness weighting in
       matching; for trucker credit ratings and priority access; and for a “Premium Cargo
       Bidding” mechanism [1][12][10]. Management argues that standalone AI agents cannot
       copy this, because long-haul matching depends on proprietary transaction data, verified
       truckers, credit records and dispute handling (secondary) [3].
  •    Monetising the trucker side. Charging truckers a commission works only if the
       platform’s loads are worth more to them than offline alternatives. Penetration rising to
       about 95% while trucker retention stayed above 85% suggests that, so far, the
       platform’s liquidity has supported the fee [5]. Management also presents trucker
       protection as part of the loop. Freight-payment protection covers all truckers and more
       than 90% of listings, alongside empty-run and cargo-damage protection, membership
       benefits and operating subsidies. In management’s words: “protecting reasonable
       trucker earnings is fundamental to creating a sustainable virtuous cycle” (secondary)
       [16][2].
  •    Adjacent services. The trucker relationship and transaction data support lending
       (credit underwriting from platform behaviour), fuel, ETC and insurance referrals.
       Brokerage, for its part, keeps invoice-needing shippers on the platform [1][10].
The loop is weakest on the trucker side. FTA’s own risk factors cite regulatory attention since
2021 to trucker income and possible limits on transaction and membership fees [1]. In May
2021 and again in January 2022, the Ministry of Transport-led joint meeting on new transport
formats summoned FTA together with other freight platforms. Its complaints included
“maliciously depressing freight rates” and “arbitrarily raising membership fees” [25][26].
Trucker complaints about low prices, freight resellers and dispute handling have also circulated
on social media (trade press, secondary) [27]. Part 3 covers this.

Go-to-market
Shipper acquisition runs through app stores and keyword search, offline advertising (high-
speed rail and subway stations) and stickers on trucks. Management describes referrals as the
“primary driver” of organic growth (secondary) [10]. Advertising spend was RMB540.7 million
in FY2025, up from RMB315.7 million in FY2023 [1]. FTA also has a large field team. It calls this
“ground force” the main driver of early user acquisition, built by visiting logistics parks town
by town, and it remains the front line for new initiatives. Sales and marketing staff numbered
4,670 at end-2025 [1]. Customer service had 1,370 staff, supported by AI-powered automated
service [1]. Ratings on both sides carry privileges. Highly rated shippers get some free posts and
priority, and highly rated truckers get priority in taking orders [1]. Enterprise and state-owned

shippers come mainly through the brokerage (invoicing) relationship. The freight offering is
now packaged as four products: express, entrusted shipping (where FTA manages fulfilment for
the shipper), general freight and LTL (secondary) [2]. The model transition under way is in
brokerage, from self-operated invoicing to the aggregator channel. Its accounting effect is to
shrink reported revenue, which carries a VAT gross-up, much faster than gross profit [16][2].

FTA is financed entirely by equity and retained cash. It has no bank borrowings or bonds [1].
The cash pile comes from the 2021 IPO (US$1.57 billion gross plus US$200 million in concurrent

Corporate History
The two founding businesses grew out of the informal freight-information trade. Huochebang
began in Guiyang in 2011 by brokering freight listings through QQ and WeChat groups.
Yunmanman started in Nanjing in 2013, and both launched mobile apps around 2013–2014 [1]
[17]. The structurally consequential events since then are:
  •      The 2017 merger. Yunmanman and Huochebang combined to form FTA, and the
         Cayman holding company was incorporated on December 27, 2017 [17][1]. The merger

       ended a subsidy war between the two leading apps. In April 2018 the combined group
       raised US$1.9 billion in its first post-merger round, co-led by the China Structural
       Reform Fund and SoftBank Vision Fund (trade press, secondary) [33]. SoftBank still
       held 7.4% of Class A shares in late 2025, down from 21.1% at end-2021 [34].
  •    Monetisation from 2018. Freight brokerage launched in January 2018 and shipper
       membership fees in 2018. Trucker commissions began in August 2020 in three cities
       [17]. These steps took FTA from an information board to a transaction platform and set
       up today’s revenue structure.
  •    The June 2021 NYSE IPO and the CAC review. FTA sold 82.5 million ADSs at US$19.00
       (US$1.57 billion gross, all primary), plus US$100 million each from Ontario Teachers’
       and a Mubadala affiliate in concurrent placements [17]. Less than two weeks after
       listing, on July 5, 2021, the Cybersecurity Review Office of the Cyberspace
       Administration of China (CAC) opened a review of the Yunmanman and Huochebang
       apps and suspended new user registration. Registration resumed on June 29, 2022 [1].
       US securities class actions filed from July 7, 2021 were settled for US$10.25 million, with
       final approval on September 5, 2024 [1]. In May 2022 the SEC named FTA under the
       HFCAA, and the PCAOB vacated its China determinations in December 2022 [1].
  •    Capital-return programme from 2023. FTA bought back US$147.7 million of shares
       in 2023 under a US$500 million authorisation [1][35]. It paid its first dividend (US$150
       million) in 2024, moved to semi-annual dividends (about US$200 million) in 2025, and
       in January 2026 adopted a policy of returning at least 50% of prior-year non-GAAP net
       income, US$400 million in FY2026 [36][12][28].
  •    The 2025 brokerage reset. On August 1, 2025 FTA raised its brokerage service-fee rate
       to reduce dependence on local-government grants, accepting a large loss of brokerage
       volume [11]. Combined with the 2026 shift to the aggregator channel, this is the largest
       change to the revenue model since commissions were introduced.
  •    Autonomous trucking: Plus to Giga.AI. FTA was an equity investor in PlusAI. In July
       2023 PlusAI split into a China entity (Plus PRC, now Giga.AI) and a US entity (Plus
       Automation), and FTA received preferred shares in both [1]. FTA lent Giga.AI US$5
       million at 12% in 2024 (repaid). It bought US$20 million of 12% convertible notes in
       May 2024 and US$16 million more in January 2025 [1]. A RMB352.7 million impairment
       in Q4 2024 related to credit impairment on an investment in an investee that could not
       meet redemption requests [1]. In May 2025 the board approved a further US$125 million
       investment [37]. On July 9, 2025, FTA paid US$85 million in cash and converted US$40
       million of notes and interest, taking 69.9% of equity and board control, and consolidated
       the business [1]. A further US$20 million purchase from Plus Automation in September
       2025 took the stake to 80.8%, and FTA still holds 17.2% of Plus Automation [1]. Among
       the executives in FTA’s May 2025 US$37.5 million repurchase of vested shares, Peter Hui
       Zhang used all of his net proceeds to buy Giga.AI preferred shares [1].
Leadership has been stable. Peter Hui Zhang has led Yunmanman since its founding in
November 2013, has been a director of the combined group since December 2017 and CEO since
December 2018, and controls 77.2% of shareholder votes through his 2.10 billion Class B shares

companies (Full Truck Logistics Information Co. and Truck Alliance Inc.). A 2021 reorganisation

Facilities. FTA leases about 41,178 m² of offices in Nanjing, plus offices in Beijing, Shanghai,

concentrations note has no customer-concentration disclosure and no customer above 10% of
revenue, which fits a base made up of millions of small shippers and individual drivers [1]. No
customers are named in any filing reviewed.

3. Industry Overview
3.1 Industry Overview
Introduction
Road freight is the main way goods move around China. Trucks carried 43.3 billion tonnes of
freight in 2025, 72.5% of all freight tonnage across road, rail, waterway and air. Because road
hauls are shorter than rail or water hauls, trucks accounted for a much smaller share of tonne-
kilometres: 7.95 trillion tonne-km, about 29% of the national total (shares are our arithmetic
from NBS figures) [44]. The industry sells transport, the movement of a load from origin to
destination. Its customers range from state-owned steel mills and automakers to millions of
small manufacturers, wholesalers and e-commerce sellers.
The economic reason the industry exists, and the reason platforms like FTA exist inside it, is
fragmentation on both sides. In 2022 China had 3.10 million registered road freight operators,
and 83.5% of them were individual operators (个体户) rather than companies (China Road
Transport Association data, secondary) [45]. Industry compilations estimate that about 87% of
road transport entities own fewer than ten trucks (vendor compilation, secondary) [46]. The
China Federation of Logistics and Purchasing (CFLP) estimates there are about 38 million truck
drivers in China, handling more than 70% of freight volume. More than 60% of them are
individual owner-operators (association survey) [47]. Shippers are just as scattered. Most cargo
comes from SMEs that ship irregularly, on lanes that change week to week, and they cannot
justify dedicated fleets or long-term contracts. Historically, layers of intermediaries brought the
two sides together, and the government’s 2024 logistics-cost action plan still describes road
freight operators as “small, scattered and weak” (小、散、弱) [48].
The industry matters to policymakers because logistics is a large cost to the economy. Total
social logistics cost, which covers all modes plus storage and administration, was RMB19.5
trillion in 2025, or 13.9% of GDP. That was down from 14.1% in 2024, and CFLP describes it as
the lowest ratio since its records began [49][50]. The ratio held at 13.9% in the first half of 2026
[51]. The State Council’s November 2024 action plan targets a ratio of about 13.5% by 2027. It

singles out the fragmented road-freight base, differentiated highway tolls, digitalisation and
standards for “online freight and other new models” as levers [48]. Digital freight platforms
therefore sit inside an explicit national cost-reduction agenda, and they are regulated with that
in mind.

How the Industry Works
The traditional value chain. In the offline model, a factory without its own fleet hands a load
to a local logistics “information department” (信息部) at a freight park. The information
department may pass the load to other brokers or post it on a blackboard and phone round for
an owner-operator. A 2018 field report from Hefei and Jinhua describes the mechanics. Brokers
earned the spread between the factory’s price and the trucker’s pay, plus a driver-paid

information fee of RMB200–500 per order. They advanced freight to drivers because factory
payment terms were longer than driver settlement. Backhaul loads paid one-third to one-half
less than outbound loads, and drivers could wait days for one (press, secondary) [62]. CIC
estimated middlemen fees at “typically 10–15% of the freight fees paid by shippers” [57]. The
model’s costs are time (waiting, empty running), price opacity and counterparty risk, since
freight was paid late or not at all.
Platform models. Digital platforms have replaced parts of this chain in three main forms.
  1.   Information-matching platforms (FTA’s core). Shippers post loads and truckers bid
       or accept. The platform charges membership or listing fees and/or a commission on the
       trip, but the contract is between shipper and trucker. Practitioner write-ups of FTA’s
       FTL flow describe the steps: the driver pays a deposit (订金) to lock in the order after
       agreeing load details, the platform charges a technical service fee equal to freight ×
       commission rate, and freight is released to the driver after the consignee signs
       (practitioner source, secondary) [63].
  2.   Online freight carrier platforms (网络货运, formerly “non-vessel/non-truck
       carriers”). The platform contracts as carrier of record with the shipper, issues the VAT
       invoice, and subcontracts to individual drivers. Revenue is gross freight and margins are
       thin. Logory reported a 9.4% gross margin in 2025 [20]. FTA’s freight brokerage is this
       model.
  3.   Asset-heavy networks, for LTL and express-LTL. These are companies such as SF
       Freight, Anneng, Kuayue and Deppon (both now JD) that operate hubs and line-haul
       themselves and sell priced services per kilogram or per shipment [64].
Intra-city platforms (Huolala, Didi’s freight service, GOGOX) run a closed-loop version of model
1 for vans and light trucks. The platform sets the price and takes a commission of roughly 9–
12% [65][66].
Why invoicing drives platform structure. Shippers registered as general VAT taxpayers
want a 9% special VAT invoice for transport so they can deduct the input tax. Individual
drivers are usually small-scale taxpayers who can issue only a 3% invoice, if they invoice at all
(financial press, secondary) [67]. A carrier platform that issues the 9% invoice owes VAT on the
full freight value. Its main cost, payments to individual drivers whose fuel, tolls and labour
often carry no deductible invoices, provides little input credit. Logory calls this a structural
“double tax burden.” Commentary on FTA dating back to 2022 noted that this input gap was
“mainly covered by government tax rebates,” which made brokerage margins depend on rebate
levels (secondary) [68]. Local governments have historically returned part of the tax to
platforms registered in their jurisdiction (“excess tax burden subsidies”). Logory describes
these as a transitional measure whose implementation “has been affected” since mid-2025,
because no national tax regime for the model has been issued and local governments have
“managed platform business volumes cautiously” [20]. CFLP’s platform monitoring found that
in 2025 about 30% of online freight platforms suspended business, operating platforms’
revenue generally fell 30–40%, and some platforms’ effective VAT burden rose to 7–9% (trade
press, secondary) [69]. The number of licensed online freight platforms fell to 3,192 at end-2025,
the first decline since 2020 (industry data cited in Logory’s results) [20].

Pricing and contracts. Owner-operator FTL is priced mostly on the spot market, lane by lane.
CFLP tracks it monthly through the China Road Logistics Freight Rate Index, with FTL and LTL
sub-indices [70]. No official split of spot and contract freight exists. Fuel (roughly 30–50% of a
trucker’s operating cost) and tolls (roughly 15–30%) dominate the cost side (trade press,
secondary) [43]. Payment terms favour the shipper. Deposits, freight-payment guarantees and
dispute handling are therefore real product features for platforms, not just conveniences.
Empty running. No official national empty-running rate is published. CFLP’s 2025 driver
survey reported that 55.1% of individual operators wait more than five hours for loads. It also
cited a fall in the “three-empties” rate (empty running, empty loading, idle time) on FTA’s
platform from 38% to 34% (association survey) [47]. Competitor Fuyou claims to have cut
empty running from an “industry average of 45%” to 6.3% (company claim) [71].

Demand Drivers and Cyclicality
Road freight volume follows industrial production, construction and consumption. CFLP’s
commentary on August 2026 freight rates illustrates the split. Heavy-goods transport demand
weakened as construction slowed, while consumer-related transport rose on summer
consumption and trade-in subsidies. Capacity “remains high, so supply exceeds demand,” and
fuel cost increases “are not fully passed on to rates” [40]. CFLP’s 2026 capacity report describes
national freight capacity as “saturated at a high level” [72]. Structurally, then, the market has
more trucks than loads, which keeps spot rates subdued and puts pressure on driver incomes.
In CFLP’s 2025 survey, 94% of individual drivers said rates were low and 79.5% reported lower
income in 2024 (secondary) [43].
Seasonality. Q1 is the weak quarter because of Chinese New Year. Factories close, drivers go
home, and volume dips before recovering in March. FTA’s own order series shows this every
year: fulfilled orders fell sequentially in Q1 2024, Q1 2025 and Q1 2026 [73][37][23]. Q3 and Q4
are the strongest.

Key Industry Trends and Developments
1. The tax and licensing reset for carrier platforms. The biggest structural change in 2025–
2026 is the end of the local-subsidy model that made VAT-invoicing brokerage viable. With
grants disrupted from mid-2025 and permanent carrier-platform rules issued in January 2026,
carrier platforms must deduct input tax “lawfully” and feed waybills to tax authorities [20][21].
The 2026 rules also formally separate carrier platforms from information-matching platforms,
which will get their own rules [21]. The effects are already visible. About 30% of online freight

platforms suspended business in 2025 [69]. Logory’s revenue fell 39.9% in 2025 [20]. FTA raised
brokerage fees and shrank that business [11]. Top online freight platforms handled 64.1% of
waybills and 54.8% of freight value at end-2025 (industry data cited by Logory) [20].
2. Fee scrutiny and “anti-involution.” FTA’s 20-F says it has attended regulatory guidance
meetings, alone and with peers. There regulators stressed setting appropriate prices, charges
and refund policies, avoiding unfair competition, transparency, and protecting trucker rights.
The filing also says MOT guidance since 2021 has “repeatedly mentioned” ensuring truck
drivers’ reasonable income and restricting transaction service and membership fees, though no
detailed rules have been issued [1]. Regulators have repeatedly targeted platform fees and low-
price competition. Freight platforms, FTA included, were summoned in 2021 and 2022 [25][26].
They signed a commission-transparency pact in July 2025 [43]. In September 2025 SAMR’s anti-
monopoly summons of Huolala cited algorithms that “unreasonably” pushed down rates and
set off “involution” [82]. Huolala then cut its fee rate from about 11% to about 9%, refunded
RMB120 million of “unreasonable fees” and dropped exclusive-sticker requirements [66]. The
2026 carrier-platform rules prohibit “unreasonable fees” charged via data or algorithms [21].
The sources reviewed found no formal national cap on freight-platform commissions [43].
3. Digitalisation and AI dispatch. Driver adoption of platforms is already high (57% of
owner-operators find cargo via apps), and platform matching cuts waiting time and transaction
costs [47][61]. A survey cited by People’s Daily put the reductions at 1–3 days down to 6–8 hours
of waiting, and 6–8% of transaction costs [61]. The next phase is AI agents that post, negotiate,
price, track and handle exceptions. FTA, Fuyou and G7 all claim AI dispatch capabilities [16][71]
[87]. CFLP describes capacity organisation moving toward “platform-based, digital and
intelligent” models [72].
4. Electrification of heavy trucks. New-energy heavy trucks went from niche to mainstream
in two years: more than 28% of 2025 heavy-truck sales (231,100 units, +182%), 38.1% of
January–September 2026 sales, and 50.3% in September 2026 [76][77]. NEV operating costs per
km are 25–60% lower for most adopters in CFLP’s survey sample [72]. For platforms,
electrification changes route economics (charging, range) and is shifting short- and medium-
haul work toward EVs. On FTA, EVs account for more than 20% of orders (secondary) [2].
Because NEVs are still only about 3% of the operating stock, the effect on long-haul FTL is
gradual [55].
5. Autonomous trucking. Several Chinese companies run commercial or pilot autonomous
freight operations with safety officers on board. Inceptio claims more than 1 billion km of
commercial autonomous mileage and works with SF Express and JD Logistics (company claim)
[88]. KargoBot reports more than 20 million km of L4 platooning (company claim) [89]. Pony.ai
holds cross-provincial expressway testing permits, and Zhijia (Plus China, now FTA’s Giga.AI)
holds China’s first licence to test heavy autonomous trucks on open roads [90]. The MOT’s 2023
trial guideline provides the regulatory basis [86]. Over the long run, autonomy changes who
supplies capacity: fleets of automated trucks rather than individual drivers. That is a structural
question for any platform built on owner-operators.
6. Consolidation of networks and logistics-cost policy. In LTL, the express-LTL segment
(about RMB184 billion, +8% in 2025) is consolidating around SF Freight (17.5% share of top-10
volume), JD (which fully privatised Deppon in 2026 after buying Kuayue in 2020), Anneng and

Kuayue. The broader large-ticket and regional LTL market (about RMB1.2 trillion) shrank
(consultancy estimates, secondary) [64][91]. Government policy favours larger, professional
carriers, differentiated highway tolls (such as Henan’s truck toll discounts of 15–30% on
selected segments) and a higher rail share [48][92].
7. Driver welfare as a policy constraint. New rules on occupational-injury insurance for
platform drivers, on-time payment and rest protection, along with CFLP and university surveys
documenting long hours and falling incomes, keep platform fees under political scrutiny [43]
[47]. The Renmin University survey put average monthly net income at RMB10,512 in 2025,
with 3.5 rest days a month (secondary) [43].

regional players in local markets, with players focused on particular segments that may move
into its own, and with providers of value-added services. It also names large technology
companies with the resources to build digital freight platforms as a potential threat [1].

Level of Competitive Intensity
Intercity FTL matching: moderate and falling, with FTA dominant. The decisive fact is
liquidity. A trucker looking for a backhaul from an unfamiliar city goes to the app with the
most loads there, and a shipper goes to the app with the most trucks. After the 2017 merger of
the two largest apps ended a subsidy war, no national challenger reached comparable two-
sided scale in long-haul. Didi and Huolala have concentrated on intra-city, and enterprise-
focused players (Fuyou, Logory, G7) serve a different customer with a different model [17][71]
[20]. Evidence of FTA’s pricing room: commission penetration rose from about 70% to about
95% between 2023 and 2026 while trucker retention stayed above 85% [18][5]. The constraint
on FTA in this segment is less a competitor than the regulator and the offline alternative.
Truckers and shippers can, and do, still find each other through brokers, WeChat groups and
freight parks.
Intra-city: high. Huolala, Didi, GOGOX and others compete on price and driver incentives. That
competition triggered SAMR’s 2025 anti-monopoly summons over “involution”, and the 2026
rectification cut Huolala’s fee rate [82][66]. Here competition hurts driver earnings, which is
exactly what regulators are watching.
Online freight carrier platforms (invoicing): high, then collapsing. Thousands of licensed
carriers competed on rebate-subsidised pricing. With subsidies disrupted and tax enforcement
data-linked, the segment is shrinking and consolidating [69][20]. Competition here was mainly
over who could offer the cheapest invoice. That basis of competition is disappearing.
LTL networks: high, consolidating. The large-ticket LTL market contracted in 2025. Scale
players (SF Freight, JD/Deppon/Kuayue, Anneng) are taking share from regional dedicated-line
carriers [64]. FTA participates in LTL by matching dedicated-line carriers rather than running a
network [30].
Barriers to entry versus barriers to scale. Building a freight app is easy. Building liquidity
across hundreds of thousands of lanes is hard, and so is building the trust infrastructure
behind it: verification, credit histories, payment protection, dispute handling. FTA’s
management makes this argument against AI-native entrants [3]. Regulation adds further
barriers. Licences are needed for carrier operations, data-security compliance is mandatory
(the 2021 CAC review froze FTA’s user growth for a year), and since 2026 tax-data integration is
required [1][21].

How intensity varies with the cycle. In a soft freight market there is too much capacity,
rates fall, and drivers resist platform fees. Regulators then tend to step in on drivers’ behalf, as
in 2021–2022 and again in 2025–2026. In a tight market shippers compete for trucks, and the
platform’s matching becomes more valuable.

Structural Dynamics and Outlook
Four forces will shape the industry over the next several years, based on the sources reviewed.
Scale advantages compounding in matching. Freight matching shows strong local network
effects. FTA’s fulfillment rate rose from about 30% (2023) to 47% (Q2 2026) and median
matching time fell from 14 minutes to about five [8][6][2]. That suggests liquidity still improves
with scale even for the leader. Fragmentation on both sides, with millions of SME shippers and
millions of owner-operators, makes it hard for asset-heavy or enterprise-focused models to
serve the long tail economically. CIC drew this contrast with the US, where shippers are
concentrated and served by scaled incumbents [57]. The 2023 collapse of Convoy, a venture-
backed US digital broker, after a freight recession and capital-market contraction, shows that
digital brokerage taking principal risk in a concentrated market works differently from asset-
light matching in a fragmented one [97].
Regulation shifting from growth to conduct and tax. The 2026 carrier-platform rules, the
pending rules for matching-only platforms, data-linked tax supervision, fee transparency
commitments and SAMR’s anti-involution enforcement all move the regulatory agenda toward
how platforms make money. Fee levels, algorithmic pricing and driver earnings are now
explicit policy objects [21][82][43]. The pending matching-platform rules (Article 31 of the 2026
Measures) bear most directly on FTA’s core model and had not been issued in the sources
reviewed [21].
Disruption risks. These are AI agents that could lower the cost of matching outside platforms,
autonomous trucks that would replace owner-operators with fleets, electrification that changes
route economics, and the eventual rationalisation of the owner-operator base that the
government’s cost-reduction plan explicitly seeks [48][88][77].
Contested segments. These include intra-city (Huolala versus Didi and others), managed or
“entrusted” shipping for enterprises (FTA, Fuyou, carrier platforms), LTL (networks versus
matching platforms connecting dedicated-line carriers), and invoicing (now moving to
compliant aggregator models). Overseas, emerging-market road freight is the next frontier for
both Lalamove, which is already international, and FTA’s QMove [58][3].
Summary. China’s road freight market is large (on the order of RMB5–6 trillion for intercity
freight), grows at low single digits, and remains highly fragmented on both sides. Platforms
have captured most drivers’ attention but still a minority of freight value. Intercity matching
has consolidated into one dominant platform, intra-city into one leader with active challengers,
and the invoicing (carrier-platform) segment is being reset by tax enforcement. The main open
variables are regulatory: how fees and algorithmic pricing will be supervised, and how
matching-only platforms will be regulated.

Three things to understand about these financials.
First, reported revenue understates the growth of the core and overstates the brokerage business.
About RMB4.7bn of FY2025 revenue was VAT collected and passed through, mostly on

and operating margins rose for the same reason. Mix explains much of the margin expansion,
and part of it is a presentation effect of the shrinking VAT gross-up.
Second, the P&L is exposed to local-government grant timing, and exposure falls as brokerage
shrinks. Grants fell from RMB2.10bn to RMB0.90bn in 2025. Their timing moved quarterly gross
margin by as much as 20 points (74% in Q1 2025, 52% in Q3 2025) [1]. The 2025–2026 shift to
higher self-operated fees and the aggregator channel reduces this sensitivity. The company has
not quantified grants for 2026.
Third, cash generation is accelerating while the cash pile earns less. FCF exceeded non-GAAP net
income in each of the last four quarters. Drivers are the run-off of the loan book, negative
working capital and minimal capex [6][2]. Interest income meanwhile fell from RMB1.14bn
(2023) to RMB0.95bn (2025) and RMB215m in Q2 2026 [1][6]. Cash is building: RMB33.4bn at
June 2026, about 82% of total equity (c). Capital-return policy (next section) therefore governs
how much of this the shareholder sees.

Key Risks to the Outlook
Brokerage tax regime and grants. The 20-F warns that grants underpin brokerage gross
profit and rest on non-statutory local agreements that could be suspended under the Fair

Competition Review rules. Grants fell 57% in 2025 [1]. The January 2026 carrier-platform rules
link waybills to tax data and require lawful input-VAT deduction [21]. Industry data show
about 30% of carrier platforms suspended business in 2025 [69]. FTA’s move to the aggregator
model reduces but does not remove exposure. Self-operated brokerage at a ~10% take rate
remains about 29% of revenue (Q2 2026) [5].
Fee regulation of matching platforms. FTA’s main profit line is a percentage commission on
truckers’ freight. The 2026 rules prohibit “unreasonable fees” via data and algorithms, and a
separate regime for matching-only platforms is pending [21]. Huolala’s 2025–2026 SAMR
rectification cut its fee rate from about 11% to about 9% and refunded “unreasonable fees” [66].
FTA was summoned over membership fees and pricing in 2021 and 2022 and signed the 2025
commission-transparency pact [25][26][43]. The 20-F lists possible limits on transaction and
membership fees as a risk [1].
Growth drivers maturing. Commission penetration is at about 95%, so transaction-service
growth depends on order growth (about +13%) and per-order monetisation [5][6]. Per-order
monetisation is the politically sensitive variable. Shipper MAU growth has slowed from about
30% to about 13%, and part of the 2025–2026 slowdown is attributed to governance actions
whose lasting effect cannot be verified from disclosures [6][3].
Credit quality. The NPL ratio rose from 2.0% to 3.8% in eighteen months, with provisions
doubling in 2H25 [4][6]. FTA keeps credit risk on partner-funded loans it guarantees [4]. Rate
caps (26%) and PBOC/NFRA guidance limit pricing [3][1]. Value-added services revenue is
already falling about 25% year on year as the book shrinks [6].
New-initiative spending and impairment. Giga.AI and Plus “have incurred significant
losses” [1]. Goodwill and intangibles were RMB4.78bn at end-2025, and the company impaired
RMB352.7m of a related investment in 2024 [1][12]. QMove is pre-revenue. Management
describes spending as paced and milestone-based (secondary) [3], but no budget is disclosed.
Freight cycle, fuel and driver economics. National road freight is growing about 3% with
capacity “saturated”, and freight rates are flat year on year [56][72][40]. Diesel spikes, as in
March–May 2026, squeeze truckers and low-value freight. Because commissions are charged as
a percentage of freight, they fall with freight rates (secondary) [2][1].
