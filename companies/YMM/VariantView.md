### What the thesis rests on

1. **Covered share.** Five years of conservative earnings plus the cash credited, over the market cap: 38% with no cash credited, **58% with cash credited at the payout rate**, 89% with all of it.
2. **How the return arrives.** With no change in the multiple, the business pays 4.3% in cash (the FY2026 target) and retains the rest. At the payout-rate reading the earnings yield is 9.2%, so a 15% return needs all-in earnings to grow about 6% a year, or the payout to rise. The dated event that could change the multiple is the **shareholder return plan for 2027, expected with Q4 results in about March 2027**. A commission cap, if one comes, is the event on the other side and has no date.
3. **Label: Discount, then Duration.** The covered share sits between one half and two-thirds, so both apply. The discount is for cash that may not reach holders and for the regulatory risk to the commission. The duration claim is that orders and earnings keep growing after the commission rollout.

Paul's estimate is below consensus and he still sees value above the price. On this frame that means he is relying on the discount leg (cash and regulatory risk priced too harshly), or on the price already assuming less growth than he does. The frame cannot say which until his numbers are on file.
