## 1.1 JD.com (JD / 9618.HK) — Company Primer

### Company Overview

JD.com is China's largest retailer by revenue: an online retailer that mostly buys goods from brands and sells them to consumers itself (first-party, or "1P"), wrapped in a marketplace for third-party sellers (3P), an advertising business, and its own nationwide logistics network. Its ADSs trade on Nasdaq (JD; one ADS = two Class A shares) and its Class A shares on the Hong Kong Stock Exchange (9618, with an RMB counter 89618) [1]. Founder Richard Liu (Liu Qiangdong) is Chairman and controls the company through Class B shares; Sandy Ran Xu is CEO and Ian Su Shan is CFO [1]. Three subsidiaries are separately listed in Hong Kong: JD Logistics (2618), JD Health (6618) and JINGDONG Industrials (7618) [1].

Scale: 2025 net revenues of RMB1,309.1bn, up 13.0% [1][5]; annual active customers above 700 million in 2025 and above 750 million on a trailing basis by Q3 2026, per management [6][7]; 776,682 employees at end-2025, or 951,420 including JD ecosystem personnel such as contract couriers [1]; over 1,600 self-operated warehouses with about 34 million square metres of floor area (including over 2,000 third-party "cloud" warehouses), plus 45 "Asia No. 1" smart logistics parks in 30 cities [1].

The core model: JD holds inventory in categories where it can be the lowest-cost, most reliable channel (electronics, home appliances, then supermarket and general merchandise), delivers those goods itself in hours or a day, and earns a thin retail margin; around that core it lets third-party merchants sell, charges them commissions and advertising, and sells logistics services to outsiders. Growth since 2024 has been led by the higher-margin service lines and general merchandise, while electronics swung with China's government trade-in subsidies. In 2025 JD added a large loss-making bet — food delivery — that took group operating income from RMB38.7bn in 2024 to RMB2.8bn [1]. The CEO's 2026 framing is "returning to positive revenue growth while accelerating profit growth", with JD Retail's operating margin heading to a long-term "healthy high single-digit" level [2][6].

### Segments

JD reports three segments [1]:

- **JD Retail** — online direct sales (1P), the online marketplace (3P), marketing services, plus JD Health and JINGDONG Industrials (both consolidated in JD Retail) and omnichannel stores (JD MALL, appliance flagship stores). 2025 revenue RMB1,126.4bn; operating income RMB51.4bn (4.6% margin) [1][5].
- **JD Logistics** — integrated supply-chain logistics for JD and external customers, express and freight (including Deppon), and since 2025 on-demand delivery for JD Food Delivery. 2025 revenue RMB217.1bn; operating income RMB5.3bn (2.4% margin) [1][5].
- **New Businesses** — JD Food Delivery, Jingxi (lower-tier value e-commerce), JD Property (logistics and industrial parks) and overseas businesses (Joybuy in Europe). 2025 revenue RMB49.3bn; operating loss RMB46.6bn [1][5]. Dada Group (on-demand delivery and JD Daojia) was taken private on June 16, 2025 and is now within the group [1].

### How the Business Model Works

#### 1. Where revenue comes from

JD reports revenue in two families [1]:

**Net product revenues (1P) — RMB1,023.8bn in 2025, 78% of revenue.** JD buys goods from brands or authorised distributors, holds them in its warehouses and sells them at a retail price; revenue is the full sale price net of returns, discounts and VAT. This is gross (principal) accounting, which is why JD's quarterly revenue (RMB309.6bn in Q3 2026) is roughly three times Alibaba's China E-commerce segment revenue (RMB98.5bn in the same quarter) even though Goldman Sachs estimates JD's GMV share at about half of Taobao/Tmall's [2][12][14]. Two lines:
- *Electronics and home appliances* — RMB605.1bn in 2025 (+7.1%): phones, computers, TVs, white goods, air conditioners. Highly sensitive to China's trade-in subsidy programme, which boosted H1 2025 and then created a high base: the line fell 12.0% in Q4 2025 and 8.4% in Q1 2026, and was –0.7% in Q3 2026 [1][2][4][5].
- *General merchandise* — RMB418.7bn in 2025 (+15.3%): supermarket (food, beverages, household goods), health, apparel, beauty, home, industrial products. Growing faster and with better margins; +8.1% in Q3 2026 [1][2].

**Net service revenues — RMB285.3bn in 2025, 22% of revenue, growing faster.** Two lines:
- *Marketplace and marketing* — RMB107.1bn in 2025 (+18.9%): commissions charged to 3P merchants as a percentage of sales, and advertising sold to both 3P merchants and 1P suppliers (search ranking, display, performance ads) [1][5]. Growth slowed to 9.6% in Q3 2026, driven mainly by double-digit advertising growth [2][6].
- *Logistics and other services* — RMB178.2bn in 2025 (+26.6%): JD Logistics' external revenue, delivery services for JD Food Delivery's merchants, JD Property income and other services [1][5].

The 1P/3P mix explains JD's margin structure. 1P revenue carries a low gross margin (cost of goods is most of it); 3P commissions and advertising have almost no cost of goods. When service revenue grows faster than product revenue, JD's consolidated gross margin rises even if nothing else changes. JD Retail's gross margin reached 20.1% in Q3 2026, its 18th consecutive quarter of year-on-year expansion, per the CFO [6].

JD Logistics earns low single digits (3.0% in Q3 2026) [2]. JD Food Delivery is loss-making; JD has not disclosed its per-order economics, but J.P. #### 3. The demand engine

JD's demand comes from Chinese household consumption of goods, with three distinct cycles:
- **Electronics and appliances** follow replacement cycles, new product launches (e.g. iPhone), and since 2024 government trade-in subsidies. China's home-appliance retail sales rose 11.0% and communication-equipment sales 20.9% in 2025, against 3.7% for total retail sales, largely because of the subsidies [11]. The 2026 programme continues with central special-bond funding, six appliance categories and four digital categories (phones, tablets, smartwatches, smart glasses) [13]. Management has flagged rising memory-chip costs as a drag on phone volumes [7].
- **Supermarket and general merchandise** behave like staples: frequent, less cyclical, driven by share gains from offline retail and other platforms.
- **Services** (advertising, logistics) follow merchant activity and JD Logistics' external customer wins.

Seasonality: traffic is weakest around Chinese New Year (Q1); peaks come with the June 18 anniversary promotion (Q2) and November 11 (Q4) [1].

#### 4. The reinforcing structure

JD's structure is a supply-chain loop rather than a traffic flywheel:
- **Scale in 1P → purchasing terms → price.** As the largest buyer of electronics and appliances, JD gets favourable terms, which supports low prices and price-protection guarantees [1].
- **Own logistics → speed and reliability → trust → frequency.** The "211" programme (orders by 11am delivered same day; by 11pm, next morning), launched in 2010, and JD's 7-day no-reason returns and price protection build the reputation for authentic goods delivered fast [1].
- **Trust and traffic → 3P merchants and advertisers.** Brands and merchants pay for access to high-intent, high-value shoppers; advertising and commission lift JD Retail's margin.
- **Logistics capacity → external revenue.** JD Logistics sells warehousing, delivery, cold chain and cross-border services to outside businesses, spreading fixed cost [1].
- **Membership.** JD PLUS (paid membership: rebates, free shipping and returns, partner perks such as Tencent Video and iQiyi) had over 40 million members at end-2025, growing double digits, with members' ARPU growing faster than the user base [1][6][7].

Food delivery was added to this loop in 2025 for traffic: management cites higher shopping frequency (+37% in Q1 2026), active-customer growth above 20% and food-delivery merchants' contribution to advertising revenue [7][8]. Whether the high-frequency, low-ticket food habit transfers to JD's high-ticket retail is the open question.

#### 5. Go-to-market and transaction model

JD sells through its apps (over 90% of 2025 orders were placed on mobile), its website, and Weixin and Mobile QQ entry points under its long-standing partnership with Tencent [1]. Offline, it runs JD MALL superstores (30 by Q2 2026), over 110 JD appliance flagship stores and 7FRESH supermarkets [3][5]. Third-party merchants sign one-year framework agreements after background checks and on-site visits, and pay commissions; top merchants earn "Five Star Store" tags [1]. Since 2023 the "Spring Dawn" programme has removed deposits and platform fees for new merchants and offers free tools and AI services; JD said in March 2024 that it had multiplied the 3P merchant base by 4.3x since January 2023 [28]. Management reported triple-digit growth in active merchants in Q1 2026 and that 3P orders exceeded 50% of total orders [8].

#### 6. Key operating metrics

JD stopped reporting GMV and quarterly active customers years ago. The metrics management uses in 2025–26:
- *JD Retail operating margin and gross margin* — the profitability benchmark [5][6].
- *Marketplace and marketing revenue growth* — the 3P/advertising health indicator [2].
- *Active customers and shopping frequency* — directional (e.g. annual active customers above 750 million; quarterly active purchasing users growing double digits for 12 straight quarters as of Q3 2026) [6].
- *JD PLUS membership* — over 40 million at end-2025 [7].
- *New Businesses operating loss* — the cost of food delivery and other bets [2].
- *Free cash flow* (operating cash flow less net capex, adjusted for consumer-financing receivables) [2].

#### 7. Funding and capital model

JD's retail model generates float: suppliers are paid on longer terms than inventory is held (accounts-payable turnover 63.3 days against inventory turnover of 41.2 days on a trailing basis at Q3 2026) [2]. The cash, restricted cash and short-term investment balance was RMB214.7bn at September 30, 2026 [2]. JD funds capex from operating cash flow, has senior notes due 2026, 2030 and 2050 and convertible notes due 2029, and had RMB243.1bn of unused revolving credit lines at end-2025 [1]. About RMB78.2bn of PRC net assets were restricted from distribution at end-2025 [1].

### Corporate History

The structurally consequential events:

- **1998–2004 — founding and move online.** Richard Liu founded Jingdong in 1998 as an electronics retailer in Beijing; it moved online in 2004 [25].
- **2007–2010 — owning logistics.** JD set up its in-house logistics in 2007 [25], introduced the 211 delivery promise in 2010 and launched the online marketplace in October 2010 [1]. The decision to own logistics, against the asset-light marketplace model, defines JD to this day.
- **2014 — Tencent partnership and Nasdaq listing.** Tencent took about 15% of JD in exchange for cash and its own e-commerce assets (Paipai, QQ Wanggou, a stake in Yixun), and gave JD entry points in WeChat and QQ; JD listed on Nasdaq the same year [1][25].
- **2016 — Walmart.** JD acquired Yihaodian from Walmart for a 5.9% equity stake; Walmart sold its JD stake in August 2024 [25].
- **2017–2025 — the listed-subsidiary model.** JD Logistics became a separate segment (2017) and listed in Hong Kong (May 28, 2021); JD Health listed (December 8, 2020); JD Property was established (2018); JINGDONG Industrials listed (December 11, 2025) [1][25]. JD itself added a Hong Kong listing in June 2020 [25].
- **2025 — food delivery and Dada.** JD Food Delivery launched in February 2025; Dada was taken private on June 16, 2025 [1]. New Businesses' operating loss widened to RMB46.6bn in 2025 [1].
- **2025–26 — CECONOMY and Europe.** JD's takeover offer for CECONOMY, parent of MediaMarkt and Saturn, secured 59.8% of shares by November 27, 2025 [1]; the deal closed on November 12, 2026 for total consideration of €1,335m, with partner Convergenta's 25.3% bringing combined ownership to 85.2% [2]. Joybuy launched in six European countries on March 16, 2026 [4].
- **April 2026 — SAMR penalty.** SAMR fined JD about RMB635m over food-safety compliance failures involving third-party cake shops and order-transfer providers [3][4].

# 2. Company Logistics

## 2.1 Customers and Distribution

### Who Are the Customers

JD serves four customer groups.

**Consumers** buy 1P goods and pay delivery fees where applicable. JD gives no user counts in its filings; management said annual active customers exceeded 700 million for 2025 (with Q4 2025 active customers up 30% year on year) and exceeded 750 million by Q3 2026; quarterly active purchasing users have grown double digits for 12 consecutive quarters; shopping frequency rose over 40% in 2025 and 37% year on year in Q1 2026, helped by food delivery [6][7][8]. JD PLUS paid members exceeded 40 million at end-2025 [7]. JD's historical core is higher-income urban consumers buying electronics and appliances; Jingxi (over one-third of new users in Q3 2026) and food delivery are its tools to reach lower-tier and younger consumers [1][6].

**Third-party merchants** pay commissions and buy advertising. JD does not give a merchant count; active merchants grew triple digits in Q1 2026, driven by new brands, industrial-belt (factory) merchants and restaurant merchants from JD Food Delivery [8]. 3P orders exceeded 50% of total orders in Q1 2026, and 3P GMV has grown faster than 1P GMV for three consecutive quarters as of Q2 2026 [8][9]. Brand flagship launches (CHANEL, ZARA, Costco's sole China e-commerce partnership) are the marquee wins of 2026 [2][3].

**Suppliers and brands as advertisers.** 1P suppliers also buy marketing services. Management attributes Q3 2026 advertising growth to brands reallocating budgets to JD, the shift toward general merchandise (which monetises better) and AI ad-targeting gains [6].

**Enterprise customers** buy logistics services from JD Logistics and industrial products from JINGDONG Industrials [1].

**Concentration.** No customer is material; JD discloses no customer above 10% of revenue [1].

### Distribution Channels

- **JD app and website** — over 90% of 2025 orders came through mobile apps [1]. JD App 16.0 and the Dongdong AI shopping assistant launched in September 2026 [2].
- **Tencent entry points** — Weixin and Mobile QQ, under the partnership that began in 2014 [1].
- **Offline** — JD MALL (30 stores at Q2 2026), over 110 JD appliance flagship stores, 7FRESH supermarkets, 7FRESH Kitchen (over 50 locations by February 2026), the first 101HOME store (Shanghai, September 28, 2026) [2][3][5][7].

- **Europe** — Joybuy online (launched in six countries March 16, 2026; 211 delivery in 80+ European towns and cities by Q3 2026) and, from November 12, 2026, CECONOMY's 1,068 MediaMarkt and Saturn stores across 11 countries [2][4][23].

Channel shifts: more 3P and more general merchandise; more advertising per transaction; food delivery's integration with retail ("deepen integration between food delivery and our core business") [9]; and AI-based shopping assistants as a new interface [2].

## 2.2 Suppliers and Procurement

**Product suppliers.** JD buys 1P goods directly from brands and authorised distributors and aims to be an authorised reseller where possible; no single supplier exceeded 10% of products purchased by value in 2025 [1]. Its largest supply relationships are with appliance and electronics brands; in Q1 2026 it signed strategic partnerships with Midea, Haier, Hisense and TCL [4]. Inventory was RMB116.2bn at September 30, 2026 (up from RMB95.4bn at end-2025), with trailing inventory turnover of 41.2 days against 35.8 days a year earlier [2].

**Payment terms.** Accounts payable were RMB197.6bn at September 30, 2026, with trailing payables turnover of 63.3 days (58.0 a year earlier) [2]. The gap between payable and inventory days is a structural source of funding; it narrowed in 2025 as inventories rose and payables fell, which held 2025 operating cash flow down to RMB19.0bn [1].

**Procurement model.** Centralised category procurement with volume rebates; consignment and joint-business-planning with large brands; a procurement AI agent that management says has generated billions of RMB in GMV on the SKUs it manages [6]; expanding private-label and C2M programmes (not quantified in sources reviewed).

**Labour.** JD's largest operating input after goods is people: 642,940 warehouse and delivery employees at end-2025 [1], and JD Ecosystem human-resource spending of RMB171.3bn over the twelve months to September 30, 2026, with total ecosystem personnel above 900,000 [2]. JD employs couriers and, for food delivery, a higher share of full-time riders with social insurance than rivals, which J.P. Morgan estimates raises its per-order rider cost [18] (secondary).

**Technology and assets.** JD runs its own cloud (JD Cloud, within JD Technology, an affiliate) and builds automated warehouses ("LangzuTech", over 20 automated warehouses in nearly 20 cities at end-2025, the first overseas in the UK); JD Logistics deployed 11 new robot types in 2026 and thousands of unmanned delivery vehicles across 20+ provinces [1][2][3][5][6].

**Commodities and inputs.** No commodity-level procurement detail is disclosed. Management cited rising memory-chip costs (raising phone and PC prices) and higher oil prices (logistics) as 2026 headwinds [6][7].

**Tariff and trade.** JD's revenue is domestic; tariff exposure is indirect, through imported goods and electronics component prices. Joybuy and CECONOMY introduce European consumer-electronics supply chains.

International activities through Q3 2026 sat in New Businesses (Joybuy, overseas JD Property, JoyExpress), whose revenue in total was RMB7.8bn in Q3 2026 [2].

# 3. Industry Overview

## 3.1 Industry Overview

### Introduction

China's online retail industry is the largest in the world. It sells physical goods — from phones and refrigerators to rice and shampoo — to consumers through apps, with delivery typically next day or faster. Three business models coexist. **Marketplaces** (Taobao/Tmall, Pinduoduo, Douyin's e-commerce) host merchants and earn commissions and, above all, advertising; they hold no inventory. **First-party retailers** (JD's core, Tmall Supermarket, Vipshop) buy, stock and resell goods and earn a retail margin. **Instant retail** (Meituan Instashopping, Taobao Instant Commerce, JD Now) delivers from nearby stores or front warehouses within an hour. The lines are blurring: marketplaces add self-operated categories, and JD has become as much a marketplace and advertising business as a retailer.

The industry exists economically because China's dense cities, low courier costs and nationwide parcel networks (largely the Tongda express companies, plus JD Logistics and SF) make delivered goods cheaper and more convenient than many offline channels, and because platforms aggregate demand that brands cannot reach as efficiently on their own.

### How the Industry Works

**Value chain.** Brand → (distributor) → platform or retailer → logistics provider → consumer. In the marketplace model the merchant owns the goods and the platform earns fees; in the 1P model JD owns the goods and earns the spread between purchase and sale price, plus supplier rebates and supplier-funded marketing.

**Margins by model.** Marketplaces earn high margins on low revenue (advertising and commission revenue of a few percent of GMV, with high operating margins at scale). 1P retailers earn low margins on high revenue (JD Retail's operating margin was 4.6% in 2025) [1]. Logistics providers earn low single digits (JD Logistics 2.4% in 2025) [1]. JD's strategy is to move its mix toward the marketplace economics while keeping 1P where it creates the trust and price leadership that bring traffic.

**Pricing and promotion.** Platforms compete on headline price, with large festivals (618, 11.11) and government-funded subsidies (trade-in) shaping demand; "lowest price" commitments and price-protection guarantees are common. JD's "Double the Price Difference" guarantee for 618 2026 is an example [29].

**Advertising.** The main profit engine for marketplaces. Merchants bid for search and feed placement; AI is increasingly used for bidding, targeting and creative generation [1][27].

**Logistics.** Most marketplace parcels move through franchised express networks (ZTO, YTO, etc.); JD and SF run direct, self-employed networks that cost more but control speed and service.

### Demand Drivers and Cyclicality

- **Household consumption** — total retail sales grew 3.7% in 2025 [11], a slow-growth backdrop.
- **Online penetration** — physical-goods online retail is 26.1% of retail sales and still rising slowly (online physical goods grew 5.2% vs 3.7% for all retail in 2025) [11].
- **Government trade-in subsidies** — the main swing factor for electronics and appliances since 2024. The 2025 programme lifted appliance retail 11.0% and communication-equipment retail 20.9% [11]; it front-loaded demand, which created a high base for JD's electronics revenue from Q4 2025 (–12.0%) through Q2 2026 (–11.8%) before normalising in Q3 2026 (–0.7%) [2][3][5]. The 2026 programme continues with central special-bond funding and local matching funds, six appliance categories and four digital categories, tighter compliance controls and no change to the overall framework [13].
- **Product cycles** — phone launches (JD highlighted minute-level delivery for the iPhone 18 Pro launch in 2026) and memory-chip price inflation [2][7].
- **Seasonality** — 618 (Q2) and 11.11 (Q4) peaks; Chinese New Year trough (Q1) [1].

### Key Industry Trends and Developments

**Instant retail and food delivery as traffic tools.** JD's entry into food delivery in February 2025 and Alibaba's response turned food delivery into a battle for daily traffic and user frequency for the whole e-commerce ecosystem. The three platforms added about RMB157bn of marketing spend between Q2 2025 and Q1 2026, per HSBC [18] (secondary). Regulators then intervened: a State Council competition investigation opened January 9, 2026; SAMR fined seven platforms (including JD) a combined RMB3.597bn on April 17, 2026; draft "Ten Rules" on delivery subsidies in June 2026 ban below-cost pricing and forcing merchants to fund subsidies [20][21][22]. By November 2026 JD said its food-delivery business was entering a "2.0" phase focused on supply-chain innovation, and its local-services head resigned [18] (secondary).

**Platform share shifts.** Douyin's content-driven e-commerce grew over 30% in 2025 and is now comparable in size to Pinduoduo; Taobao/Tmall grows mid-single digits [12] (secondary). JD's 16% estimated share has been stable in relative terms [12] (secondary).

**Anti-"involution" policy.** The 2026 Government Work Report called for rectification of "involution-style" competition; SAMR has used summons, investigations and rule-making to restrain price wars [19][21][22]. This caps the subsidy weapon for every platform.

**AI shopping assistants and AI advertising.** JD (Dongdong, Jingyan; JoyStreamer for merchants), Alibaba (Qwen app, Taobao AI search) and Meituan (Xiaotuan) are building AI interfaces; the Jingyan assistant was used by close to 80 million shoppers in Q1 2026, per trade press [30] (secondary). AI is also raising ad conversion, which JD cites as a driver of advertising growth [6].

**Supply-chain ownership in instant retail.** Platforms are building or buying front warehouses and kitchens: Alibaba's Hema front warehouses (500+ by June 2026, 1,000+ targeted), Meituan's Xiaoxiang (2,000+ warehouses) and the Dingdong acquisition, JD's Qixian Kitchen and Qixian Supermarket [18] (secondary).

**Cross-border expansion.** Chinese platforms are moving abroad — PDD's Temu, Alibaba's AliExpress, Meituan's Keeta, and JD's Joybuy plus the CECONOMY acquisition, which gives JD Europe's largest consumer-electronics retailer [2][23].

### Level of Competitive Intensity

- **Core e-commerce: high but rational in 2026.** Platforms compete hard on price during festivals and with subsidy programmes, but anti-involution regulation and slower growth push all of them toward profitability. Alibaba's China E-commerce revenue fell 5% in the September 2026 quarter after reclassifying merchant subsidies as contra-revenue [14], a sign that incentives remain elevated.
- **Electronics and appliances: moderate to high.** JD's supply-chain advantage (scale purchasing, installation, after-sales) is strongest here, but trade-in subsidies equalise price for consumers across channels.
- **General merchandise and supermarket: high.** Every platform competes in fast-moving consumer goods; JD's edge is speed and trust; PDD's and Douyin's is price and content.
- **Food delivery and instant retail: very high in 2025, still elevated.** JD faces two incumbents with greater density; J.P. Morgan estimates rivals' cost per order is RMB0.3–0.5 above Meituan's, with JD's full-time rider model adding to its cost [18] (secondary).
- **Barriers.** Barriers to entry for marketplaces are low for a traffic-rich entrant (Douyin proved this); barriers to replicating JD's 1P supply chain and self-operated logistics are high and capital-intensive (over 1,600 warehouses, 34m sq m) [1].

### Basis of Competition

| Dimension | What it means | Who it favours |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Everyday low price, subsidies, festival discounts | PDD; Douyin; JD in electronics (scale) |
| Authenticity and trust | Guaranteed genuine goods, returns, price protection | JD (1P model, "zero tolerance" for counterfeits [1]) |
| Delivery speed and reliability | Same/next-day, installation, after-sales | JD (own logistics, 211 promise); Meituan/Alibaba for <1 hour |
| Assortment | Breadth of SKUs and brands | Taobao/Tmall; JD improving via 3P |
| Content and discovery | Livestream, short video, recommendations | Douyin; Xiaohongshu |
| Traffic frequency | Daily-use entry points | Meituan (food); Alibaba (Taobao, Alipay, Amap); JD buying frequency via food delivery |
| Advertising technology | Targeting, conversion, AI tools | Alibaba (largest scale); JD (high-intent traffic) |
| Membership | Paid loyalty programmes | Alibaba 88VIP (~66m) [14]; JD PLUS (>40m) [7] |

### Structural Dynamics and Outlook

Three structural dynamics shape the industry around JD. First, **traffic is fragmenting** toward content platforms (Douyin, Xiaohongshu) and daily-frequency apps (Meituan, Taobao Instant Commerce), which is why JD bought frequency through food delivery and Jingxi. Second, **policy is now an active participant**: trade-in subsidies move electronics demand by double digits, and anti-involution rules cap promotional warfare in both e-commerce and delivery [11][13][21]. Third, **the profit pool is shifting to advertising and services**, where JD is under-monetised relative to marketplaces because most of its revenue is 1P [1][6].

Summary: China's online goods market grows mid-single digits; JD holds roughly a sixth of it with a differentiated 1P-plus-logistics model that wins on trust and speed, especially in electronics. The contested territory is frequency and daily traffic — where JD's food-delivery entry proved expensive — and, from 2026, Europe via Joybuy and CECONOMY.

The three things to understand about JD's financials:
1. **Food delivery explains almost all of the 2025 profit decline.** Group operating income fell RMB36.0bn (38.7 → 2.8) while New Businesses' loss rose RMB43.8bn and JD Retail's profit rose RMB10.3bn [1]. The 2026 recovery in group profit is mainly the reduction of that loss and of marketing spend.

**Leadership.** No CEO change announced; Sandy Xu is CEO and Richard Liu Chairman [1]. JD's local-services head, Guo Qing, resigned in November 2026 for personal reasons [18] (secondary).

### Key Risks to the Outlook

**Food delivery's cost and purpose.** New Businesses lost RMB9.6bn in Q3 2026 alone, despite the 50% reduction in food-delivery losses [2][6]. JD's order share is estimated at 6–10% [17][18] (secondary) and management gave no breakeven timeline [6]. The return on this investment depends on whether frequency gains (shopping frequency +37% in Q1 2026) convert into retail and advertising revenue [8].

**Policy-driven electronics demand.** Electronics and appliances were 46% of 2025 revenue [1]. The trade-in programme's size, timing and categories are set annually by government [13]; front-loading in 2025 produced three quarters of 8–12% declines [2][3][4][5]. Management flagged memory-chip cost inflation as a further 2026–27 headwind [7].

**CECONOMY integration.** The acquisition adds a large, low-margin (about 2% adjusted EBIT) European store-based business to a company whose Europe experience is a six-month-old Joybuy [2][4][23]. Accounting allocation, purchase-price amortisation and synergy targets have not yet been disclosed [6].

**Regulation.** JD was fined RMB635m in April 2026 for food-safety failures [3]; the State Council competition investigation and draft subsidy rules constrain promotional tactics across the industry [21][22]. JD also operates through VIE structures (6.8% of 2025 revenue) and faces PRC limits on distributing about RMB78.2bn of restricted net assets [1].

**Working capital and cash flow volatility.** Inventory days rose from 35.8 to 41.2 over the year to Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 free cash flow was –RMB23.9bn [2]. Trailing free cash flow of RMB18.8bn is well below 2024's RMB43.7bn [2][5].

**Control.** Richard Liu's 73.1% voting power means minority shareholders cannot block strategic bets such as food delivery or CECONOMY [1].

## 4.5 Questions Before Forming a View

The questions below are the ones this fact base cannot settle. Materiality reflects how much the answer would change an assessment of the business.

| # | Question | Materiality | Why it matters / where the evidence would come from |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Does JD Food Delivery reach breakeven, shrink to a contained loss, or get exited — and how large is the remaining loss in 2027? | High | RMB9.6bn New Businesses loss in Q3 2026; no breakeven date given [2][6] |
| 2 | Does food delivery's frequency gain convert into durable retail and advertising revenue? | High | The strategic rationale for the loss; JD discloses only directional user metrics [6][8] |
| 3 | How far can JD Retail's margin rise toward "high single digit", and how much of the gross-margin gain is durable (procurement, private label) versus mechanical (1P electronics shrinking)? | High | Margin engine of the group [2][6] |
| 4 | How dependent is electronics revenue on the annual trade-in programme, and what happens if it shrinks in 2027? | High | 46% of 2025 revenue; three quarters of declines on the high base [1][13] |
| 5 | What will CECONOMY contribute and cost — synergies, purchase accounting, integration — and is Europe a growth engine or a distraction? | Medium–High | €24bn of low-margin sales added from Q4 2026 [2][23] |
| 6 | Can 3P and advertising grow back to double digits on a larger base, and what is JD's ad take rate relative to Alibaba and PDD? | Medium | Marketplace and marketing growth slowed to 9.6% in Q3 2026 [2] |
| 7 | Will working capital normalise (inventory days 41 vs 36) and free cash flow return toward 2024's RMB44bn? | Medium | Funds buybacks and dividends [2][5] |
| 8 | Governance: how are major bets decided under Richard Liu's 73% voting control? | Medium | Food delivery and CECONOMY were both founder-led bets [1] |

**Further research.** JD Q4 2026 results (first CECONOMY consolidation); CECONOMY's final FY2025/26 report (December 14, 2026) and JD's purchase-price allocation; JD Logistics, JD Health and JINGDONG Industrials' own filings; the 2027 trade-in programme; broker estimates of JD food-delivery loss per order; merchant interviews on JD advertising ROI versus Tmall and PDD.

**Companies to study alongside.** Alibaba (Taobao/Tmall, Taobao Instant Commerce); PDD (value e-commerce; ad monetisation benchmark); Meituan (food delivery economics); Suning.com and Gome (electronics retail history); CECONOMY and Fnac Darty (European electronics retail); Amazon (1P/3P mix and advertising).
