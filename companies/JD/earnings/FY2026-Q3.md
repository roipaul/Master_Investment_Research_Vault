**Provisional baseline (Claude's framing, not Paul's view).** The return depends on four things: (1) JD Retail holding and slowly growing an operating profit of about RMB 54bn a year, with margin drifting up through advertising, supermarket and gross-margin mix even when electronics is flat; (2) New Businesses losses (food delivery, Jingxi, Joybuy) having peaked in 2025-26 at about RMB 39bn a year and falling by half or more by 2028-29; (3) the cash (RMB 141bn consolidated net cash, about 61% of the market value) and the free cash flow being returned through the dividend and buybacks and not spent on new ventures; (4) a starting price of about 8x depressed earnings.

What to track: JD Retail revenue growth and operating margin; electronics and home appliances against the trade-in base; marketplace and marketing growth; New Businesses loss and its split; R&D ratio; trailing free cash flow and inventory days; buyback pace and share count; Ceconomy's contribution.

What would break it: JD Retail operating income down year on year for two quarters running; 2027 New Businesses loss at or above 2026; a new large loss-making initiative or an acquisition above about US$2bn; the buyback left unused while cash goes to deals; trailing free cash flow below about RMB 10bn.

## 1. Bottom line

**Thesis: Intact (against the provisional baseline).** Revenue returned to growth as management said it would in August (+3.5%; JD Retail +2.9% from -4.7%), JD Retail held its margin against last year's record (5.8% against 5.9%), and the New Businesses loss fell 39% year on year with a first explicit commitment to cut food-delivery investment in 2027. The weak leg is capital allocation: free cash flow fell, capex doubled, Ceconomy closed, and the buyback went unmentioned. (Claude's read)

**Fresh money at US$25.43 (2026-11-17): Wait.** Price is not the obstacle: the base case returns about 28% a year over three years. The base case rests on New Businesses losses falling from about RMB 39bn to about RMB 15bn, and this quarter the loss fell only 2% from Q2 while Joybuy, Ceconomy and AI spending all grew. The readings that settle it come with Q4/FY2026 results in early March 2027: a Q4 New Businesses loss at or below RMB 9bn with a stated 2027 budget, and an annual dividend of at least US$1.00 per ADS with the buyback resumed. On price alone, the conservative case meets the 15% hurdle at about US$21. (Claude's read)

## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**A. Food delivery moves from land-grab to managed loss (call, Jefferies Q&A)**
- What changed: loss down more than 50% YoY and 7% QoQ; subsidy per order down "significantly"; group marketing expense -26.2%; investment to fall significantly in 2027. Fact: the segment loss. Claim: the 2027 path.
- Puts: the largest single drag on earnings shrinks; Jingxi "past the investment peak"; food delivery is credited with new users and order frequency.
- Takes: no breakeven date; the loss is still about RMB 9-10bn a quarter in total; Meituan said in August that industry subsidies would "take a few quarters to normalize", so a rival can reopen the fight.
- Net effect: if New Businesses loses RMB 30bn in 2027 against about RMB 39bn in 2026E, that is about RMB 7bn after tax, about 24% on 2026E earnings (e). Plays out over 2027.
- Updates: baseline leg 2. Confidence: medium on direction, low on size. Confirm: Q4 loss at or below RMB 9bn and Q1 2027 below RMB 8bn. Reverse: a Q4 loss above RMB 10bn, or the saving redirected to Joybuy.

**B. JD Retail margin mix against the AI R&D step-up (release, CFO)**
- What changed: gross margin 20.1% (+76bp), advertising up double digits, general merchandise outgrowing electronics by nine points; opex ratio +84bp with R&D +45.4%.
- Puts: 18 quarters of gross-margin gains is mix, not promotion timing; advertising is the highest-margin line; management holds the high-single-digit target.
- Takes: the gain was fully spent this quarter; R&D at 2.5% of group revenue against 1.9% is a new cost level whose return is asserted, not shown; trade-in receivable collections and subsidy-driven volume supported 2025's record base.
- Net effect: JD Retail operating income grows with revenue (low single digits) until R&D growth slows, against a baseline of 4% a year. If R&D holds at this ratio and gross margin adds 50bp a year, margin rises about 0.3-0.5pt a year from 2027 (e), about RMB 3-5bn.
- Updates: baseline leg 1. Confidence: medium. Confirm: JD Retail operating income growth above revenue growth by Q2 2027. Reverse: margin down YoY in Q4 against the 3.2% base.

**C. Europe: Ceconomy consolidated from 12 November, Joybuy scaling (release, UBS Q&A)**
- What changed: JD owns 59.8% of a store-based electronics retailer with more than 1,000 stores in 11 markets; Joybuy revenue +35% QoQ with rising investment.
- Puts: scale in JD's strongest category; sourcing and fulfilment overlap with Joybuy; bought with cash that was earning little.
- Takes: a thin-margin business in mature markets; group revenue rises by a large amount with little profit (Ceconomy's annual revenue is on the order of EUR 22-23bn as recalled from its last annual report, not re-read this run); management attention; a second loss-making expansion alongside food delivery.
- Net effect: roughly neutral to earnings in the base case, negative for the "cash comes back" leg: about RMB 10-11bn (e) of net cash spent, 4-5% of market value.
- Updates: baseline leg 3 and the "new initiative" falsifier (not triggered: below US$2bn). Confidence: low until one full quarter is reported. Confirm: Ceconomy profitable at the operating line in Q1 2027 and Joybuy's loss margin still improving.

**D. Working capital: inventory days up five quarters running (deck p.7). Unresolved: structural or cyclical**
- 35.8 → 37.8 → 38.3 → 40.5 → 41.2 days; payable days 58.0 → 63.3, so suppliers funded most of it.
- Structural reading: more supermarket and own-inventory general merchandise, offline stores (JD Mall, 101HOME). Cyclical reading: stocking electronics ahead of price rises (the CEO cited "industry price inflation" in phones; [[0700-HK]] cited DRAM up about 400%) and slower sell-through after the subsidy peak.
- What settles it: inventory days after Q4 (Singles Day sell-through) and any write-down. A reading above 42 with electronics still flat points to slow stock.

**E. On-demand delivery moved to JD Logistics (release). One-off for comparability:** New Businesses revenue and JD Logistics' internal revenue are not comparable with 2025; segment profit is.

**Expected by now and not seen:** a buyback in the quarter; operating leverage in JD Retail; any number for the food-delivery loss.

**Peers** (same set each period: Alibaba, PDD, Meituan; bellwether NBS retail sales; plus [[0700-HK]] from the vault).
- **Alibaba, June quarter (20 Aug):** customer management revenue -7%, +1% like for like, on "weaker transaction activities"; quick commerce revenue +45% with losses "substantially narrowed"; profitability targeted for FY2029. - **PDD, Q2 (24 Aug):** revenue +8%, online marketing about +3% (computed), operating profit +8%; competition "remained fierce"; quick commerce has "limited scope for synergies". Agrees on weak advertising demand; stays out of the subsidy war.
- **Meituan, Q2 (28 Aug):** core local commerce back to a 7.9% margin; subsidies "still much higher than 2024", a few quarters to normalise; Q3 delivery cost per order guided higher. Partly disagrees: easing, but not over.
- **[[0700-HK]], Q3 (18 Nov):** advertising market weak "for several months", payment value per transaction still falling. Agrees with the macro read.
- **NBS (16 Nov):** retail sales +0.4% in October; online goods +2.9% for January-October against +3.8% for January-September; communication equipment +6.5% in October from +31.1% in September. The full-year RMB 250bn of trade-in funds is fully allocated; nothing official on 2027.
- **JD Logistics, Q3 (17 Nov):** revenue +16.1%, external customers about 69% of revenue (computed), non-IFRS operating profit +37.9%. See [[china-express-delivery-cycle]].

**Phase.**
- Consumer durables and trade-in: last period **Downturn** (electronics -11.8%); now **Trough**. For: the base effect is nearly through, phones grew double digits. Against: October retail sales +0.4%, online goods slowing, and management's own warning on pulled-forward demand. Pace: as management guided in August.
- Food-delivery and instant-commerce subsidy war: last period **Rolling over** from the Q3 2025 peak; now the same, further along. For: JD marketing expense -26%, Alibaba and Meituan both reported narrower losses. Against: Meituan guided Q3 unit costs up; no player has left.

**Earnings against mid-cycle.** Group earnings are well below mid-cycle: 2026E non-GAAP net income about RMB 30bn (e) against about RMB 48bn in FY2024 (recalled from the FY2024 release, not re-read), almost all of it the New Businesses loss. JD Retail alone is at or slightly above mid-cycle on margin: 5.3% for nine months, a record, helped by 2025-26 subsidy volume.

**What the price assumes.** About 8x 2026E earnings with 61% of the market value in consolidated net cash: that the New Businesses loss is permanent or replaced by the next venture, and that the cash is not coming back.

**Next period (Q4 2026, early March 2027).** Confirms the read: electronics positive YoY; JD Retail operating income up against the 3.2% margin base; marketing ratio at or below 5.5% in the Singles Day quarter; New Businesses loss at or below RMB 9bn; inventory days no higher than 41. Phase wrong if: electronics negative again, or the New Businesses loss rises QoQ.

| Return from RMB 170.5 | +26% total, **about 8% a year** | +108% total, **about 28% a year** | **-44% total** |

What the price implies: close to the conservative case with no re-rating. The base case meets the 15% hurdle with room to spare; the conservative case meets it at about RMB 141, US$21 (214 / 1.15³). Ceconomy is carried at no net contribution in all three cases.

**Share-price move since the print:** -7.66% in the US on 17 November and -7.18% in Hong Kong on 18 November, on a quarter in which revenue returned to growth. The market appears to have taken the 7% sequential fall in net income, the flat retail margin and the Ceconomy closing as more of the same: profit improvement spent as it arrives.

**Fresh-money view.** Someone meeting JD today gets China's largest direct retailer at about 8x earnings that are depressed by a RMB 39bn annual loss in ventures started in the past two years, with net cash equal to more than half the price and a 3.9% dividend. What has to be true: that the retail core keeps its RMB 54bn of operating income once subsidies stop helping electronics; that the venture losses shrink and are not replaced; and that the cash is returned. The evidence: the core held its margin against a record base this quarter and its mix keeps improving, though the gain went into AI spending. Food delivery is being reined in and management has now put a year on it. On the third point the evidence went the wrong way: free cash flow fell, capex doubled, an acquisition closed and nothing was said about buybacks. The harsh case (-44%) is the one in which each saving funds the next expansion, and the last two years give it a real base rate.

**Call: Wait.** The business case stands and the base-case return is far above the hurdle, but it rests on a loss reduction that has been promised for 2027 and not yet delivered, and on a capital-return leg that weakened this quarter. With no thesis, debate or variant view on file, the cautious call applies. What moves it to Own: at Q4/FY2026 results (early March 2027), a New Businesses loss at or below RMB 9bn with a 2027 budget or a visible Q1 run-rate below RMB 8bn, plus an annual dividend of at least US$1.00 per ADS and repurchases resumed; or, on price alone, about US$21. What moves it to Don't own: 2027 New Businesses spending guided flat or up, another acquisition, or JD Retail operating income falling in Q4.

No earlier verdict is on file to compare with.

**Model.** None on file. **Build due** before any decision: segment model with JD Retail, JD Logistics and New Businesses separate, Ceconomy as its own line, and parent-level net cash split from the listed subsidiaries'.
