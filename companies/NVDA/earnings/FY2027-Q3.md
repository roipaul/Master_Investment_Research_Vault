Gaps: **Preliminary.** The 10-Q is not filed; the company said it will follow "in the coming weeks", unlike recent quarters, when it came on results day. NVIDIA posts no slide deck, so the CFO commentary stands in for one. **No `Thesis.md`, debate, variant view or model on file**: this run uses the provisional baseline below. Consensus figures come from press reports and are context only.

**Provisional baseline** (this is Claude's framing; Paul has not stated a view on NVDA, and the Nov-2026 chats hold Claude's suggestions, not his). The return depends on two things: (1) FY28 revenue of roughly $740B (e) gets delivered, and (2) the floor after FY28. Revenue needs to settle no more than about 15-20% below that peak, at gross margins of 67-70% or better, with the cash returned to holders. Track: the guide and the beat each quarter; gross margin against the 71-72% trough; supply commitments, guarantees and DSO; customers' capex and how it is funded; share gained by ASICs (Broadcom AI revenue, TPU); how the governance matters resolve. What would break it: FY28 growth cut below about 50%; gross margin below 68%; a guarantee called or neocloud commitments failing; an enforcement action against NVIDIA itself. Missing: a thesis, a debate with probabilities, a variant view, a model.

## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**S1. Leadership and governance.** Huang resigned 12 Nov (8-K); the board says he will not be reinstated. Kress is interim CEO and stays CFO; Heidrick & Struggles runs the search. Background: Huang admitted an affair with Alice Huang, the former Megaspeed executive US officials reportedly regard as a Chinese intelligence asset. NVIDIA's general counsel contacted Commerce during its Megaspeed probe, which then moved to DOJ (NYT via press). Semafor reports DOJ probes that could reach NVIDIA, including an antitrust inquiry; no charges are indicated. A securities class action was filed around 9-10 Nov, and Huang faces House committees in December.
- *Puts:* an independent chair; a CEO-CFO who reportedly pushed back on risky bets, so capital is likely to be allocated more conservatively; the roadmap runs 2-3 years ahead (Rubin Ultra 2027, Feynman 2028); customers have no near-term alternative at scale.
- *Takes:* loses the architect of CUDA, the platform strategy and the top customer relationships; succession is open; retention is at risk, with unvested stock down about 35% from the October peak; export-control liability; the China door is shut for good; Huang holds about 811.5M shares (about 3.4%, Form 4, 18 Sept), a potential overhang if he sells.
- *Net:* -1 to -2 turns on the exit multiple (e), about -6% to -12% of base value; the legal tail cannot be sized before the 10-Q. Plays out over 6-18 months.
- *Updates:* management quality and incentives; adds a new critical question (Q5 below).
- *Confidence:* low. Confirm: an external permanent CEO with a product background, a clean 10-Q, no charges. Reverse: an enforcement action, senior engineering departures, Huang selling.

**S2. Balance-sheet intensity.** Supply and capacity commitments are $390B (Q2 $279B), "primarily multi-year memory". Cloud and lease commitments are $102B (Q2 $74B). Maximum guarantees are $118B: $105B for SB Energy's PORTS-Pike campus (4.25 GW leased to OpenAI for 20 years, first building in FY29), $3.5B neocloud land/power/shell, $9.5B power and systems; none paid to date. Future equity commitments are $20B; DSO is up on extended terms.
- *Puts:* it locks up HBM and packaging that rivals cannot get, so the supply moat is real now; it pulls demand forward and keeps customers on NVIDIA.
- *Takes:* the asset-light model now carries fixed obligations worth about 2.4x book equity ($630B vs $267B). If the cycle turns, NVIDIA holds memory bought at peak prices and its customers' credit risk.
- *Net:* nothing in the base case. Harsh case about -$7/share (e): the guarantee's maximum is $4.3/share, plus a write-down on excess supply.
- *Updates:* Q3 (financing). *Confidence:* medium on direction. Confirm the risk if neocloud financing fails or DSO goes above 70.

**S3. Customer mix shifts to labs, neoclouds and sovereigns.** ACIE grew +195% against hyperscale +71% and is now 44% of Data Center (computed). Frontier labs are about 15% of Data Center this year.
- *Puts:* a broader base, and customers less likely to build their own ASICs.
- *Takes:* these buyers depend more on financing, and NVIDIA is often the one financing them (S2).
- *Net:* positive for growth, negative for quality; not sized. *Updates:* Q3.

**S4. Capital return step-up.** The $227.3B to be spent by the end of FY28 is about $188B annualised, or 4.8% of market value at $162.94 (computed); the dividend adds about 0.6%.
- *Puts:* about 5% shareholder yield, the share count falls 1-1.5B by FY28 (e), and SBC is now inside non-GAAP.
- *Takes:* it competes with S2 commitments if cash tightens.
- *Net:* +5% a year of yield through FY28 (e).

**S5. Gross margin reset (unresolved: structural or cyclical).** The guide goes from 74.9% to a 71-72% trough, then 72-73% in FY28; the August call tied this to memory pricing. SK Hynix posted 80% operating margins in Q3 CY26, with DRAM ASPs up high-20s% q/q.
- If structural (rising HBM content per GPU, with the memory makers holding power), margins stay at about 72%.
- If cyclical, they recover once memory supply catches up (Hynix says shortage through 2030).
- *Net:* about 3 pts, about $22B of FY28 operating profit (e). Settling reading: GM above 73% by H2 FY28 means cyclical.

**S6. China data-center compute is gone.** $2.4B of Hopper in Q3, none in the Q4 guide; about 2% of revenue. *Net:* small. The open point is the China/HK billing line ($11.05B).

**Expected but not visible:** share loss to custom silicon. NVIDIA Data Center grew +110%, against Broadcom AI semis +221% (Sept quarter, from the Oct-2026 chat note) and AMD Data Center +97%. At the margin, ASICs are growing faster. The print gives no read on share; Broadcom's Q4 FY26 (December) is the next reading.

**Peers** (latest periods):
- *TSMC*, Q3 CY26 (15 Oct): revenue +41% y/y to $46.7B; 2026 growth about 44%; AI demand "exceeds supply"; AI accelerator CAGR mid-to-high 50s% through 2029. Agrees.
- *AMD*, Q3 CY26 (3 Nov): Data Center $8.5B (+97%); Q4 guide about $17B on the MI450/Helios ramp. Agrees on demand, and is a growing alternative.
- *SK Hynix*, Q3 CY26 (28 Oct): 80% operating margin, supply booked to 2029. Agrees on demand, and shows memory taking margin from NVIDIA.
- *Hyperscalers*, Q3 CY26: Alphabet and Meta expect 2027 capex to rise significantly, and Amazon "meaningfully more". Alphabet's and Amazon's FCF turned negative, and the capex is increasingly debt-funded. This is the main disagreement with NVIDIA's tone: buyers are stretching to pay.

**Phase:** *Late cycle* for the AI data-center build. Last period: not assessed (first run).
- *For:* customers' capex is outrunning their cash flow and is debt- and vendor-financed; supplier guarantees and extended terms are rising; memory margins are at extremes; NVIDIA's own margin is guided down; the political backlash is real (39 of 40 state data-center ballot measures passed, Nov 2026).
- *Against:* supply-constrained, older GPUs re-priced upward, rental rates rising, TSMC and Hynix both short of capacity, FY28 guide raised.
- Read: demand is not the problem yet; funding is where the strain shows first.

**Earnings against mid-cycle:** over-earning. Q3 operating margin was 66.9% at about 75% gross margin. A mid-cycle EPS of about $11-14 (e) is roughly 65-80% of FY28e EPS of about $17.4. Pre-AI margins were far lower (not re-verified here).

**What the price assumes:** $162.94 is about 9.4x FY28e EPS and about 15x the Q4 annualised run-rate (13-week basis, about $10.6, e). At a 17x exit multiple, the price needs about $14 of FY31 EPS to earn 10% a year, which means revenue roughly 15-20% below FY28e. It needs about $17 to earn 15%, which means FY28 earnings held. So the market prices FY28 as close to the peak, followed by a moderate step-down, not a bust.

**Next period.** On track if: 2027 capex guides of +25% or more (late Jan to early Feb); Q4 at least $130B with a Q1 FY28 guide implying about 15% q/q or more; neocloud commitments funded; GM bottoms at 71-72%. Phase wrong (rolling over) if: a hyperscaler cuts or holds capex flat, a neocloud financing fails, rental rates fall, or DSO goes above 70 with inventory still building.

**Market reaction:** +4.8% after hours, after a fall of 32% from the 6 Oct close to 13 Nov. Read: relief that demand is intact under new management; the 9x FY28 multiple still carries a governance discount and a peak-cycle discount.

**Fresh money: Wait.** Meeting this today: at $163 you get the dominant AI-compute platform at about 9x next year's earnings, with about 5% a year coming back as buybacks and dividends. For that to work, (a) FY28 earnings must largely hold for three more years, and (b) the governance shock must not damage the roadmap or bring an export-control penalty. The evidence on (a) is strong for FY28 itself (raised guide, supply locked, peers short of capacity). It is weak beyond FY28: customers are funding capex with debt, NVIDIA is now financing and guaranteeing part of its own demand, and margin is moving to memory. On (b) there is no evidence either way until the 10-Q and the board investigation. The base case clears the hurdle by about one point, the harsh loss of 35-40% is large against the family book's 10-20% drawdown tolerance, and no variant view is on file to say what the market is missing. Between Own and Wait the rules take the cautious call.
