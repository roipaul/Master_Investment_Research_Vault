# TME: lessons from the entertainment-industry books (2026-11-24)

Sources read for this note:
- Passman, *All You Need to Know About the Music Business* (11th ed., 2023)
- Vogel, *Entertainment Industry Economics* (10th ed., music chapter)
- Smith & Telang, *Streaming, Sharing, Stealing* (2016)
- Elberse, *Blockbusters* (2013)
- Keating, *Netflixed* (2012)
- Henry, *Streaming Wars* (2025)
- Seabrook, *The Song Machine* (2015)

None of the books covers China. Each lesson below is applied to TME by analogy, and the analogy is labelled.

## Takeaways

1. **TME's margin is the exception, not the rule.** Rights holders take about 55-70% of streaming revenue, and a service's scale does not lower that cost (Passman, Seabrook). Spotify took 16 years to make a full-year profit (Henry). TME's core EBITA margin of about 30% is an outlier. The 2021 ban on exclusive licensing removed one of its sources. Undifferentiated content gets priced down toward cost (Smith & Telang), which fits Soda selling at about half TME's price. Expect drift toward lower margins, not a collapse to Spotify-like levels.

2. **Whoever controls attention gains power.** The new scarce resources are knowledge of customers and control of their attention (Smith & Telang); "distribution is the kingdom" (Vogel); TikTok is now the main place music is discovered (Passman). ByteDance owns both discovery (Douyin) and the player (Soda), much as Encarta won by being bundled free with PCs. TME's counter, the Weixin Channels funnel, is the right move but unproven: no numbers have been disclosed.

3. **Free listening lowers revenue per user, but labels push back as it grows.** Free users bring in far less than payers (Passman). New services get cheap licensing terms at first, then pay more as they scale (Passman's "technology cycle"). Labels kept Spotify's free tier desktop-only to push mobile users to pay (Seabrook). Inference: ByteDance's licence renewals are where Soda's cost advantage could narrow. Track them.

4. **Light users go to the hits; heavy users are the profit pool.** Hits capture light users (McPhee's laws, cited by Elberse), and the most profitable customers explore widely (Smith & Telang). SVIP is tiered pricing aimed at heavy users, while Soda's free, Douyin-driven hits draw light users. This fits management saying SVIP is growing while casual payers leak, and organic membership being flat. The likely outcome is a smaller, higher-paying base. Risk: lost users rarely return (Smith & Telang's NBC/iTunes and e-book evidence).

5. **The bigger bundle wins** (Smith & Telang; Henry on Spotify adding podcasts and audiobooks). Ximalaya follows the same logic even at a low return on its RMB 14bn price. ByteDance's bundle is still bigger.

6. **How incumbents lose.** Britannica protected its high-margin model and lost to a cheap, bundled, "good enough" rival (Smith & Telang). Blockbuster's costly response to Netflix was working until shareholder pressure on costs ended it (Keating). TME is responding early (Free Music app, free Kugou, Weixin funnel). Watch whether it keeps going or retreats to protect margins.

7. **Concerts and merchandise are lower-quality growth.** Live music is the most cyclical part of the business (Vogel), and superstars keep most of the economics (Passman, Elberse). Fandom can be industrialised, as K-pop shows (Seabrook, on SM Entertainment). Value this growth below membership revenue.

8. **AI-generated music** dilutes the royalty pool, and labels resist (Passman; Henry on Deezer receiving about 10,000 fully AI tracks a day). It is a cost lever for platforms that control traffic and have their own models, which favours ByteDance. Near-term effect on TME: neutral to slightly negative.

## Conclusions

**Business.** The books point to erosion more than renewal. The likely end state:
- A smaller, higher-paying subscriber base and flat to slowly falling membership revenue.
- Margins drifting from about 30% toward the mid-20s.
- Growth mainly from lower-margin concerts and merchandise.

**Investment.** This raises the weight on the conservative case.
- Scenario values per ADS at end-2029: US$16.09 (base), US$12.54 (conservative), US$8.63 (harsh); see [[earnings/FY2026-Q3]].
- Weighted 30% base, 50% conservative, 20% harsh, the expected return at US$9.45 (Nov 17, 2026) is about 10% a year.
- The 15% hurdle is met at about US$8.30.
- Fresh money: **Wait**, unchanged.

**What would move the call to Own:**
- Year-end paying users of 125m or more (FY2026 results, about March 2027).
- Labels tightening the terms of free tiers, Soda's included.
- TME sustaining its free-tier response with margins at or above about 26%.
