# Salesforce (CRM) — Dreamforce 2026 and investor session review (2026-09-17)

> **Imported** on 2026-11-26 from the Global Tech Project chat "CRM" (2026-09-17). Claude's reply is copied as written, with tool calls removed. **Claude's framing, not Paul's view.** The chat flagged a conflict of interest (Anthropic is Salesforce's partner on Claudeforce); that caveat is kept below.

## "Salesforce has released its Dreamforce presentation and transcripts in the last two days. Can you evaluate and provide the key takeaways from the conference based on our past understanding on the sector and the company?"

**Verdict:** the strategy message was important, but there was little new financial information. Salesforce confirmed it will give up the screen to defend the system of record. It gave no new numbers on our two open questions, cRPO-to-revenue conversion and Agentforce unit economics.

**What's new**
- **AIforce:** it exposes Salesforce's data, workflows, permissions and governance to any AI interface, launching with Claudeforce, Slackforce and Agentforce Coworker.
- **Pricing:** Salesforce's response to seat compression is a more expensive seat. AIforce Max is $550 per user per month. Each 1% of the core base moved to the premium edition is worth about $100M, and upgrades have built $1B of ACV over eight quarters at a 60–80% uplift.
- **Demand:** pipeline growth is in the high teens, contract lengths are increasing, account executives are up 30% over two years, and management promised organic reacceleration in H2 FY27.
- **Financial framework:** it was only reaffirmed. The $63B FY30 target was set in February, so headlines calling it new are wrong. Oppenheimer reads it as implying an 11% CAGR and a 39%+ operating margin.
- **Capital returns:** 190% of FCF is being returned in FY27, including a $25B ASR at an average of about $182 that cuts the share count by at least 14%. Benioff said the Anthropic stake cost hundreds of millions, is likely worth tens of billions, and will probably be sold to repay the buyback debt.
- **Hedges against the frontier labs:** Koa is a CRM reasoning model post-trained on Nvidia's open-weight Nemotron. Guardian and Agent Fabric govern agents from Azure, AWS and Google. That is the same control-plane claim ServiceNow makes.

**Against our framework**
- **Systems-of-record screen:** Salesforce is now explicitly selling canonical data plus governed workflow, and treating the UI as expendable. That reads well for deep systems of record such as SAP, Workday and Veeva, and badly for thin seat-based apps.
- **Bear case:** it is sharper as well. If users work inside Claude or Slack, someone else owns the front door, and a $550 seat only pays off if seat counts hold.
- **Adoption evidence:** it is thin. Siemens is a 25-user, one-week pilot that starts with read-only use. Beta numbers conflict: about 40 customers before the public link versus up to 1,000 sign-ups.
- **Earnings quality:** this is the same issue we found at Alphabet. The Q2 EPS beat included $2.61B of investment gains, and $33.3B of notes are outstanding, so the Anthropic stake is both the hidden asset and the debt backstop.

**Still unanswered**
- **Agentforce:** there was no ARR update or target, and no gross margin or inference-cost disclosure.
- **cRPO:** it grew 14% against 11% revenue growth. Informatica closed last November, so part of the 14% may be inorganic and would drop out of the year-on-year comparison in Q4. That is my inference and needs checking. The 14% Q3 guide excludes Contentful and Fin.

**Bottom line:** the ranking doesn't change. Salesforce stays the reference case and the last of our deep dives. The stock closed at $250.54 and fell 2% the next day. Sell-side targets run from $265 to $400, and Oppenheimer found the event underwhelming even as its confidence rose. The real test is the Q3 print, around early December, on organic subscription growth and Agentforce ARR.
