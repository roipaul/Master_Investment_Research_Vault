# ServiceNow (NOW) — Q3 2026 earnings evaluation and follow-up (2026-10-29 to 2026-10-30)

> **Imported** on 2026-11-26 from the Global Tech Project chat "ServiceNow" (2026-10-29 to 2026-10-30). Claude's replies are copied as written, with tool calls and the closing offer of further work removed; Paul's questions are kept as the section headings. **Claude's framing, not Paul's view.** Paul's own observation is quoted in section 2's heading.

## 1. "Can you provide a comprehensive evaluation of ServiceNow's earnings release and update?"

**Bottom line:** the quarter itself was fine; the Q4 contracted-backlog guide was not, and at a 2026-style "AI eats SaaS" multiple the market no longer extends ServiceNow the benefit of the doubt. The stock went from ~$150 to ~$110 in a day. Whether that is an opportunity hinges on one number: organic cRPO growth once you strip out Armis and management's accounting explanations — and on that number the honest answer is that it has drifted from ~20% toward the mid/high-teens.

### What was reported (Q3 2026)

- Q3 subscription revenue was $3.988B, up 21% YoY reported and 20.5% in constant currency; total revenue was $4.106B, up 20.5% reported. Subscription revenue beat by 50 bps above the high end of guidance, cRPO grew 21% cc (100 bps above guidance), and operating margin was 32.5% (150 bps above guidance).
- cRPO reached $13.65B, up 20.5% reported and 21% in constant currency; RPO was $29.7B, up 22%. The renewal rate was 98%.
- Earnings: adjusted EPS of $1.00 missed the consensus estimate of $1.02; net income of $277 million, GAAP EPS of $0.27, short of estimates of $0.33. Acquisition-related charges compressed GAAP operating margin to 9.5% from 17% a year ago, and stock-based compensation rose 29% year-over-year to $633 million in Q3.
- Cash: $839 million in operating cash flow and $822 million in free cash flow during the quarter.

### Guidance — where the damage was done

- FY26: subscription revenue of $15.785 billion, slightly above its prior midpoint, and free cash flow margin of 36%, up from 35%. It kept its subscription gross margin outlook at 81% and lifted operating margin guidance to 31.5%. The raise is token: $15 million at the midpoint from $15.77 billion to $15.785 billion — about half of the Q3 beat, nothing added for Q4.
- Q4: subscription revenue of $4.245 billion to $4.255 billion, 21.5% year-over-year growth in constant currency, cRPO growth of 18.5% and operating margin of 32%.
- The key inorganic point: Armis is expected to affect FY subscription revenue growth by ~125 bps, subscription gross margin by ~25 bps, and free cash flow margin by ~200 bps; its expected Q4 cRPO growth impact is ~175 bps. Armis closed in April, so Q3 and Q4 both carry it. Back it out and the Q4 cRPO guide is roughly 16.5–17% organic, versus ~19% organic in Q3.

Management's bridge for the step-down: early renewals pulled deals into the third quarter, the CFO said (about half the Q3 cRPO beat); a higher mix of on-prem deals and a rising balance of T4C backlog, both of which contribute to revenue but not cRPO; and a new deployment-support carve-out where only the value of those deployments are carved out of subscription revenue and they're included in professional services rather than subscription revenue. Each item is individually plausible (~25 bps on-prem, ~50 bps T4C, ~50 bps early renewals), and together they get you back to roughly 18–19% organic. The problem is that the explanations arrived at the same moment as the deceleration, and the carve-out is a new definitional change that happens to flatter the headline. The Fool's framing is the market's framing: when backlog grows slower than revenue, revenue growth usually follows it down. The raised target for AI annual contract value hasn't yet shown up as faster backlog growth.

### The AI numbers are real but still small relative to the base

- ServiceNow raised its 2026 AI annual contract value target to at least $1.7 billion, up from $1 billion at the beginning of the year. AI net new ACV grew 65% YoY; the number of AI deals over $1 million grew 4.5x year-over-year.
- Large-customer metrics stayed strong: 680 customers generating more than $5 million in annual contract value, up approximately 23%; 19 deals above $5M net new ACV (up 135%); federal $1M+ deals up 30%+.
- Pricing: management says customers still prefer seat-based predictability, with consumption ("assists") layered on top. The AI-cannibalization question (Blair) got a qualitative answer — usage rises 3–4x and renewals come with more tokens — but no quantified customer-cohort spend data. That remains the single most important unanswered question for the equity.

Arithmetic check: $1.7B of AI ACV on a ~$16B revenue base is ~10%. Even at 65% growth, AI adds roughly 4 points of total growth; the core (ITSM/ITOM/HR etc.) is therefore compounding somewhere around 14–15%. That is the number the bears are pricing.

### Quality of earnings

Three things I'd flag beyond the headline miss:

1. **Gross margin erosion.** Non-GAAP subscription gross margin narrowed to 80% from 83%. Armis explains ~25 bps; the rest is AI inference cost and the deployment teams. Guided 81% for the year implies it stays below prior levels.
2. **SBC and GAAP.** GAAP income from operations declined 33% year-over-year in Q3 to $385 million due to acquisition costs, restructuring, and stock-based compensation. SBC at ~$2.5B annualised is ~15% of revenue, so the "36% FCF margin" is closer to ~20% after treating SBC as a cost. Note the FCF raise came partly from lower capex, not operations.
3. **Balance sheet and bandwidth.** ServiceNow faces execution risks from integrating $8.8 billion in recent acquisitions, notably Armis and Veza, funded with cash and debt, so interest expense is now a line item. Also a receivables concentration worth noting: federal channel partner represents 20% of accounts receivable as of September 30, 2026.

### Valuation after the move

Street reaction was the now-familiar pattern of cutting targets while keeping Buys: UBS cut from $150 to $140 with a neutral rating, Citigroup to $157 from $170 with a buy, Jefferies $145, Canaccord $145, Keybanc issuing a 'Sell' recommendation and forecasting $110, with CLSA at $72 underperform as the low. Price ended around $110–114.

Rough math at ~$112 and ~1.05B diluted shares: market cap ~$118B. FY26 FCF ≈ 36% × ~$16.3B ≈ $5.9B → ~20x FCF, ~7x revenue, ~28x FY26 non-GAAP EPS (~$3.95). On FY27 with ~18% growth: ~17x FCF, ~24x EPS. After SBC, call it ~33x FCF. For a 98%-renewal platform at 18–20% growth, that is not expensive by its own history (five-year average P/E was ~68x), but it is no longer cheap on an SBC-adjusted basis if growth settles in the mid-teens — and that is precisely the scenario the Q4 guide hints at.

### Read-through

Salesforce fell 6% and Workday dropped 5% as investors reacted to ServiceNow's outlook. The market is treating this as a sector signal: seat-based SaaS growth is decelerating while AI monetisation is additive but not yet large enough to offset. Notably, ServiceNow was supposed to be the "AI winner" in the group; if its organic backlog can slow, the read-through to lower-quality SaaS is worse.

### What I'd take from it

- **Not thesis-breaking, but it moves the growth assumption.** Model 17–19% organic subscription growth for 2027, not 20%+. Management's "true acceleration is still ahead" line is now a 2027 promise with no numbers behind it.
- **Watch, in order:** (1) Q4 cRPO actual vs the 18.5% guide and the Armis-adjusted organic figure; (2) whether the deployment carve-out stays "temporary" or grows; (3) subscription gross margin trajectory as inference costs rise; (4) any cohort data on AI-upsell net of seat compression; (5) January's 2027 guide — anything below 18% organic sub growth confirms the bear case.
- **What would falsify a constructive view:** organic cRPO printing below ~17% for two consecutive quarters, or renewal rate slipping below 97%, which would mean AI is substituting for seats rather than expanding wallet share.
- **What would re-rate it:** 2027 organic guide at 19%+ with gross margin stabilising at 81%+, which would mark Q3 as an accounting/timing trough rather than the start of a slide.

## 2. "The deceleration seems to be confirmed, no matter if it is in the revenue recognition or in the cRPO growth, the total is decelerating. What do you think the reason is behind this? And do you think the normalized margin will be trending toward a lower base with the narrowing margin we saw from this quarter?"

Short version: I think the deceleration is mostly structural — a maturing core plus the AI pricing transition — with a cyclical layer on top, and the gross margin step-down is the real cost of that transition rather than a one-quarter blip. Operating margin can still hold because they're cutting headcount, but the margin *structure* is getting lower-quality.

### Why it's decelerating

Four things stack, in roughly this order of importance:

**1. The core is mature and the arithmetic has caught up.** 85% of the Fortune 500 already run ITSM. At a $16B base, 20% growth means finding ~$3.2B of net new revenue every year, almost entirely from existing customers. Renewal rate is 98%, so this isn't churn — it's slowing *expansion*. The Pro/Pro Plus AI SKU uplift (20–30% price increases) that powered 2024–25 is now lapping, and you can't reprice the same seat twice.

**2. AI is deflating the seat model while the consumption model hasn't filled the gap.** ServiceNow prices per fulfiller (IT agents, HR agents). The product's own pitch to customers is fewer tickets and fewer fulfillers. Hybrid/consumption is already ~50% of new business, and consumption ACV is committed more cautiously, recognised as used, and (via T4C clauses) partly excluded from cRPO. That is exactly the pattern Q4 shows: revenue guide fine, backlog guide weak. Management's refusal to give cohort data on net spend from AI adopters, while insisting it's "accretive," is the tell — if upsell clearly exceeded seat compression they'd show it.

**3. Budget crowding-out.** Enterprise IT dollars are flowing to GPUs, hyperscaler AI capacity and model vendors. Application software is where CIOs find the offset. McDermott said it himself: customers "now demand ROI," and point solutions are being eliminated. ServiceNow is on the right side of that consolidation, but a consolidator in a shrinking app-software budget still grows slower than it did when the pie was growing.

**4. Cyclical/execution noise.** Middle East slippage in Q1, federal on-prem mix, early renewals pulled forward — real, but each quarter has had a new one, and the organic trend across eight quarters (roughly 23% → 21% → 19% → ~17% guided) is too smooth to be timing. The $12B M&A spree is the behavioural confirmation: management buys growth when it sees organic growth fading.

So my read: a ~15% organic core plus an AI layer growing 60%+ off a $1.7B base, where some portion of that AI ACV is replacement rather than addition. That blends to high-teens, trending lower unless AI consumption inflects materially.

### Is the margin base lower now?

Gross margin: yes, structurally. Three drivers, none transient:
- **Inference cost.** AI SKUs carry lower gross margin than seat software, and management's own target is AI going from ~10% to 30% of ACV by 2030. Blended subscription GM mechanically declines as mix shifts — 83% → 80% this year is the first leg, not the last. I'd underwrite 78–80% as the new base.
- **Buying adoption with services.** The deployment-support carve-out is effectively free implementation to get AI live. Booked in professional services (roughly break-even margin), it drags total GM. Management calls it temporary; I'd treat it as recurring under a different name, because the "true acceleration still ahead" story depends on it.
- **Armis** (~25 bps) — small but permanent.

Operating margin: can hold or even expand modestly, but through opex, not gross margin. Total headcount is already down slightly, sales ops down "significantly," R&D and support being automated. Guide is 31.5% for the year, +50 bps, with GM down 300 bps — that's a lot of opex leverage already consumed to stand still. Two caveats on the quality of that margin: SBC at ~15% of revenue (up 29% YoY, oddly, while headcount falls — worth asking about), and FCF margin lifted partly by capex cuts, which is odd for a company claiming to scale AI inference.

Net: I'd model the normalised business as ~79% GM / 32–34% non-GAAP op margin, versus the old 83% / 30%-and-expanding. That's still a very good business. What's changed is the *direction* of margin leverage: it used to come from pricing power on a fixed cost base; now it comes from cost-cutting against a rising cost of revenue. The first is durable; the second has a floor. And if growth settles in the mid-teens, discounting pressure at renewal becomes the next margin risk — which is the one to watch, because it would show up first in net new ACV, not in the P&L.
