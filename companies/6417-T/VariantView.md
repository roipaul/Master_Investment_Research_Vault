### Value-trap check

| Test | Result | Numbers |
|---|---|---|
| Earnings base | **Unclear** | FY3/2027 plan profit of ¥40bn is about 55% above the ten-year average. The last trough averaged under ¥10bn a year (FY3/2017-22). That trough followed the 2018 rule change, which tightened payout limits and forced every older machine out of halls by January 2022, with COVID on top [S5]. The structural case for a higher base is that smart machines (from 2022-23) and relaxed specifications revived pachislot, and that SANKYO's share rose. It is plausible but unproven across a full regulatory cycle |
| Structural decline | **Fail** | Industry: the pachinko installed base fell from 2.34m to 1.87m machines in 2021-25, halls close at about 4% a year, and pachinko market units fell from 857k (FY3/2025) to 799k, with 700k planned [Primer 3.1, 4.1]. Yano puts the machine market at ¥861bn in FY2024, down 2.4%, with fewer units sold per title [S6]. Against that, SANKYO gained share: over 30% of pachinko units and first in pachislot in FY2024 [S7]. The decline is in the market, not the company, but it is not cyclical |
| Balance sheet | **Pass** | No debt. Equity is 86.5% of assets [Primer 4.2] |
| Cash reaching shareholders | **Pass**, with a note | ¥212bn returned against ¥155bn of profit in FY3/2024-26. The floor is the higher of a 50% payout or a 5% DOE, about ¥12.5bn a year [Primer 4.3]. *Note:* in FY3/2017-22 the company held cash, with net assets of about ¥337bn at FY3/2019 [S3]. It bought its headquarters from the chairman's company for ¥12.95bn [Primer 4.3]. No buyback is open for FY3/2027 |
| Earnings quality | **Pass** | Free cash flow was 89%, 117%, 101% and 74% of net profit in FY3/2023-26. The low year is the headquarters purchase; excluding it, about 102% [S8]. Japanese GAAP, no adjustments |

**Why is it cheap?** The price assumes earnings fall back towards the ten-year average. The pachinko market is shrinking, and the last four years rode a replacement wave of smart machines. The structural-decline Fail and the Unclear earnings base both support that reading.

### What the thesis rests on

1. **Covered share.** Five years at the ten-year average (¥130bn) plus ¥150bn of cash, over ¥435bn: **64%**. On the FY3/2027 plan it would be 80%, and on FY3/2026 actual 88%.
2. **How the return arrives.** No base case of Paul's is on file. On the company's plan of ¥40bn, with no re-rating, the business pays about 12-14% (earnings yield less a 0-2% decline). Of that, 4.6% is the ¥100 dividend [Primer 4.4], and the rest comes only if buybacks resume. A dated event that could move the multiple: the next medium-term plan. The present one ends in FY3/2027, so the next is likely with full-year results in about May 2027 (inferred; not announced). A buyback authorisation is decided "flexibly" [Primer 4.3].
3. **Label: Duration, then Near-term.** The covered share is between half and two-thirds. The reason given for the low price is that earnings fade. The claim the idea needs is that earnings power stays well above the ten-year average, at about ¥40bn or more, for most of the hold.
