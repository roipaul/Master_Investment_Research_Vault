**Provisional baseline (Claude's framing, not Paul's view).** At about 7 times earnings and a dividend yield near 10%, the return depends on four things: (1) Kazakhstan earnings growing again after two flat years (9M net income KZT 792bn in 2026, KZT 791bn in 2025), through lower deposit costs as the National Bank cuts and through rising marketplace monetisation; (2) consumer credit holding, with the NPL ratio near 7% and cost of risk near 0.6% a quarter while the regulator restrains consumer lending; (3) Türkiye staying a bounded bet: about US$1.36bn paid for 93.5% of Hepsiburada and about US$300m of capital for Hepsi Bank, with the investment falling from 2027; (4) the cash coming back: KZT 1,000 per ADS a quarter held or raised, inside the bank's capital headroom. Around all four sit the tenge and Kazakh tax and regulation.

What to track: adjusted EBITDA and net income growth; Fintech cost of funding; NPL ratio, cost of risk and restructured loans; average net loan portfolio growth; Marketplace GMV and take rate; Kazakhstan revenue; Türkiye investment; dividend, buyback and the k2 capital ratio; the tenge.

What would break it (Claude's proposals): adjusted EBITDA down year on year two quarters running; NPL ratio above 8% or quarterly cost of risk above 1.0%; the dividend cut below KZT 850 a quarter; more than about US$500m of further capital into Türkiye beyond the Hepsi Bank capitalisation, or a new acquisition above about US$1bn; a tax or regulatory change that removes more than about 10% of earnings.

Missing: Paul's view, primer, debate with probabilities, variant view, model.

## 1. Bottom line

**Thesis: Intact (against the provisional baseline).** No proposed falsifier triggered: adjusted EBITDA guidance of "around 5%" was kept and 9M is at +5%, cost of risk was flat at 0.6%, the NPL ratio edged down from 7.0% to 6.9%, and the dividend stayed at KZT 1,000. But growth slowed on every volume line, third-quarter EBITDA grew 1%, and the earnings recovery is a 2027 argument, not yet a number. (Claude's read)

**Fresh money at US$85.80 (2026-11-19): Wait.** Price is not the obstacle: the base case returns about 16% a year, a point over the hurdle, and it breaks even with the hurdle at about US$88. The base case needs earnings per share to grow about 10% a year in tenge, and they have not grown for two years. The readings that settle it come with Q4 results in late February 2027: cost of funding falling from 14.9%, fourth-quarter adjusted EBITDA growth of 5% or more, and 2027 guidance of about 10% with a stated Türkiye budget. (Claude's read)

## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**A. Marketplace monetisation is rising faster than volume.**
- What changed: take rate 11.9%, +90bp; e-commerce third-party take rate +210bp to 16.3%; advertising and delivery revenue +42% in constant currency (release, deck).
- Puts: revenue grows without GMV; Marketplace EBITDA +8% on GMV +8% with Hepsiburada investment inside it.
- Takes: merchants pay more while their prices per item fall; a take rate that climbs 2 points a year invites pushback from merchants or the competition authority. No sign of either was found in this run.
- Net effect: each 1 point of Marketplace take rate on about KZT 9tn of annual GMV is about KZT 90bn of revenue (e), about 6% of group EBITDA if it all dropped through. Plays out over two to three years.
- Updates: leg 1. Confidence: medium. Confirms: take rate at or above 12% with GMV growth back above 10%. Reverses: take rate flat while GMV slows.

**B. Lower ticket per item in Kazakhstan e-commerce (unresolved: structural or cyclical).**
- What changed: items +24%, value per item -8%; electronics -15%, auto goods -14%, sports and travel -17% (deck).
- Management's claim: the tenge is 20% stronger against the dollar over the year, imports are cheaper, merchants cut prices.
- Against it: the National Bank reports non-food inflation of 11.0% in September. Falling tickets alongside rising shop prices point at least partly to mix (more low-value, frequent purchases) or trading down.
- What settles it: value per item once the tenge move annualises (from mid-2027), and whether GMV outside e-commerce recovers.

**C. The funding base has outgrown the loan book.**
- What changed: customer accounts +12% since December against loans +7%; mandatory reserves +61%; securities +34% (statements). Halyk attributes half a point of its own margin loss to the new reserve rules.
- Puts: liquidity and a k2 ratio up 1.6 points, which protects the dividend.
- Takes: deposits paying about 15% are funding reserves and securities, not loans yielding about 24%.
- Net effect: KZT 188bn of extra reserves at a 15% funding cost is about KZT 28bn a year (e), about 2% of pre-tax profit. It persists until the reserve rules change.
- Updates: leg 1. Confidence: medium.

**D. Kaspi is taking on balance-sheet businesses outside its core.**
- What changed: Hepsi Bank (bought July, about US$300m of capital); Magnum Daily stores funded by Kaspi; minorities bought out for KZT 83bn (statements, deck, call).
- Puts: Türkiye lending is the route to earning a return on Hepsiburada; the store economics, if they hold, pay back in 18 months.
- Takes: both are capital-heavy and outside the high-return marketplace and payments model; the buyback lapsed in the same year.
- Net effect: not yet sized in earnings; Türkiye is "around breakeven" at EBITDA before the bank. Cash committed in 2026-27 is about US$360m (e), about 2% of market value.
- Updates: legs 3 and 4. Confidence: low until a Türkiye budget is given.

**Expected and not seen:** a net income line that grows with revenue; a renewed buyback.

**Peers.** Halyk Bank, 1H 2026 (released 2026-08-18): net income -15.3%, interest expense +21.6% against interest income +12.2%, margin 6.8% from 7.3% (7.2% without the reserve change), Stage 3 at 8.6% and rising, with management naming tighter retail-lending rules and a moratorium on selling bad retail loans. It agrees with Kaspi on the cause (funding cost and regulation) and is worse on the outcome. National Bank, 23 October 2026: consumer-lending growth is in "normalization" under its measures; household consumption is growing more slowly than a year ago; inflation risks are "tilted to the upside". That does not support a fast run of cuts.

**Phase: Trough (margin), with volumes still slowing.** The second quarter, read on the same indicators, was Downturn. For: first deposit cuts, base rate falling, credit costs flat. Against: cost of funding still rose in the quarter, loan growth and GMV slowed, and the National Bank is cautious. Payments is steadier than the other two segments but loses float income (24% of its revenue) as rates fall.

**Against mid-cycle:** under-earning. Fintech's EBITDA margin is 7.5 points below a year ago. If the cost of funding fell back three points, net income would be about 10-15% higher after lower yields on float and securities (e).

**What the price assumes:** about 7 times earnings and a 10.6% yield capitalise roughly flat earnings. No recovery is paid for.

**Next period.** Confirms: cost of funding below 14.9%, Fintech EBITDA growing again, NPL at or below 6.9%, a National Bank cut on 4 December or in January. Phase wrong: cost of funding flat or up, NPL above 7.5%, loan growth below 10%.

## 6. Valuation and the fresh-money view

- Price US$85.80 (2026-11-19). Shares 190.03m (30 Sep 2026). Market value about US$16.3bn, KZT 7.2tn.
- About 6.8 times 2026E earnings of US$12.70 (e); 2.7 times book; annualised return on equity about 40%; dividend at KZT 1,000 a quarter is US$9.07, a 10.6% yield before any withholding tax.

The base case meets the 15% hurdle at about US$88, so today's price clears it by a point. At 8 times on exit it returns about 20%. The price implies something between the conservative and base cases: low single-digit growth in tenge.

Since the print the shares are down 6.4%. The market appears to have taken the guidance cut and the flat earnings, and given no credit yet for 2027.

**Fresh money.** Someone meeting Kaspi today gets a business earning about 40% on equity, paying out two-thirds of it, at 7 times earnings. What has to be true: earnings grow again; credit holds; Türkiye and the new projects do not absorb the cash; the tenge does not give back its gain faster than assumed. The evidence on file supports the second, is turning on the first, and is silent on the third because management would not size it. The base return clears the hurdle by one point on inputs that are all estimates, with no primer or model behind them, and the harsh case loses half.

**Call: Wait** (evidence, not price). What would move it to Own: fourth-quarter results showing the cost of funding falling and EBITDA growth at 5% or better, 2027 guidance near 10%, and a Türkiye budget. What would move it to Don't own: a falsifier above.
