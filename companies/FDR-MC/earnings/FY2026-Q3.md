**Backfill.** Results came out on 2026-10-29, before the watch started; run on 2026-11-25 at Paul's request, on the same day as [[POOL]]. Valuation uses the 2026-11-24 close of EUR 18.55. That is 0.9% below the last close before the release (EUR 18.71) and 1.9% below the results-day close (EUR 18.91). The shares rose to EUR 19.86 by 4 November and have given it back.

**Provisional baseline (Claude's framing, not Paul's view).** At 13.3 times guided 2026 adjusted earnings, 9.0 times trailing adjusted EBITDA and a 3.5% dividend yield, the return depends on five things: (1) the aftermarket, about 65% of sales, growing in the low-to-mid single digits through price and a slowly growing installed base, whatever new construction does; (2) Fluidra holding or gaining share with the two large US distributors and in Europe against Chinese entrants; (3) the adjusted EBITDA margin moving from 22.9% in 2025 towards the company's "above 25%" objective, on the EUR 120m efficiency plan (2026-2030), without one-off help; (4) adjusted profit turning into cash, with restructuring charges falling away and leverage near 2 times; (5) capital put into deals and Aiper earning its cost. A recovery in new-pool construction (US 55-56 thousand in 2026 against an 80 thousand ten-year average) is upside the baseline does not need.

What to track: organic growth and its split between price and volume; North America sell-in against distributors' sell-through; Southern Europe; adjusted EBITDA margin with and without tariff refunds; efficiency savings; restructuring charges; operating working capital to sales, and how much of it is payables; net debt and leverage; Aiper's growth and margin; commercial pool.

What would break it (Claude's proposals): organic growth below zero for a full year with volume the cause; adjusted EBITDA margin back below 22.9% (the 2025 level) before refunds; restructuring and integration charges above EUR 30m a year after 2027; leverage above 3 times; loss of preferred-supplier status at Pool Corp or Heritage; an Aiper Phase II payment made at a price set by targets Aiper has not met.

Missing: Paul's view, primer, debate with probabilities, variant view, model.

No proposed falsifier is triggered. (Claude's read)

## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**A. Margin quality and the cost of the efficiency plan.**
- What changed: guide narrowed to 23.3-23.5%; refunds of about 6 net in Q3; restructuring 40 in nine months against a five-year estimate of about 50; efficiency savings 23 in nine months (plan: 120 over five years).
- Puts: the savings are arriving at about the planned rate; Tangier started production in Q3; gross margin held at 56.0% against 4% inflation, tariffs and mix.
- Takes: the savings are being consumed by inflation and mix, so little reaches the margin; the one-off costs are running far ahead of the plan's estimate; 2027 has no refunds and Europe's price increase is below inflation.
- Net effect: against a path to 25% by 2029 (about 50bp a year), 2026 delivers 20-30bp before refunds (e). On 2029 sales of about EUR 2.6bn (e), each 100bp of margin is about EUR 26m of EBITDA, roughly EUR 0.10 of adjusted EPS (e). A base case at 24.3% in 2029 in place of 25% takes about EUR 0.07 off (e).
- Updates: leg 3. Confidence: medium. Confirms: a 2027 guide mid-point of 23.8% or more with restructuring under EUR 20m. Reverses: a guide that starts at 23.5% or lower, or a new restructuring programme.

**B. Pricing power in Europe against Chinese entrants.**
- What changed: management said in terms that Europe takes about 2% and North America 3.5-5% because Europe is more open to Chinese brands. Maytronics, the robot-cleaner maker, reported Europe down 15% in local currency in Q2 and blames Chinese product diverted from the US (press).
- Puts: Fluidra owns 27% of Aiper, one of those entrants, and Aiper grew 20% in Q3; Fluidra is number one in Spain and France (company claim) and Southern Europe is up 5.9% for nine months.
- Takes: price below inflation on 44% of sales; Rest of Europe flat for a year; US tariff policy pushes more Chinese volume towards Europe.
- Net effect: on about EUR 1bn of European sales, a 2% price rise brings about EUR 20m, and 4% inflation on a cost base of about 76% of sales takes about EUR 30m; so about EUR 10m a year of EBITDA to be found from savings or volume for each year the gap persists (e), about 0.4 points of group margin. The estimate assumes Europe's cost structure matches the group's.
- Updates: legs 2 and 3. Confidence: low to medium; one statement and one peer. Confirms: Rest of Europe still flat in 2027 with price under 2%. Reverses: European price realisation at or above inflation.

**C. The North American channel.** Unresolved: structural or cyclical.
- What changed: Leslie's filed for Chapter 11 (30 September) and is closing stores; Pool Corp has a new chief executive, is cutting inventory and has agreed to buy Team Horner, which makes heat pumps and salt chlorinators under the AquaCal AutoPilot name; Pentair lost about US$280m of channel inventory in two quarters and says it will "compete to recover any lost share"; Hayward says it will pass its tariff refunds to US customers, worth about 1% of price over six months.
- Puts: Fluidra's sell-through is ahead of its sell-in, so the channel holds less of its product than a year ago; it is growing with both large distributors; early-buy orders are "in line".
- Takes: Hayward is growing three times as fast in North America (+8.7% against +2.6%), mostly on price; Pentair is coming back for share in 2027 with lower price increases (3-4%); a distributor that owns a manufacturer is a new kind of competitor if Pool Corp keeps AquaCal; Fluidra has not said it will pass refunds on, and its list increase is the highest of the three.
- Net effect: not sized. North America is 41% of sales; one point of Fluidra's North American sales is about EUR 9m.
- Updates: leg 2. Settled by: Q4 and Q1 sell-in against Hayward's and Pentair's, and what Pool Corp says on 2027-02-18 about vendor programmes and AquaCal.

**D. Capital allocation.** Buyback of EUR 40m at about EUR 19.31, cancelled. Hydrapro (France, about EUR 30m of sales) to close in early 2027; VarioPool and Riaan completed. Aiper Phase II deferred. Puts: shares bought near the low of the year; leverage steady. Takes: Aiper's 6% margin against a 15% trigger means the 27% stake (about EUR 85m paid) is earning little so far. Net effect: small. Updates: leg 5.

**Expected by now and not seen:** margin progress beyond the refunds; any turn in new construction; a number for share gain.

**Peers** (calendar Q3 2026 unless stated).
- Pentair (release, 27 October): Pool sales -17.7%, segment margin 24.6% from 32.8%. Call (third-party transcript): destock complete; sell-through up mid-single digits; 2027 sell-through planned flat with price +3-4%; "do not anticipate market recovery".
- Hayward (release and 10-Q, 28 October): net sales +6.5%, price +5.4 and volume +1.1; North America +8.7%; Europe and Rest of World -6.4%. Early-buy orders ahead of last year. Call (third party): the industry is "coalescing around flat 2027 volumes".
- Pool Corp (see [[POOL]] FY2026-Q3): sales +4% with 3 points of price; equipment +5%; inventory -3%; new pools "slightly lower" than 2025; no change expected in 2027.
- Maytronics (Q2, press): sales flat excluding currency, Europe -15%; Q3 guide down about 11% excluding currency.
- Bellwethers: Latham plans for a flat 2027; Home Depot: housing turnover "hasn't moved in 4 years" and rising rates are a pressure.
- They agree with Fluidra that the aftermarket is growing on price with little volume, that new construction is flat to down, and that 2027 looks the same. They differ on North American growth (Hayward well ahead) and on Europe (Hayward and Maytronics both down; Fluidra up, on Southern Europe).

**Phase: Trough, extended.** The same reading as the half-year. New construction has been falling since 2021 and has not turned; the housing data that lead it got worse in the autumn. The aftermarket does not have a cycle of this kind and carries the company. Against "Trough": if rates stay above 7%, 2027 could bring another leg down in new build and remodel, in which case this is still Downturn for the discretionary third of sales.
- Aftermarket (about 65% of sales): steady, price-led.
- New build and remodel, residential: Trough, no sign of recovery.
- Commercial (9%): pausing after a strong 2025; Middle East projects delayed.
- Channel inventory, North America: destocking in 2026, said to be complete at Pentair and "in a good position" at Fluidra.

**Against mid-cycle:** under-earning on volume, not on margin. New-pool volumes are about 30% below the ten-year average while the margin is at its highest level. Not sized: it needs the split of sales between new build, remodel and aftermarket by region, which a primer would give.

**What the price assumes:** at 13.3 times adjusted earnings and 9 times EBITDA, with broker estimates near 24% margin for 2027 (as cited on the call), the price pays for a flat market and modest margin progress. It does not pay for a recovery in construction. It does assume that the add-backs are temporary.

**Next period.** Confirms: Q4 organic growth of 4% or more with volume positive; North America ahead of Q3; early-buy orders up; full-year margin at 23.4% or better with the refund stated. Phase wrong (still falling): Q4 volume negative again; distributors cutting orders for the 2027 season; a 2027 new-pool estimate below 55 thousand.

The base multiple is set a little above today's 13.3 and below the 15 times or more the shares carried when the add-backs were smaller, because restructuring charges have recurred for four years. Sensitivity: at 15 times the base case returns about 16% and at today's 13.3 about 12%; if restructuring keeps running at EUR 30m a year, take about EUR 0.12 off EPS and about EUR 1.70 off value. The base case meets the 15% hurdle at about EUR 17.80, 4% below the price; the 52-week low is EUR 16.91. The price sits between the conservative and base cases.

Since the print the shares are down 1%. The market seems to have taken the narrowed guide as expected, liked the tone on 2027 for a week, and then followed the housing data down.

**Fresh money.** Someone meeting Fluidra today gets the largest pool-equipment maker, with two-thirds of sales tied to the upkeep of existing pools, at 13 times adjusted earnings and 3.5% in dividends, at the bottom of a five-year fall in new construction. What has to be true: the aftermarket keeps growing on price; share holds; the margin rises without one-offs; the adjusted profit is real. The evidence on file supports the first, from Fluidra and three peers. It half-supports the second: ahead of Pentair, behind Hayward, weaker in Europe. It does not yet support the third: this year's rise is 20-30bp before refunds, and Europe's 2027 price is below inflation. On the fourth, the largest add-back is running at three times last year's level. The harsh case costs about 30% and the balance sheet can carry it. The return is close to the hurdle without any recovery in construction, and a recovery would be extra; that is the attraction. The cautious reading is that the two things that would lift the base case, margin and add-backs, are the two the print left open.

**Call: Wait** (price and evidence; close to Own). What would move it to Own: a price near EUR 17.80 with the baseline intact; or a 2027 guide in February with a margin mid-point of 23.8% or more and restructuring under EUR 20m. What would move it to Don't own: a falsifier above; a second year of the margin guide ending at the bottom of its range; Q4 volume negative with distributors cutting 2027 orders.
