# Alcoa Corporation (AA) - FY2026 Q3 earnings update (reported 2026-10-15)

**Backfill.** Results came out on 2026-10-15, before the watch started; run on 2026-11-26 at Paul's request. Valuation uses the 2026-11-25 close of US$35.07. That is 13.2% below the last close before the release (US$40.42) and 15.4% below the next day's close (US$41.45, +2.5% on the results). Since then the three-month aluminium price on the London Metal Exchange has fallen to about US$2,780 a tonne (24 November) from a third-quarter average of US$3,226. The shares are 58% below their high of US$84.38 on 2 June and 40% below the US$58.79 at which the shares to be issued to South32 were valued in June.

**A correction to the queue note.** The note says Alcoa is buying South32's aluminium assets for US$3.1bn. That is the cash part only. The company's headline is US$4.1bn up front (US$3.1bn cash plus about 17.0m new Alcoa shares valued at about US$1.0bn in June), plus a contingent payment of up to US$750m and about US$0.6bn of assumed net debt and leases. Alcoa calls it the AliGroup acquisition. Mozal is excluded; Worsley is included.

**Provisional baseline (Claude's framing, not Paul's view).** At about 5 times this year's earnings and 3.4 times this year's EBITDA, the market is treating 2026 as a peak. The return depends on five things: (1) what the combined company earns through a cycle once the South32 assets are in, which on the company's own sensitivities is about US$3bn of EBITDA with London aluminium near US$2,800 and alumina near US$400; (2) the US tariff and the Midwest premium continuing to pay Alcoa's US smelters more than the tariff costs its Canadian metal; (3) the Alumina segment returning to profit, which needs both price and a fix to its costs: Pinjarra's stability, bauxite grade and the Western Australia mine approvals; (4) the acquisition closing on the agreed terms and debt coming down afterwards from cash flow, the Ma'aden shares and site sales; (5) EBITDA turning into free cash after capex of about US$750m, about US$360m a year of environmental and closure payments, and tax.

What to track: London price, Midwest and Rotterdam premiums, alumina index; segment EBITDA and cost per tonne in each segment; the tariff cost; alumina and aluminium production against the guide; free cash flow, working-capital days and adjusted net debt; the acquisition timetable, locked-box cash and the reset debt target; mine approvals; site sales and the Ma'aden stake; San Ciprián.

What would break it (Claude's proposals): Alumina segment EBITDA negative for the full year 2027 with alumina above US$350; mine approvals not in hand by mid-2027; Aluminum segment EBITDA below US$400m in a quarter with London above US$2,700; leverage above 2.5 times EBITDA after the acquisition closes; the acquisition re-cut on worse terms or blocked; adjusted net debt above US$5.5bn at the end of 2027.

## 1. Bottom line

**Thesis: Intact (against the provisional baseline), with the alumina leg behind.** The quarter was past the peak and still strong: adjusted EBITDA US$789m (Q2 US$901m; a year ago US$270m), free cash flow US$472m, adjusted net debt down to US$1.0bn before the acquisition. The fall was all price; costs and volumes were better than guided. But the Alumina segment made a loss for the third quarter running, its cost per tonne is 11% higher than a year ago, and the mine approvals it needs have slipped into 2027. No proposed falsifier is triggered. (Claude's read)

**Fresh money at US$35.07 (2026-11-25): Wait.** Evidence and cycle, not price. At today's three-month aluminium price the base case returns about 17% a year and clears the 15% hurdle; at a London price US$150 lower it returns nothing, and forecasters expect a surplus in 2027. Alcoa takes on about US$3.5bn of debt to close the acquisition into that. The readings that would settle it are the January outlook, the outcome of the US-Canada tariff talks and the closing itself. (Claude's read)

## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**A. The South32 assets (announced 2026-06-30; closing first half of 2027).**
- What changed: Alcoa buys 86% of the Worsley refinery and Boddington mine, all of the Hillside smelter, and South32's stakes in the Brazilian operations Alcoa already runs (36% of the Alumar refinery, 40% of its smelter, 33% of MRN). Up front US$3.1bn in cash and about 17.0m shares; up to US$750m more over four years if prices exceed set strikes; about US$0.6bn of leases and debt and US$1.1-1.2bn of rehabilitation provisions assumed. Alcoa has the assets' cash flows from 1 April 2026.
- Puts: alumina capacity up by about 5.2 Mt and aluminium by about 0.9 Mt; EBITDA of about US$0.9bn in 2025, so 5-6 times on the company's figures; pro forma first-half EPS 13% higher; full ownership economics at Alumar; the debt is raised and fixed.
- Takes: the price was set when Alcoa's shares were at US$59 and London aluminium was near a four-year high. Worsley earned US$5m before interest and tax in South32's year to June 2026 and Hillside US$581m, so the earnings bought are mostly a single third-quartile smelter in South Africa at peak prices. Leverage goes from about 0.3 times to 1.5-2.0 times as the cycle turns. The contingent payment's strikes fall after 2026 (aluminium US$2,825 for 2027), so South32 keeps some of the upside. Wood Mackenzie called it "a cycle-timed exit by South32". About a dozen approvals are outstanding in part.
- Net effect: at the base-case prices in section 6, about US$0.9bn of EBITDA for about US$4.5bn of enterprise value at the agreed share value, including the leases and the ticking fee and net of locked-box cash (e), about 5 times; about US$4.1bn with the shares at today's price. At conservative prices about US$0.7bn (e) and about 6 times.
- Updates: legs 1 and 4. Confidence: medium on closing; low on what the assets earn through a cycle. Settles it: South32's December-half segment results (February 2027 (e)); the closing balance sheet; the reset debt target.

**B. The Alumina segment's cost position.** Unresolved: structural or cyclical.
- What changed: a loss in each quarter of 2026 at realised prices of US$324-353. Cost per tonne US$354, up 11% on the year, with Kwinana closed in 2025 and Pinjarra unstable in the spring. Mine approvals (Myara North, Holyoake) now expected in 2027; the company has added six months of mining in current areas and says approval by mid-2027 does not "further materially affect supply, quality, or cost".
- Puts: cost fell US$14 a tonne in the quarter as Pinjarra recovered; the index is US$45 above Q3's realised price; rivals are constrained too (Rio's Yarwun down 40% from Q4; Hydro's Alunorte cut in August).
- Takes: third-party shipments are down 25% on the year because more alumina goes to Alcoa's own restarted smelters; the 10-K's own warning on the cost quartile; "harder decisions" if approvals slip past mid-2027; China became a net exporter of alumina in August and forecasters see a surplus of 1.3-1.5 Mt in 2026.
- Net effect: at US$400 alumina the segment earns about US$0.4bn a year on Q3 costs (e); at US$350 nothing. Each US$10 of cost per tonne is about US$95m.
- Updates: leg 3. Confidence: low. Settles it: Q4 and Q1 segment EBITDA at an index near US$390-400; the ministerial decision.

**C. The US tariff and the Midwest premium.** Unresolved: structural or cyclical.
- What changed: the tariff has been 50% since June 2025. The duty-paid premium averaged US$2,420 a tonne in Q3 and peaked at US$1.19 a pound in May. A July proclamation lets companies with approved US smelter plans import a matching volume at half the rate; Century, building in Oklahoma with EGA, qualifies. Alcoa has announced no US smelter plan in what was read.
- Puts: about 35% of Alcoa's shipments earn the duty-paid premium and another 25% the premium less duty; the US still needs about 4 Mt of imports.
- Takes: the benefit can be cut by a trade deal; Alcoa pays the tariff on Canadian metal (US$571m in 2025) and has stopped disclosing it; a new US smelter by 2029 adds domestic supply.
- Net effect: a fall in the premium from US$2,420 to about US$1,800 would take about US$0.6bn a year off EBITDA on the company's sensitivity, before any fall in the tariff paid (e).
- Updates: leg 2. Confidence: low. Settles it: the terms of any Canada deal; the tariff cost, if disclosed again.

**D. Closed sites and the Ma'aden shares as a source of cash.**
- What changed: Massena East sold for up to US$315m, US$50-65m of it at closing within six months; two more sales "could follow quickly"; the Ma'aden shares (US$1.4bn) are to be sold "over time" to cut debt.
- Puts: together up to about half of the debt taken on for the acquisition, if all of it arrives.
- Takes: Alcoa keeps the remediation liabilities; most of the Massena money depends on the buyer's data-centre build; the Ma'aden shares move with the same cycle.
- Updates: leg 4. Confidence: medium.

**E. Capital allocation.** No buybacks in 2026 with the authorisation unused and the shares at 5 times earnings; the dividend is US$0.40. Management chose to grow at the top of the cycle and pay for it with debt. That is consistent with what it says, and it means the equity carries the cycle risk with more leverage than a year ago.

**Expected by now and not seen:** the mine approvals (ministerial decision had been due by the end of 2026); a profitable Alumina segment; any return of capital.
