**Provisional baseline (Claude's framing, not Paul's view).** At 19.4 times guided 2026 adjusted earnings and about 15 times trailing operating income, the return depends on five things: (1) sales growing in the low-to-mid single digits from the upkeep of a growing installed base plus price, without help from new construction; (2) gross margin holding near 30% through scale, private label and supply-chain work, against freight costs and a shift towards larger customers; (3) expenses growing more slowly than gross profit, so that operating income grows faster than sales; (4) profit turning into cash and going back to shareholders, with leverage inside 1.5-2 times; (5) Pool Corp keeping its position as the largest distributor against Heritage (Home Depot's SRS), and using acquisitions to add density without upsetting its three main equipment suppliers. A return of new-pool construction towards the 80 thousand a year the chief executive calls normal is upside the baseline does not need.

What to track: sales growth split into price and volume; building materials and equipment growth; chemicals; gross margin and its drivers; expense growth against gross-profit growth; operating income; operating cash flow against net income; inventory, payables and receivable days; buybacks and net debt; sales-centre count; POOL360 share of sales; what the equipment makers say about the two largest distributors.

What would break it (Claude's proposals): full-year gross margin below 28.5%; expenses growing faster than gross profit for a second full year (2027); sales growth below the equipment makers' reported sell-through for two quarters running; leverage above 2.5 times while buybacks continue; operating cash flow below net income for a full year; an acquisition that puts Pool Corp into manufacturing against Pentair, Hayward or Fluidra and costs it vendor programmes.

Missing: Paul's view, primer, debate with probabilities, variant view, model.

## 1. Bottom line

**Thesis: Intact (against the provisional baseline), with the margin and cost legs behind.** Sales growth picked up to 4% and the full-year sales guide was raised; cash flow was strong and the earnings guide was held. But operating income fell 3% on sales up 4%: gross margin fell 60bp where management had guided 30bp in July, and expenses rose 5% where it had guided 2-3%. Earnings per share were flat only because the share count is 3.5% lower. No proposed falsifier is triggered. (Claude's read)

**Fresh money at US$213.85 (2026-11-24): Wait.** Price. The base case returns about 7% a year against a 15% hurdle and meets the hurdle at about US$173, which is where the shares stood the day before the results. The quarter did not show the operating leverage that the 24% rise since then pays for; the reading that could change the base case is the 2027 framework on 2027-02-18. (Claude's read)

## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**A. Gross margin: freight and customer mix.** Unresolved: structural or cyclical.
- What changed: gross margin 29.0%, down 60bp, on inbound freight, larger customers growing faster than the base, and the comparison with last year's mid-season price increases. Second quarter running of the same two causes.
- Puts: prices on freight-heavy products went up in August and are holding; a delivery surcharge since Q2; the rest of the range reprices at the start of the 2027 season; private label is growing in chemicals and building materials; larger customers add gross-profit dollars.
- Takes: management absorbed cost on jobs customers had already bid; freight was still rising in October; if service companies and builders keep consolidating, mix pressure is permanent; the makers' 2027 increases (3-5%) have to be passed through in a market with no volume growth.
- Net effect: full-year 2026 gross margin about 29.3% against 29.7%. If half of the 40bp returns in 2027 and half is mix, about US$11m of gross profit comes back, about US$0.23 a share (e).
- Updates: leg 2. Confidence: low. Settles it: Q1 and Q2 2027 gross margin after the season-start increases; a stated margin by customer size, if the framework gives one.

**B. A new chief executive and a different operating model.**
- What changed: John Watwood, who joined in January from an industrial distributor, replaced Peter Arvan in May. Field incentives, national accounts, regional pricing, a technology review with outside advisers, network consolidation, acquisitions as a stated lever.
- Puts: sales re-accelerated in his first full season; inventory and cash improved; network costs are being cut where density allows.
- Takes: expenses rose 5% with "special" incentives and outside fees he would not size; the plan of 5-8 new centres has become a net reduction of two; the growth culture of a 45-year compounder is being rebuilt by an outsider, and it is one season old.
- Net effect: not sized until the February framework. Each point of expense growth is about US$10m, about US$0.21 a share.
- Updates: leg 3. Confidence: low. Confirms: a 2027 framework with expense growth below gross-profit growth and numbers attached. Reverses: another year of expenses ahead of gross profit.

**C. Team Horner (agreed 2026-11-16; closing Q1 2027).**
- What changed: 18 HornerXpress centres, 17 in Florida; AquaCal AutoPilot (heat pumps, salt chlorinators); fiberglass-pool plants in France; stone, chemicals and pavers. Funded from the revolver and cash. Pool Corp is "evaluating the long-term opportunities" for the manufacturing businesses.
- Puts: density in its largest state against Heritage, which bought six South Florida centres in late 2025 and seven commercial sites in April 2026; a competing independent distributor leaves the market.
- Takes: no price and no sales figure given; leverage rises from 1.83; keeping AquaCal would make Pool Corp a competitor of its equipment suppliers, and selling it is a task for a new team; the deal needs regulatory approval, which suggests it is not small (inference).
- Net effect: not sized.
- Updates: leg 5. Settles it: the Q4 call and the 10-K.

**D. Capital return funded partly by debt, and the swap expiry.**
- What changed: US$375.6m of buybacks in twelve months with debt up US$105m; September purchases at US$171. Swaps on US$300m end in February 2027.
- Puts: shares bought near the low of the year; the share count falls about 3.5% a year; leverage is still inside the target.
- Takes: earnings growth per share now comes from the share count; about US$8m of extra interest in 2027 (e); Team Horner competes for the same borrowing capacity.
- Net effect: 2027 EPS about US$0.17 lower from interest (e); buybacks at the current pace add about 3% a year to EPS.
- Updates: leg 4. Confidence: high on the arithmetic.

**Expected by now and not seen:** operating leverage on a sales recovery; the 5-8 new sales centres; any improvement in new-pool construction.

It meets the 15% hurdle at about US$173 (at 18 times) or about US$191 (at 20 times). The shares closed at US$172.44 the day before the results and bottomed at US$152.70 on 8 October. The price sits above the base case. No construction recovery is in any of the three; a move from about 57 to 80 thousand new pools would add to the base case, and is not sized here.

Since the print the shares are up 24%. The market seems to have taken three things from it: volume growth is back, the guide survived a change of chief executive, and the company bought stock heavily at US$171-188. It has looked past the fall in operating income.

**Fresh money.** Someone meeting Pool Corp today gets the largest pool-supplies distributor, with most of its sales tied to the upkeep of existing pools, strong cash conversion and a long record of buying back shares, at 19 times earnings near the bottom of the construction cycle. What has to be true: sales keep growing without construction; the gross margin stops falling; expenses come back under gross profit; cash keeps funding the buybacks. The evidence on file supports the first and the fourth. On the second and third the last two quarters went the wrong way, and the fix is a plan to be presented in February by a team in its first year. The suppliers' results show Pool Corp has bargaining power over inventory; they do not show it gaining share. The harsh case costs about a third and the balance sheet can carry it. At US$173 this was a business at the hurdle with a recovery for free; at US$214 the buyer pays up front for the operating leverage and gets the recovery option at a full price.

**Call: Wait** (price). What would move it to Own: a price near US$173-190 with the baseline intact; or a February framework that shows operating income growing faster than sales in 2027, with the gross margin back near 29.7%, which would lift the base case. What would move it to Don't own: a falsifier above; a second year of expenses ahead of gross profit; Team Horner at a price or with a structure that damages vendor relations.

**Model:** none on file.
