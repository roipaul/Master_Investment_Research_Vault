Gaps:

**Provisional baseline (Claude's framing, not Paul's view).** The return depends on four things. (1) Pinduoduo's advertising line (online marketing services, RMB 218bn in FY2025) resumes high-single-digit growth once the 2024-25 merchant fee relief has annualised. It is the earnings base and carries most of the profit. (2) Temu's losses narrow as it moves from cross-border parcels to local fulfilment, without a permanent loss of the EU and US markets. (3) The investment programmes (the RMB 100bn+ support programme from April 2025, about RMB 100bn of first-party brands) do not permanently lower the non-GAAP operating margin below about 24%. (4) The starting price is about 8x non-GAAP earnings, with surplus cash at about half the market value; any capital return is a free option.

What to track: online marketing growth; transaction services growth as a Temu signal; sales and marketing and total non-GAAP opex ratios; non-GAAP operating margin; effective tax rate and HNTE status; "other" income and investment income; cash, cash location and any capital-return language; regulatory actions (SAMR, EU DSA).

What would break it:
- Online marketing growth below 5% for two quarters after the relief annualises (Q4 2026 and Q1 2027).
- Full-year non-GAAP operating margin below 20%.
- A formal cap on platform fees, or Temu exiting a top-three market.
- First-party brand spending above about RMB 40bn a year, or an acquisition.
- An effective tax rate above 22% for a full year.

Missing: Paul's view, debate with probabilities, variant view, model.

## 1. Bottom line

**Thesis: Intact (against the provisional baseline).** The domestic advertising line re-accelerated to +12% from +3%. This is the first evidence that the merchant-relief drag is annualising, and it lifted the non-GAAP operating margin to 30.1%, the highest in at least eight quarters. Against that, transaction services fell 12% in the first EU-duty quarter, and almost all of the operating gain was absorbed by lower investment income and a higher tax rate (18.2% against 12.0%). (Claude's read)

**Fresh money at about US$86 (intraday, 2026-11-19): Wait.** The base case returns about 15% a year to end-2029, which only just meets the hurdle; the conservative case is about flat and the harsh case loses 38%. The evidence that would settle it comes at Q4/FY2026 results, about late March 2027:
- online marketing growth of at least 10% against Q4 2025's +5%;
- sales and marketing at or below 26% of revenue through the Double 11 and Black Friday quarter;
- in the FY2026 20-F, confirmation that the 15% HNTE tax rate continues after 2026.

On price alone, the conservative case clears 15% only at about US$53. (Claude's read)

### Cash, balance sheet and earnings quality
- **Cash flow.** Operating cash flow was RMB 43.0bn. Trailing operating cash flow is RMB 109.3bn, against trailing net income of RMB 93.8bn (computed). Investing outflow was RMB 37.8bn, mainly deposits and investments. Exchange-rate changes reduced cash by RMB 3.7bn. There were no buybacks or dividends, and financing cash flow was nil [1].
- **Balance sheet at 30 September.** Cash and short-term investments were RMB 455.4bn. Restricted cash was a further RMB 82.2bn, and RMB 115.7bn of other non-current assets are mainly time deposits. Against these sit RMB 112.0bn of payables to merchants and RMB 18.9bn of merchant deposits, leaving surplus cash of about RMB 440bn (computed). There is no debt.
- **Fixed assets and equity.** Property, equipment and software rose to RMB 6.4bn from RMB 1.3bn at the end of 2025. The company has not explained the increase; the Q2 call referred to an office building and service centres [3]. In the first nine months, the "other" loss is RMB 9.5bn against a RMB 3.3bn gain a year earlier, including RMB 7.4bn in Q2, and it is still unexplained. ## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**1. Temu's shift from cross-border to local (Europe now following the US).**
- *What changed.* Transaction services fell 12% (RMB -6.4bn), cost of revenue fell RMB 1.6bn, and sales and marketing fell RMB 5.0bn [1]. Third-party data shows Temu's share of EU online transactions falling from 16% to 8% after 1 July, with average order value up 37% [8]. The company has not linked these figures to Temu; the link is inference.
- *Puts.* Lower fulfilment and customer-acquisition cost per order. Paid acquisition stops on orders that no longer earn their keep. The shift fits the "local" model management describes [3].
- *Takes.* Lower revenue and slower growth in Temu's largest region (about 40% of 2025 volume, secondary [9]). Local sellers compete with Amazon on its terms. A second DSA fine of up to 6% of global turnover is possible after the preliminary findings of 17 November [7].
- *Net effect.* About neutral on Q3 operating profit (+0.2bn, computed). The risk is that Temu's terminal value is lower than a cross-border model at scale would have given it (e). No baseline figure changes yet.
- *Updates:* baseline leg 2. *Confidence:* medium on direction, low on size. *Confirms:* Q4 transaction services stabilising QoQ with sales and marketing held down. *Reverses:* sales and marketing rebounding without transaction-services growth.

**2. The domestic advertising line re-accelerating.**
- *What changed.* Online marketing grew +12% after +2% and +3% [1][2]. The 2025 relief programmes were booked partly as contra-revenue [10], so the quarter in which they annualise should show a jump. This one did.
- *Puts.* Margin: online marketing is now 55% of revenue and almost all incremental profit.
- *Takes.* Part of the jump is base effect (Q3 2025 was +8%). The Internet Platform Pricing Conduct Rules, in force since April 2026, require consultation before fee changes, which caps the take-rate lever [11]. Peers show no matching recovery: Alibaba's like-for-like customer management revenue grew +1% [12].
- *Net effect.* If growth holds at about 10% through 2027, the advertising line adds about RMB 23bn of revenue a year and about RMB 13bn of net income (e), roughly 13% of earnings.
- *Updates:* baseline leg 1. *Confidence:* low after one quarter. *Confirms:* Q4 at or above 10% against the +5% base. *Reverses:* Q4 below 6%.

**3. A higher tax rate.**
- *What changed.* The effective rate was 18.2% in Q3 and 19.5% for the nine months, against 12.0% and 15.1% a year earlier (computed). The FY2025 20-F shows an 8.0-point benefit from the 15% HNTE rate, available "until 2026", and a 1.5-point drag from valuation allowances on loss-making entities [13].
- *Puts.* None.
- *Takes.* Each point is about RMB 1.2bn of net income. Losing HNTE status would cost up to about 8 points, about RMB 10bn (e). The rising rate is consistent with loss-making overseas entities whose losses cannot be offset against Chinese profits.
- *Net effect.* Conservative and harsh cases carry 22% and 25%.
- *Updates:* baseline leg 4. *Settles it:* the FY2026 20-F tax note (about April 2027).

**Expected by now but not seen:**
- Any revenue, cost or balance-sheet disclosure for the first-party brand company, eight months after its creation.
- Any quantified "return" from the support programme beyond the online marketing line.

## 4. The cycle

PDD's domestic business is tied to everyday consumer spending and merchants' advertising budgets, not to a durable-goods or capital cycle. Two outside factors matter: Chinese consumption and the platform subsidy contest. A third, cross-border trade rules, is regulatory and structural rather than cyclical. **Peers** (same set each period: Alibaba, JD, Meituan; bellwether NBS retail sales; plus [[0700-HK]] for the advertising market).
- **Alibaba, September quarter (19 Nov).** Customer management revenue was -5% reported and +1% like for like. Management said consumption "remained under pressure" into December, with "consumers becoming more cautious" and staples outgrowing discretionary spending. Quick-commerce losses were "significantly" reduced, with a plan to halve them next year [12]. This agrees on demand. It disagrees on advertising: PDD's +12% is far ahead of Alibaba's +1%.
- **[[JD]], Q3 (17 Nov).** Retail revenue grew +2.9%, marketing expense fell 26%, and advertising grew double digits. The CFO committed to cut food-delivery investment in 2027 (vault). This agrees: the subsidy contest is easing, and advertising budgets are moving to platforms that can show returns.
- **Meituan, June quarter (vault).** Subsidies are "still much higher than 2024", with a few quarters to normalise. Q3 is due 27 November.
- **[[0700-HK]], Q3 (18 Nov).** The advertising market has been weak "for several months" (vault). This agrees on macro, and makes PDD's advertising acceleration more likely to be company-specific (relief annualising) than market-driven.

**Phase.**
- *China consumer spending:* last period not assessed; now **Downturn, slowing** for everyday goods. For: October retail sales +0.4%, online goods slowing, Alibaba's caution. Against: Double 11 +4.8% and staples holding. Durables are at Trough per the theme note.
- *Platform subsidy contest:* **Rolling over**, as in the theme note. JD and Alibaba are cutting, and PDD stayed out.
- *Cross-border trade rules (EU):* no cycle phase applies. This is a structural step-down, now one quarter in.

**Earnings against mid-cycle.** Trailing non-GAAP net income is about RMB 99.9bn, against RMB 122.3bn in FY2024. The gap is policy (support programmes, investment) and tax, not the consumer cycle. The trailing non-GAAP operating margin is 25%, against 30% in FY2024 (computed).

**What the price assumes.** At about US$86, the market pays about 8.5x trailing non-GAAP earnings and about 4.5x trailing after-tax operating profit once surplus cash is excluded (computed). That implies flat to falling earnings, a permanent investment phase, and cash that is never returned.

**Next period (Q4 2026, about late March 2027).** The read is confirmed if online marketing grows 10% or more, NBS online goods holds about 3% or better, and sales and marketing stays at or below 26% of revenue with transaction services flat QoQ. The phase read is wrong if online marketing falls back below 6% while Alibaba's like-for-like growth stays at about 1%. That would mean the Q3 jump was a one-quarter base effect.

**What the price implies.** Close to the conservative case: flat earnings and no re-rating. At the pre-print price, the base case cleared the hurdle comfortably. After the jump, it only just clears it. The conservative case reaches 15% at about RMB 357, or US$53 (543 / 1.15³ / 6.7045). Cash is not valued beyond what the P/E already implies in any case. Surplus cash could reach about RMB 740bn by 2029 (about RMB 500 per ADS, e). That is worth something only if PDD returns it, and it has not signalled that it will.

**Share-price move since the print.** The shares were up about 11% intraday on 19 November, after +6% premarket [15][5]. The market appears to have taken the 30% margin and the sales and marketing cut as evidence that the "investment phase" can coexist with rising operating profit. It looked past the revenue miss.

**Fresh-money view.** Someone meeting PDD today gets China's lowest-cost marketplace at about 8.5x earnings. More than half the market value is surplus cash, the business needs almost no capital, and its advertising engine has just re-accelerated. What has to be true:
- The re-acceleration is real rather than a one-quarter base effect.
- Temu's retreat in Europe costs growth but not profit.
- The tax rate does not jump.
- Management keeps some of the margin gain rather than spending it in the next programme.

The evidence on each is one quarter deep. The structure of the case is unchanged: the cash is not coming back, and the company has given no end date for investment. The base case only just meets the hurdle at this price, and the harsh case (-38%) has a real path through tax, Temu and first-party losses. No earlier verdict is on file to compare with.

**Call: Wait.** The business case stands and the price is low on any multiple. The base-case return does not leave room for error after the 11% move, and there is no variant view or debate on file. What would move it to Own: at Q4/FY2026 results (about late March 2027), online marketing growth of at least 10% and sales and marketing at or below 26% of revenue, followed by confirmation in the FY2026 20-F that the 15% HNTE rate continues after 2026. On price alone, the conservative case clears 15% at about US$53. What would move it to Don't own: online marketing growth back below 6%, a full-year tax rate above 22%, or a large new loss-making programme.

**Model.** None on file. **Build due** before any decision. It should keep the domestic advertising line separate from transaction services (Temu proxy), model tax by jurisdiction (PRC HNTE versus non-PRC losses), and split cash between surplus and merchant float, onshore and offshore.
