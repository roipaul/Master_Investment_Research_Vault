# PDD Holdings — understanding the layers

2026-11-22 · Claude, for Paul (living copy: https://claude.ai/artifact/Tq8U7we7ofV3ktYXvTg5xE)

## Why look now, and what has to be true

At about US$86 you pay roughly 4.5x after-tax operating profit for the business, once the RMB 440bn of surplus cash (52% of market value) is set aside. The catch: PDD has never returned cash and says it won't, so the case rests on the business and what management does with the cash, not on the cash itself.

**What the two famous buyers did (13F filings; no public explanation from either):**

- **Li Lu (Himalaya Capital).**
  - First bought in Q4 2020 near US$178 and sold it all by end-2021 at a large loss. He was out for three and a half years ([Xueqiu reconstruction](https://www.xueqiu.com/8970835897/408594291)).
  - Re-entered in Q2 2025. In Q2 2026 he raised the stake 134% to 10.76m ADS, paying for it by selling six holdings, including Bank of America, Occidental, Moody's and S&P Global ([HK01/Gelonghui](https://global.hk01.com/财经快讯/60380518/13f报告揭秘-段永平-李录齐大手笔加仓这中概股)).
  - In Q3 2026 he added 9% more, to 11.76m ADS worth US$917m. That is 24.2% of his US book and now his largest position ([Valuesider](https://valuesider.com/insights/2026-q3/li-lu-pdd-outsized-position)).
  - Estimated average cost of this round is about US$89 (Xueqiu estimate, not official).
- **Duan Yongping (H&H International).**
  - He has added for seven or more quarters. In Q3 2026 PDD rose from 9.99% to 12.69% of his book, his third-largest holding ([Sina/JRJ](https://cj.sina.com.cn/articles/view/1704103183/65928d0f0200bi4cc)).
  - Press accounts say he has said more than once that he does not fully understand the business model, and that he backs it on trust in Huang and his team (secondary).
  - Duan is Huang's long-time mentor, so his buying is not independent evidence.

**What Li Lu has said, without naming PDD.** At Columbia's Graham & Dodd lunch on 5 October 2026 he said many excellent companies trade at very low prices, "在中国，情况可能更加极端" (in China it may be more extreme).

He blamed property prices down about 40% and middle-class household wealth down 60–70%. He posed the question every China holding has to answer: "你究竟是国家要支持的目标，还是实现目标所要付出的代价？" Is the company the goal the state supports, or a cost of reaching it ([transcript](https://business.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/imce-uploads/36th%20Annual%20Graham%20%26%20Dodd%20Luncheon%20Transcript%2020261005%20CBS%20Chinese.pdf))? For PDD that question runs through Layer 5.

**Three things the case needs.** Following them is not a thesis: neither has published one, and both are concentrated, patient holders with longer horizons than three to five years.

1. **The domestic marketplace keeps earning about RMB 100bn a year after tax.** That needs the cost advantage to hold against Douyin, Taobao and the new pricing rules (Layers 1, 2 and 5).
2. **The reinvestment earns something.** Temu, the first-party brand company and merchant support are where the cash goes, so they decide whether the cash is worth par, a discount, or less (Layers 4 and 6).
3. **Governance and the state take no large slice.** Control sits with the operators, and regulators now treat PDD as a problem case (Layers 3 and 5).

The fall from US$119.58 to US$77.39 over the year to 17 November ([Benzinga](https://kr.benzinga.com/quote/PDD/charts)) says the market doubts all three. Points 2 and 3 matter most for a 15% hurdle.

## Layer 1 — The machine: how an order makes money

Out of every RMB 100 a shopper spends on Pinduoduo in China, PDD keeps roughly RMB 5.3 as revenue and RMB 2.3 as operating profit (e, FY2025). Almost all of it is paid by merchants, mostly as advertising, not by shoppers. The model is a toll on merchants who want to be seen, run with few people and almost no assets.

**The flow of one order.**

1. A shopper finds the item, mostly in a recommendation feed and partly through search, and pays through WeChat Pay, Alipay or PDD's own wallet.
2. PDD holds the money until delivery is confirmed, then pays the merchant, less a 0.6% technical service fee.
3. The merchant ships through a third-party express firm (ZTO, YTO, J&T and others). PDD owns no warehouses for its domestic marketplace.
4. The merchant got that order largely because it bid for traffic, so most of PDD's take is the merchant's ad bill.

&#91;embedded content: One Pinduoduo order, per RMB 100 of sales (FY2025, e)\]

The shopper's money passes through PDD to the merchant. PDD's revenue comes from what the merchant pays back to be shown.

**The take, per RMB 100 of domestic sales (FY2025, e; GMV and Temu figures are Visible Alpha consensus estimates).**

| Piece | Per RMB 100 | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advertising (online marketing, RMB 218bn) | about 4.2 | Merchants bid for placement in search and the feed |
| Transaction fee (payment processing, RMB 22bn) | about 0.4 | The 0.6% fee on each order |
| Duoduo Maicai (community grocery, RMB 34bn) | about 0.7 | Revenue from the grocery pick-up business |
| **Domestic revenue** | **about 5.3** | RMB 274bn on about RMB 5.1tn of GMV (marketplace 4.9tn plus grocery 0.26tn) |
| **Domestic operating profit** | **about 2.3** | Group non-GAAP operating profit RMB 102.6bn plus about RMB 15.8bn of estimated Temu losses |

**Three things that make the machine unusual.**

- **The money arrives before it leaves.** PDD owed merchants RMB 112bn at 30 September 2026, about eight days of domestic sales (e). That float is part of the cash pile, and it is not PDD's (Layer 6).
- **The advertising is auctions, increasingly run by PDD's own algorithm.** Since 2023–24 PDD has pushed merchants toward full-site promotion (全站推广): they set a target return on ad spend, and the system bids for them everywhere. PDD's revenue then depends on how hard merchants compete for the same buyers, not on raising a list price. That is why the take rate roughly doubled after 2019 even as headline fees stayed at 0.6% (TIP podcast, Library).
- **Very little capital or headcount.** About 25,500 staff in 2025: roughly RMB 17m of revenue and RMB 4.2m of non-GAAP net income per employee (e). Property and equipment was only RMB 1.3bn at end-2025. Logistics, payments and most cloud are bought in (Tencent alone was paid RMB 6.5bn in 2025).

**Where Temu sits.** Temu is booked mainly in transaction services. In its original fully managed model PDD buys from the supplier at a set price, so revenue is the consumer price less the supplier payment, and PDD pays for shipping (Layer 4). Consensus estimates Temu lost about RMB 16bn in 2025. Not disclosed; treat it as a range.

**What follows for an investor.** The domestic machine is a high-margin toll whose size depends on two things: how much merchants sell, and how much of their margin they will hand over to win traffic. The merchant scuttlebutt found a third of surveyed merchants losing money in 2025, so the second lever is closer to its limit than the first (Layer 2).

## Layer 2 — Why buyers and merchants come, and why copying it hurts the copier

PDD's edge is the lowest cost of matching a cheap product to a buyer, not lock-in. Buyers and merchants both use several platforms. What protects PDD is that rivals who copy its low-price model damage their own economics more than PDD's. That held in 2023–24; the threats now are Douyin's different route to the same buyers, and regulators removing PDD's price-forcing tools.

**Why it works.**

- **The supply is unbranded.** Factories and traders without brands sell straight to buyers. A broker's estimate put white-label and new brands at about 67% of PDD's GMV in late 2022 ([Woshipm, citing Hua'an Securities](https://www.woshipm.com/share/6097469.html)). No brand premium, no distributor margin.
- **The feed, not the keyword.** Taobao grew up selling search keywords, which favours sellers who can pay for them. PDD's recommendation feed lets a small seller reach buyers without "owning the keywords" (Business Breakdowns, Library).
- **Tuned for conversion.** On the TIP podcast's account, PDD optimises for the chance of a purchase: cheap, high-probability items, one item per checkout, no cart. Alibaba and JD optimise expected value per view.
- **Not just lower-tier cities.** PDD told Business Breakdowns its users' city mix "pretty much mirrors the population distribution", calling the lower-tier label "a common misperception". By 2020 its 788m buyers were nearly every online shopper in China.

**Counter-positioning: rivals who copied it paid more than PDD did.**

| When | Rival move | What happened |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2023 | Alibaba "back to users": low prices, red packets, 百亿补贴 | Taobao/Tmall revenue grew only 5% in the year to March 2024, and adjusted profit fell 11% in the March quarter ([Woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/share/6093923.html)) |
| Dec 2023 | Taobao adopts refund-only (仅退款), first used at PDD | Merchant complaints spread to Taobao; through 2024–26 platforms scaled refund-only back under regulatory pressure (PDD in August 2026) |
| 2024 | Douyin makes “price strength” (价格力) its top priority | Growth slowed from above 60% to below 30%; Douyin dropped it as the top goal in July 2024 ([Qianzhan/Xinshang](https://www.qianzhan.com/analyst/detail/329/240814-4c657f74.html)) |
| 2024 | JD 百亿补贴 and low-price push | JD moved its main fight to food delivery in 2025 |

The logic: Alibaba's profit comes from brands paying high ad prices on Tmall. Pushing traffic to the cheapest unbranded goods cuts that. Douyin's strength is live-stream and content, where hosts and brands need margin, so it cannot match white-label prices for long. When PDD's market value passed Alibaba's in November 2023, Jack Ma told staff to congratulate PDD, adding that only those willing to reform at any cost earn respect ([Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/11547458.html)).

**Where the edge is eroding (all evidence; Claude's read on weight).**

1. **Douyin reaches the same buyers by another route.** It sells to people who are browsing and not looking for anything. Goldman's Q4 2025 GMV shares: Taobao/Tmall 31%, Douyin 24%, PDD 19%, JD 16% (secondary). PDD's daily app users (404m in October 2026) now trail Taobao's (422m) (QuestMobile; Company Logistics).
2. **The user base is saturated.** Growth must come from more spending per user and a higher take. The scuttlebutt suggests merchants have little room left to pay more (Layer 1).
3. **The price-forcing tools are gone.** PDD dropped the lowest-price clause in March 2026 and eased refund-only in August, partly ahead of the April 2026 pricing rules. Lowest price now has to come from merchant competition and subsidies, not rules.
4. **Rivals have stopped copying.** By mid-2024 Taobao, Douyin and PDD had all moved back to GMV goals from pure low price ([Woshipm](https://www.woshipm.com/it/6099218.html)). Less copying means less damage to rivals, so counter-positioning protects PDD less in a truce than in a price war.

**A lesson from your own 2022 note.** The May 2022 valuation note expected growth bought with subsidies to stall and margins to settle near 0.7% of GMV. Instead the ad auction monetised the traffic: operating profit reached about 2.3% of domestic GMV (Layer 1). Today's bear case, that the RMB 100bn support programme buys growth that won't last, has the same shape. It deserves testing before it is believed.

## Layer 3 — The people and the culture

PDD is run by a small, engineering-led team on a founder's creed that puts customers first, tells staff to ignore the share price, and prizes focus over explanation. That creed built a 25,000-person company earning about RMB 100bn a year. The same creed explains the silence to shareholders and a record of hard-edged behaviour toward staff, merchants and regulators.

**The founder and his lineage.**

- Colin Huang (Huang Zheng) worked at Google from 2004 to 2007. He founded and sold Ouku, ran an agency (Leqi) helping brands sell on Tmall and JD, then started Pinduoduo in 2015 out of a games studio (Acquired, Library).
- His mentors were Duan Yongping (BBK, the group behind OPPO and vivo) and William Ding (NetEase). In 2006 he went to Buffett's charity lunch as Duan's guest (Acquired; TIP).
- He left the CEO role in 2020 and the board in 2021, gave up super-voting rights and entrusted his votes to the board. His trust still holds 24.8%, and the Partnership vehicle he directs holds another 6.5%.

**The creed, in his words.**

- A 2018 internal letter defined 本分, roughly "keeping to one's own job", in five points ([Sina](https://finance.sina.cn/chanjing/gsxw/2021-04-21/detail-ikmyaawc0871066.d.html)):
  1. Be honest and trustworthy.
  2. Do your own job, whatever others do.
  3. Shut out outside forces and stay true to the original aim.
  4. Don't take advantage of others, even when you can.
  5. When something goes wrong, blame yourself first.
- For management, the letter says, 本分 means creating value for consumers and to "忽略股价的波动": ignore share-price swings. That one line explains the lack of guidance, the sparse disclosure and the indifference to buybacks better than any regulatory story.
- The 2018 IPO letter described PDD as a cross between Costco and Disney: value plus fun ([Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/4304561.html)).
- The 2019 shareholder letter, as headlined at the time: PDD could turn a profit whenever it chose, but "拿钱存定期不是好主意": parking money in time deposits is not a good idea ([TMTPost](https://www.tmtpost.com/3904628.html)). Today about RMB 440bn of surplus cash sits mostly in deposits and bonds. Expect it to be spent on the business, not returned.

**Today's operators.**

- **Chen Lei**, an engineer, has been CEO since 2020 and co-chairman since December 2025. He holds about 0.5% of shares plus 54.3m unvested shares, together worth roughly US$1.8bn at US$86 (e).
- **Zhao Jiazhen**, who built Duoduo Maicai, has been co-CEO since 2023 and co-chairman since December 2025. He was personally fined RMB 6.94m in the April 2026 SAMR case.
- **Board and pay.** Six members, three independent. There is no CFO title; a financial director was appointed in December 2025. Cash pay for all directors and officers was US$2.2m in 2025; nearly all reward is equity at a nominal strike, vesting over four years followed by a three-year lock-up.
- **Alignment (Claude's read).** Pay rides on the share price over seven years, which is good. But the board also votes Huang's 24.8%, and his creed tells it to ignore the share price. No outside holder can force capital returns.

**Behaviour under pressure.**

| When | What happened | What it shows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Apr 2026 | SAMR found PDD refused materials, supplied false information and used "violence and soft resistance" against investigators (Layer 5) | Fighting the regulator, at a cost of the top fine rate |
| Mar 2026 | On the Q4 2025 call management admitted governance and talent had "lagged" the global business; more than 180 governance measures followed by Q3 | A response, after the damage |
| Aug 2024 | Chen Lei: buybacks and dividends not needed "in the foreseeable future"; the ADSs fell 28.5% that day | Indifference to the share price, in practice |
| Mar 2023 | Google suspended the Pinduoduo app; Kaspersky said some versions "contained malicious code" exploiting Android flaws ([TechRadar](https://techradar.com/news/the-pinduoduo-malware-executed-a-dangerous-zero-day-against-millions-of-android-devices), [Krebs](https://krebsonsecurity.com/2023/03/google-suspends-chinese-e-commerce-app-pinduoduo-over-malware/)). PDD rejected the accusation | Willingness to cross lines for growth |
| Mar 2023 | Merchants angry at platform rules mass-ordered and refunded PDD's new own-brand store, forcing it offline within hours ([Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/11547458.html)) | Merchant relations at their worst |
| Dec 2020 – Jan 2021 | A 23-year-old Duoduo Maicai employee died after collapsing on her way home at 1:30 am. A verified PDD account posted "你们看看底层的人民，哪一个不是用命换钱", then PDD blamed a vendor's employee. Shanghai labour inspectors investigated ([Beijing News](https://m.bjnews.com.cn/detail/160975967215827.html), [China Digital Times](https://chinadigitaltimes.net/chinese/661111.html)) | An intense culture, defensive when exposed |

**What this means for the case (Claude's read).** The culture is both the moat and the main risk. The focus and frugality that keep costs low are hard for rivals to copy (Layer 2). The same "shut out outside forces" instinct produced the malware episode, the employee-death response and the SAMR obstruction finding. In China in 2026 those are not just reputational: they shape how the state treats PDD (Layer 5). Watch whether the governance overhaul since 2025 shows up as fewer clashes, not just more announced measures.

## Layer 4 — Temu: the second engine and its siege

For valuation, Temu matters more as a loss to stop than as a profit to add. Consensus has it losing about RMB 16bn in 2025 and making about RMB 8bn by 2028, against roughly RMB 118bn from the domestic business (VA estimates). Its model has shifted from shipping parcels out of China to selling from local warehouses. That lowers revenue, raises margin per order, and pushes cost and risk onto sellers. Each step came under trade and safety rules.

**Why PDD built it.** The domestic user base was saturated by 2020. PDD's skill is matching cheap factory supply to demand at low cost, and China has spare factory capacity. Temu exports that engine. It launched in the US in September 2022 and was in more than 50 countries within two years.

**How the model has changed.**

|  | Fully managed (2022–) | Semi-managed and local (2024–) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who sets the price | Temu, by reviewing the supplier's bid (核价) | Temu still largely, through counter-offers |
| Where goods ship from | China, small parcels by air | Warehouses in the US or EU, held by sellers |
| Who pays logistics and holds stock | Temu pays shipping; suppliers send stock to Temu's China warehouses | The seller |
| How PDD books revenue | Consumer price less supplier payment: large revenue, thin margin | Commission and fees: smaller revenue, wider margin |
| What broke it | US de minimis ended May 2025; EU €3 duty from 1 July 2026 | Seller capital and returns logistics; slow listing |

This shift explains much of the Q3 2026 split: transaction services (Temu's main line) fell 12% while group operating profit rose 21%. Fewer, more profitable orders. The scuttlebutt adds that Temu began charging fully managed suppliers a reported 5–11% commission from May 2026 and raised promotion fees toward 8–10% (sellers' report).

**The size (all third-party, low confidence).** GMV about US$14bn in 2023. For 2024, estimates range from US$54–60bn (Tech Buzz China) to US$71bn (ECDB). For 2025, about US$75–93bn. Europe, about 40% of GMV, has overtaken the US, about 31%.

**The siege, by market.**

- **US.** The end of de minimis cut fully managed volume. By July 2025 sales were back to April levels through local stock (Tech Buzz, secondary). Several state attorneys general have sued over data and products. Texas settled in October 2026 for US$3m, US data storage, and bans on whole groups of linked stores within two working days of an unsafe-product finding.
- **EU.** Temu was fined €200m under the Digital Services Act in May 2026. A second preliminary finding in November 2026, on how it controls illegal products, could cost up to 6% of global turnover. The €3 duty from July 2026 halved Temu's share of EU online transactions, from 16% to 8% (ECDB, secondary).
- **Elsewhere.** Still suspended in Vietnam and Uzbekistan; investigations open in several EU states, Australia and Korea (FY2025 20-F).

**The breakeven date keeps slipping.** Morgan Stanley expected profit from 2026 (October 2023). UBS expected quarterly breakeven by end-2026 (September 2025). Consensus now has a small loss in 2027 (Library). One newsletter says US semi-managed reached breakeven while Europe runs about −1% to +1% (secondary).

**What it means for the case (Claude's read).** If Temu simply stops losing money, group profit rises about 15% with no help from China. That is the most plausible near-term upside, and the Q3 2026 margin is consistent with it. The risk is the reverse: the same instinct that built Temu sends the cash into the next overseas push, such as New Pinmu's own-brand exports, which sit outside any disclosed segment. Temu's value to a holder therefore depends on management stopping, and the record says it rarely stops.

## Layer 5 — The state: goal or cost?

Since 2025 Beijing's line on platforms has moved from "compete on price" to "stop involution, protect merchants, collect tax". PDD's model was the clearest example of what is being restrained, and its December 2025 clash with investigators made it the example. The state has not tried to break the model. It has removed PDD's sharpest tools and raised the cost of conflict. Expect containment, not dismantling, unless the relationship worsens again.

**Li Lu's question applied to PDD (Claude's read).**

- **The case for "goal":**
  - Cheap goods for households in a deflation.
  - A sales channel for about 16.8m merchants, many of them tiny.
  - Farm products sold direct, and free delivery to villages.
  - Through Temu, an export outlet for spare factory capacity.
- **The case for "cost":**
  - Merchants squeezed on price and refunds, the "involution" Beijing now attacks.
  - Unlicensed "ghost" stores selling food.
  - Small sellers outside the tax net.
  - Trade friction abroad.
- PDD is both. Which one the state sees depends partly on PDD's own conduct.

**What changed, and what it does to the model (dated log in Desk findings).**

| Rule or event | What it does | Effect on PDD |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Revised Anti-Unfair Competition Law, in force 15 Oct 2025 | Article 14 bars platforms from forcing merchants, openly or in disguise, to sell below cost under platform pricing rules ([Dehenglaw](https://www.dehenglaw.com/CN/tansuocontent/0008/034395/7.aspx?MID=0902)) | Outlaws using traffic, sign-up rights or ranking to push merchants below cost |
| Platform tax-reporting rules (State Council Decree 810), first reports from 1 Oct 2025 | Platforms report each merchant's identity and income to the tax bureau ([gov.cn](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202506/content_7029052.htm)); PDD started reporting in early October ([Xinhua](https://www.news.cn/world/20251016/2915ac29619c4b84a603b12cc326b577/c.html)) | Small merchants' tax costs rise; over 80% of surveyed merchants say costs went up. Less margin left to spend on ads |
| Internet platform pricing rules, in force 10 Apr 2026 | Bans forcing price cuts, lowest-price clauses and automatic price-following through traffic or fee penalties. Fee changes need cost justification and 7 days' public notice | No numeric cap on take rate, but every fee change is now public and challengeable |
| SAMR penalty, 17 Apr 2026 | RMB 1.52bn, the top per-store rate, and a finding of obstruction | A regulator that now treats PDD as a problem case |

**The December 2025 clash, from reports.** A SAMR team began an on-site inspection at PDD's Shanghai office on 3 December 2025.

- China Quality News, published by SAMR, reported that an employee slammed a door and broke an officer's finger. The next night a security head led a group that pushed officers to the ground ([HK01](https://global.hk01.com/即时中国/60342364/拼多多被查-有员工写字提示同事保持沉默被发现-将a4纸生吞入肚)).
- An employee caught passing a note reading "silence" swallowed it.
- Caixin reported several PDD staff detained by police, and government-relations staff dismissed (via [Zaobao/V2EX](https://www.v2ex.com/t/1179476)).
- Bloomberg reported that inspectors had asked for highly sensitive transaction data (via HK01).
- The penalty decision later recorded "暴力、软对抗" (violence and soft resistance).

**Where the remaining policy risk sits (Claude's read, in order of size).**

1. **Process caps on monetisation.** Ad yield can still rise through auctions, but any structural fee change is now visible to merchants, media and regulators. In a truce among platforms, Beijing will not tolerate PDD restarting a price war.
2. **Tax.** The 15% rate is worth about RMB 9.6bn a year, and renewal falls in 2027 (Desk findings). A refusal would be a quiet way to tax a disfavoured company.
3. **Further enforcement.** Food safety, product quality and data are open fronts, and the data demand in December 2025 shows how far inspectors will reach.
4. **Political standing.** Unlike Alibaba in 2020–21, PDD has no founder making public speeches. Its risk is behavioural, so it is partly in management's hands.

**What would show the relationship mending:** an HNTE renewal, positive state-media coverage of PDD's farm and village programmes, no new penalties through 2027, and PDD cited as a good example, as Xinhua did for its early tax reporting in October 2025.

## Layer 6 — The money: whose cash, where, and what you pay

Of about RMB 653bn of cash and deposits at 30 September 2026, roughly RMB 440bn is surplus after the money owed to merchants. Set that aside and the business costs about RMB 411bn, about 4.5x after-tax operating profit. Whether that is cheap depends on what the cash is worth, and PDD has given holders no reason to value it at par.

&#91;embedded content: Q3 2026 release balance sheet; trailing after-tax operating profit of RMB 91.3bn (e, about RMB 111bn pre-tax at 18%)\]

The money owed to merchants is added back because it sits inside the cash but belongs to them. Restricted cash (RMB 82.2bn) is left out on both sides.

**Whose cash it is.**

- **Not PDD's:**
  - RMB 82.2bn of restricted cash, buyer funds held for merchants.
  - RMB 112.0bn owed to merchants and RMB 18.9bn of merchant deposits. That is about eight days of domestic sales passing through.
- **PDD's, but at risk:** about RMB 66bn of listed equities sit inside short-term investments (end-2025), holdings undisclosed. Fair-value gains were RMB 10.4bn in 2025. In the first nine months of 2026 an unexplained "other" loss of RMB 9.5bn appeared, RMB 7.4bn of it in Q2.
- **PDD's, idle:** the rest, mainly time deposits, money-market funds and bonds.

**Where it sits.**

- At end-2025, 43% of cash and short-term investments was held in mainland China and about 57% (roughly US$40bn) outside it.
- The listed Cayman parent itself held RMB 46m, and no subsidiary has ever paid it a dividend. RMB 150.6bn of onshore profit is treated as permanently reinvested, so returning onshore cash would cost withholding tax (FY2025 20-F).
- Buybacks could be funded offshore without touching capital controls. The barrier is management's choice, not the law.

**What the cash does to reported earnings.**

- In 2025 interest received was about RMB 12.3bn and equity gains RMB 10.4bn. Together that is roughly a fifth of pre-tax profit, from money not used in the business (e).
- Interest income is falling with lower rates and RMB appreciation: interest and investment income was down 37% in Q3 2026. The cash props up net income, and that prop is shrinking.

**Why the cash has not come back, and probably won't.**

1. **Stated policy.** In August 2024 Chen Lei said buybacks and dividends were not needed "in the foreseeable future". The topic has not come up on a call since.
2. **Creed.** Huang's letters say ignore the share price, and that parking money in deposits is "not a good idea" (Layer 3).
3. **Plans.** RMB 100bn for first-party brands over three years, the RMB 100bn+ merchant programme, and supply-chain spending. Property and equipment rose from RMB 1.3bn to RMB 6.4bn in nine months of 2026.
4. **Politics (Claude's inference).** Sending billions offshore to foreign shareholders while asking merchants and regulators for goodwill would sit badly in Beijing in 2026.

**A US tax trap for a family holder.** The FY2025 20-F says it is "possible" PDD was a passive foreign investment company in 2025, and the risk rises the longer cash sits idle and the share price stays low. PFIC status brings punitive US tax treatment unless a holder makes an election (QEF or mark-to-market). Check with your tax adviser before buying in a US-taxable account.

**How the market prices it.** Morgan Stanley's November 2026 downgrade applied a 70% discount to net cash, citing "an absence of clarity around shareholder returns" (Investing.com via Yahoo, secondary). Macquarie's upgrade put net cash at 62% of market value (secondary).

Practical reading: if the cash is worth 30 fen on the yuan, the business costs about 8x after-tax operating profit (e), still cheap for a 40%-margin franchise if that margin holds.

## Layer 7 — What Li Lu may be seeing, and what the sceptics see

Neither Li Lu nor Duan Yongping has explained the position, so the bull case below is Claude's reconstruction from their record and Li Lu's public remarks. The two sides mostly agree on the facts. They differ on time horizon and on whether management's reinvestment creates value.

**How Li Lu got here.** He bought near US$178 in late 2020 and sold everything by end-2021, down about two-thirds. He came back in Q2 2025 after tariff fears took the stock from about US$135 to about US$90. He then roughly doubled the position in 2026 as it fell to the US$70s, selling six other holdings to pay for it. Per a Xueqiu account of Xu Xin's comments (secondary), one of his analysts spent nine months on nothing but PDD, and he phoned everyone he knew who held it ([Xueqiu](https://www.xueqiu.com/1536512895/416018865)).

**The bull case as he might frame it (reconstruction).**

1. **A wonderful business at a deep discount.** A domestic marketplace earning about 40% operating margins and about RMB 100bn a year after tax, with almost no capital needs, at roughly 4–5x earnings once cash is set aside.
2. **A trough in China, not a permanent state.** His Columbia remarks blame falling property prices and lost household wealth for low prices across Chinese assets. He argues the stock market must, over time, become a policy "goal" because equity grows with the economy.
3. **Reinvestment as optionality, not waste.** In three years Temu built a business of roughly US$75–95bn GMV (estimated). A patient owner may see the cash as fuel for the next such bet rather than dead money.
4. **Culture vouched for by people he trusts.** Duan's long relationship with Huang, and 本分 as an operating creed.
5. **Horizon.** Himalaya can wait a decade for the China discount to close and the cash to compound. Your hurdle is 15% a year over three to five.

**The sceptic's case.**

- **Cash is not shareholders' money.** No buyback ever, a stated no, and RMB 100bn plans to spend it. Morgan Stanley values it at 30 fen on the yuan; the TIP podcast's co-host passed because investors are "unlikely" to see it.
- **The domestic franchise has peaked.** Users are saturated, Douyin is gaining, merchants are stretched, and regulators removed the price tools. Revenue was flat in Q3 2026.
- **The reinvestment treadmill.** Core marketplace, then Duoduo Maicai, then Temu, now first-party brands and supply chain. Each phase has no end date, and Temu's breakeven has slipped a year every year.
- **State and governance risk.** Obstruction finding, DSA cases in Europe, US state suits, a tax break up for renewal, a VIE structure, payment rails owned by two executives, and the founder's votes in management's hands.
- **Opacity.** No GMV, users or Temu figures since 2020, so every model rests on outside estimates.
- **Consensus.** "Hold", with an average target of US$107 (MarketBeat, 9 November 2026, secondary).

**Where that leaves you (Claude's read).** The facts favour the bulls on business quality and price, and the sceptics on cash and governance. Li Lu's horizon lets him win even if the cash stays trapped for years. Yours does not.

Your Q3 earnings note found that at US$86 the base case (earnings +8% a year, 10x exit, no cash returned) only just clears 15% a year, and the conservative case loses money. At the pre-results US$77 the base case gave about 19.5%. Price discipline matters more for you than for him.

## Desk findings: tax renewal, Fufeitong, platform-rules timeline

Three desk checks you asked for. The tax break is a renewal risk in 2027, not a scheduled expiry. Fufeitong is a governance flag of small economic size. The rules timeline shows the state tightening on exactly the tools PDD relied on.

### 1. The 15% tax rate (HNTE)

- **The end date rolls forward in three-year steps.** Shanghai Xunmeng: "2018 to 2020" in the FY2020 20-F, "until 2023" in FY2022–23, "until 2026" in FY2024–25. Walnut Street (Shanghai) followed the same pattern. Each 20-F gives the current certificate's end date, and the status was renewed twice before.
- **What is at stake.** The rate saved RMB 9.6bn in 2025. At a 10-point rate gap, that implies about RMB 96bn of taxable profit in the two entities, roughly three-quarters of PDD's PRC pre-tax profit of RMB 125.7bn (e).
- **Who earns the profit.** The 20-F's entity table shows the VIE group (which includes Shanghai Xunmeng) with revenue falling from RMB 131.9bn (2023) to RMB 113.7bn (2025) and net income of RMB 20.7bn in 2025. Other subsidiaries, including Walnut Street and the overseas companies, earned RMB 77.0bn.
- **Renewal test.** Under the 2016 HNTE rules the applying company needs:
  - R&D of at least 3% of its own sales over three years (the bar above RMB 200m of sales);
  - technical staff of at least 10% of headcount;
  - high-tech products or services of at least 60% of revenue.
- **Can't verify.** Group R&D was 4.4% / 3.2% / 3.8% of revenue in 2023–25, but R&D by entity is not disclosed. That is the one fact that would settle the renewal odds.
- **Read:** treat it as a renewal risk weighted by the regulatory relationship, not a certain loss. The FY2026 20-F (around April 2027) will show a new end date (2029) or none.

### 2. Fufeitong, PDD's payment licence

- **What it is.** One of the first licensed payment firms (May 2011). Its licences cover stored-value accounts (nationwide Class I, Shanghai Class II) and nationwide payment processing. It runs the "多多钱包" wallet inside the Pinduoduo app. In 2026 its licence was renewed as long-term ([CNFin](https://www.cnfin.com/hb-lb/detail/20261109/4493038_1.html)).
- **Who owns it.** Chen Lei and Zheng Zhenwei, through Ningbo Hexin, funded by RMB 910.6m of interest-free loans from PDD. The vehicle took control in January 2020, went to 80.52% in March 2025 and to 93.89% later in 2025. PDD can call the loans and buy the vehicle, subject to approval. The 20-F's stated reason is a backstop in case banks or third-party payment firms cut PDD off.
- **How big it is.** 2025 revenue RMB 1.06bn and net profit RMB 0.3m; a net loss of RMB 27m in H1 2026. A 0.7% stake offered by China Post Capital in November 2026 implies a value of about RMB 976m. PDD supplied over 70% of its revenue (RMB 811m of services in 2025) ([CNFin](https://www.cnfin.com/hb-lb/detail/20261109/4493038_1.html)).
- **Recent changes.** The People's Bank approved new deputy general manager and technical heads in September 2026, after another set of changes in January ([Beijing Business Today](https://www.bbtnews.com.cn/2026/0913/605799.shtml)).
- **Read:** economically small. RMB 5.47bn receivable at end-2025 is mostly settlement float. The point is governance: critical payment rails sit with two executives rather than the listed company. If the loans are called and the vehicle bought at cost, the issue disappears. Ask why that has not been done.

## Correction 2026-11-22 — surplus cash includes the restricted cash held for merchants
