# PDD — Thesis Debate (2026-11-22)

Claude's debate of the deciding questions. Verdicts are proposed, not Paul's view. Word and PDF: [[files/PDD_Holdings_PDD_Thesis_Debate.docx]], [[files/PDD_Holdings_PDD_Thesis_Debate.pdf]].


# Thesis and Verdict

**Business verdict: Inconclusive.** Both thesis-critical questions sit near 58% and rest on Low-confidence evidence. Worse, they point in opposite directions. The domestic marketplace looks more likely to grow profit at 5–7% a year than at the 8–10% the thesis needs (Q1, bear lean). The heavy spending that began in 2024 looks slightly more likely than not to have peaked in 2026, as Temu's losses narrow (Q2, bull lean). Neither leans far enough on good enough evidence to call the business case supported or broken.

**Price check: Stretch.** At US$86.05 the shares trade at about 8x 2026E non-GAAP earnings. Reaching 15% a year by end-2029 needs about RMB 877 per ADS. On the path the verdicts support (domestic profit +6% a year, Temu losses nearly closed), 2029 EPS is about RMB 89. That clears the hurdle only if the multiple rises to about 10x. At today's 8x it returns about 7% a year.

**The call depends on:**

1. **Q1.** Can merchants keep paying more for traffic? Advertising revenue grew 12% in Q3 2026 after 2–4% in the first half [2]. Merchant economics look stretched [12][15].
2. **Q2.** Does the investment phase end? Temu losses are narrowing on the evidence of Q3 margins [2][13]. But management still says the company is in a "strategic investment phase" with no end date. It is also committing about RMB 100bn to first-party brands [3][5].
3. **Q4.** Will the cash ever count? If it stays idle or is spent on low-return projects, a re-rating to 10x needs earnings growth alone to persuade the market.

**The thesis as it now stands.** PDD's domestic marketplace is a high-margin toll on merchants who pay to be seen. Its user base is saturated and its price-forcing tools have been regulated away, so profit growth now depends on how much more of their margin merchants will hand over. That looks closer to mid-single digits than high-single. The stock is priced for flat earnings and for cash that never comes back. Two things could deliver the hurdle without help from China's consumer. If Temu stops losing money, group operating profit rises about 9% on its own: consensus has about RMB 11bn of Temu losses in 2026E, closing to near zero [8]. If management then signals any return of capital, the multiple has room to rise. Without one of those, the case is a cheap stock that stays cheap.

# Business Description

**What PDD sells.** PDD runs two marketplaces:

- **Pinduoduo**, in China. It earned about RMB 274bn of revenue in 2025 on an estimated RMB 5.1tn of GMV: about 4.2% from advertising, 0.4% from a 0.6% transaction fee, and 0.7% from Duoduo Maicai grocery (e) [1][8][14].
- **Temu**, abroad. It is booked mainly in transaction services. Consensus estimates it lost about RMB 16bn in 2025 [8].

Group revenue was RMB 431.8bn in 2025 and non-GAAP operating profit RMB 102.6bn. Adding back estimated Temu losses gives domestic operating profit of about RMB 118bn, roughly 43% of domestic revenue and 2.3% of domestic GMV (e) [1][8].

**What drives it across cycles.**

- **Buyers.** Pinduoduo had 788.4m annual buyers in 2020, about every online shopper in China, and has disclosed none since [7]. Growth now comes from spending per buyer and from the share of each sale PDD takes, not from new buyers.
- **Market growth.** China's online sales of physical goods grew 5.2% in 2025 to RMB 13.1tn [24]. In a downturn shoppers trade down, which helped PDD in 2021–24. Merchants also lose margin, which limits how much more they can pay for ads (Q1).
- **The advertising auction.** Merchants bid for placement. PDD's automated full-site promotion (全站推广) bids for them against a target return. Revenue therefore rises when more merchants compete for the same buyer, without any change to list prices [10][14].

**Industry structure.**

- **Four platforms take most domestic online GMV.** Goldman's Q4 2025 shares: Taobao/Tmall 31%, Douyin 24%, PDD 19%, JD 16% [30].
- **Douyin is the fastest grower.** It grew more than 30% in 2025 to about RMB 4.3tn [29][30], then slowed to roughly 20% in the first half of 2026 (36Kr; Douyin disputes the figure) [28].
- **Merchants are fragmented** (16.8m active on PDD in 2025 [1]) and sell on several platforms: 70% of surveyed merchants use Taobao, 51% Pinduoduo, 51% Douyin [15].
- **PDD depends on a few suppliers of its own.**
    - Tencent, for payments, advertising and cloud: RMB 6.5bn in 2025.
    - Third-party express firms.
    - Fufeitong, a payment company controlled by two PDD executives [1][52].
- **Regulators now set the rules of competition.**
    - The revised Anti-Unfair Competition Law (in force 15 October 2025) bars platforms from forcing merchants to sell below cost [50].
    - The internet platform pricing rules (in force 10 April 2026) ban forced price cuts and require public notice of fee changes [51].

**The business model in one line.** An asset-light toll: about 25,500 staff earn RMB 4.2m of non-GAAP net income each (e) [10]. Payables to merchants (RMB 112bn) fund part of the RMB 455bn cash pile [2]. Property and equipment were RMB 1.3bn at end-2025 before the 2026 build-up [1][2].

**Temu.** Temu exports the same engine: unbranded factory supply, prices set by the platform, a recommendation feed. The fully managed model (Temu sets prices and ships parcels from China) worked until the US ended duty-free entry for small parcels (May 2025) and the EU added a €3 duty (July 2026). Temu is now moving to local warehouses run by sellers. That shrinks revenue per order but raises the margin on it [13][14].

# Management

**Who runs it.**

- **Founder.** Colin Huang left the CEO role in 2020 and the board in 2021. He gave up super-voting rights and entrusted his votes to the board, while his trust kept 24.8% [1][9].
- **Operators.** Chen Lei (CEO since 2020) and Zhao Jiazhen (co-CEO since 2023) became co-chairmen in December 2025 [6].
- **Board.** Six members, three independent. There is no CFO title; a financial director was appointed in December 2025 [1].

**Incentives.**

- **Pay is almost all equity.** Cash pay to all directors and officers was US$2.2m in 2025. Awards over about 7.2% of shares are outstanding at a nominal strike, vesting over four years and then locked up for three [1][10].
- **Chen Lei** holds about 0.5% of shares plus 54.3m unvested shares, together worth about US$1.8bn at US$86 (e) [10].
- **What that aligns and what it doesn't.** It ties management to the share price over seven years. But no outside holder can force capital decisions: the board votes Huang's stake, and changing the Partnership's rights or the board's rules needs 95% of votes cast [1].

**Behaviour in past crises.**

- **The 2021 crackdown.** An employee's death and a defensive response; labour inspectors investigated [60]. The founder stepped back, and PDD pledged money to agriculture.
- **2023.** Google suspended the Pinduoduo app after malware was found in some versions [59].
- **The 2024 margin squeeze.** In August 2024 management said buybacks were not needed "in the foreseeable future", and the shares fell 28.5% that day [58][61]. It responded to merchant anger with fee cuts worth about RMB 10bn a year [9].
- **2025–26.** It obstructed a SAMR inspection in December 2025. The April 2026 penalty decision recorded "violence and soft resistance" [47][48]. Since then it has announced more than 180 governance measures and a co-chair structure [3][6].
- **The pattern.** Operating decisions in a crisis are quick and often effective: fee cuts, the Temu pivot to local warehouses. Dealings with outside parties (staff, regulators, shareholders) are combative or silent until the cost becomes clear.

## Q4. Will cash be returned, or reinvested in ways that create value for outside holders, before 2029?

**Why it matters.** Surplus cash is about RMB 440bn, 52% of market value [11]. The price check assumes none is returned, so this question shapes the size of the return rather than whether the business compounds. If cash is returned, or reinvested at good returns, the gap between price and value closes faster. If it goes into projects that never earn their keep, the gap may never close.

**Bull case.**

- **The cash is reachable.** About 57% of liquid cash was offshore at end-2025, so buybacks need no approval to move money out of China [1][10].
- **Pressure is building.**
    - The FY2025 20-F warns PDD may be a passive foreign investment company, which hurts US holders and argues for using cash [1].
    - Morgan Stanley's November 2026 downgrade centred on the lack of capital return [57].
    - Large value holders arrived through 2025–26: Himalaya made PDD its largest US position, H&H its third largest [56].
- **Chinese peers changed course.** Alibaba pays a regular dividend, about US$2.5bn for fiscal 2026, even while spending heavily on AI and quick commerce [62].
- **Reinvestment can work.** On the bull view, PDD's record of reinvesting is good: domestic advertising scaled to RMB 218bn, and Temu reached about US$75–95bn of GMV (estimated) in three years [8][13]. If New Pinmu makes branded goods for export, the cash funds a third leg rather than sitting idle.

**Bear case.**

- **No has been said, and the creed backs it.** Chen Lei said buybacks and dividends were not needed "in the foreseeable future" (August 2024) [58]. The topic has not been raised on any S&P transcript since [3][4][5][6].
- **Huang's letters point the same way.** They tell management to ignore the share price, and say parking money in time deposits "is not a good idea" [53][54].
- **The money is spoken for.**
    - RMB 100bn for first-party brands over three years [5].
    - The RMB 100bn+ merchant programme, now extended into research, production and delivery [20].
    - Property and equipment up fivefold in nine months of 2026 [2].
- **Governance blocks pressure.** Outside holders cannot force a change. The two executives' ownership of Fufeitong shows a habit of keeping strategic assets close [52].
- **Politics (inference).** Returning tens of billions to foreign holders while asking Beijing and merchants for goodwill is unattractive in 2026.

**Weighing the evidence.**

- **Verified:** the stated position, the spending plans and the governance structure. The bull case rests on pressure and analogy, not on any statement by PDD.
- **The Alibaba analogy is weaker than it looks.** Alibaba had activist holders, a founder who had left management, and a share register that rewarded buybacks. PDD has a founder whose votes sit with management and a written creed against managing to the share price.
- **The reinvestment record is mixed.** Domestic ads were a great return on investment. Temu's return is not yet proven. New Pinmu's is unknown, and the rollout "has taken longer than we originally expected" [4].

**Verdict: Bear more likely — ~65%; confidence: Medium.**

**What would change the verdict.**

- Any buyback authorisation, or capital-return language on a call: on calls, or at the AGM (December).
- New Pinmu disclosed as a segment with its own margin: FY2026 20-F, about April 2027.
- A change in the board or in Huang's arrangement with it: 6-K filings.

**Implication for the thesis.** Treat the cash as worth well below par. The return has to come from earnings and the multiple. A buyback would be pure upside.

## Q4a. Is conduct risk (staff, regulators, overseas) a material threat to value?

Scored in Q3 below. The management record adds one point: every serious episode since 2021 came from the same instinct, to shut out outside forces, that the creed endorses [53]. Expect conduct to improve only as fast as the governance measures show up in fewer clashes. Measures announced are not the same thing.

# Current Situation: What Creates the Opportunity

**The shares fell by about a third while the business stayed highly profitable.** They fell from US$119.58 to US$77.39 in the year to 17 November 2026 [14]. They jumped about 11% to around US$86 on the Q3 results [11].

**What the market is debating:**

- **Revenue went flat.** Q3 revenue of RMB 108.3bn was unchanged from a year earlier [2]:
    - Online marketing grew 12%, after 2.5% in Q1 and 3.5% in Q2 [2][9].
    - Transaction services fell 12% as Temu moved away from cross-border shipping under the EU duty [2][39].
- **Margins jumped.** The non-GAAP operating margin reached 30.1%, up 5 points, because sales and marketing fell 17% to RMB 25.3bn [2][19].
- **There is still no end date.** Management repeated that PDD is in a "strategic investment phase" and that competition remains intense, with no platform pulling back [3][20][21].
- **The state turned harder.** SAMR's obstruction finding in April 2026, the pricing rules, the EU's second preliminary finding (17 November 2026) and the 15% tax rate up for renewal in 2027 all weigh on sentiment [44][47][51].
- **There is no capital return.** Morgan Stanley moved to Equal-weight on 9 November, valuing net cash at a 70% discount (secondary) [57].

**What has changed.**

1. **The three domestic rivals have stopped fighting on price.**
    - Alibaba's take-rate gains have slowed: like-for-like customer management revenue grew 7–8% in its fiscal 2026 [22][24].
    - Alibaba and JD are pouring money into quick commerce, and JD cut marketing in Q3 [18].
    - Douyin is turning to monetisation over growth [31].
    - PDD's 12% ad growth in Q3 came while Alibaba's reported merchant fees fell 5% [18].
2. **Temu is being rebuilt around local warehouses, and seller fees are rising** (Q2).
3. **Two well-known value investors are buying.** Li Lu made PDD Himalaya's largest US position, and Duan Yongping kept adding [56]. In October Li Lu said many excellent companies trade at very low prices, and that in China it may be more extreme [55].

**The opportunity, in one line.** The price assumes the investment phase never ends and the cash never counts [11]. If the phase is peaking (Q2) while domestic profit grows even mid-single digits (Q1), earnings re-accelerate within a three-year window.

# Return Drivers: The Key Debates

## Revenue growth and margin

### Q1. Can domestic profit grow 8–10% a year over the next three to five years?

**Why it matters.** The domestic marketplace earns about 90% of group operating profit before Temu losses [8][14]. The base case that clears 15% a year needs earnings per ADS to grow about 8% a year to 2029 at a 10x exit, and the domestic business must supply most of that [11]. At 5% domestic growth the hurdle needs a large re-rating or a capital return.

**What would have to be true.**

- *Bull:* advertising revenue keeps growing at 8% or more (GMV growth of about 5% plus a slowly rising take), while domestic costs grow slower than revenue.
- *Bear:* the take has reached what merchants can bear, rules block new fees, and the market grows at about 5%.
- *What separates them:* advertising as a share of GMV over the next four quarters; merchants' ad spend as a share of sales; domestic cost trends.

**Bull case.**

- **Q3 showed the engine still works.** Online marketing rose 12% to RMB 59.8bn [2]. That happened while Alibaba's reported merchant fees fell 5% and JD cut marketing [18]. PDD appears to be taking a larger share of merchants' ad budgets as rivals pull back from price competition and spend on quick commerce.
- **The drag from fee cuts is ending.** The analyst at Dolphin Research saw PDD's ad monetisation rate still slightly down in Q2 2026, but with the decline narrowing as the 2024–25 support programmes lap [12]. Growth in 2027 starts from a cleaner base.
- **Profit can grow faster than revenue.** Group sales and marketing fell 17% in Q3 [19]. Part of that is domestic consumer incentives, which the RMB 100bn programme front-loaded in 2025. On the bull view the domestic margin rises as programmes mature: management says the programme is entering a stage where investment "is beginning to yield tangible results" [4]. A year ago Morgan Stanley already projected PDD's marketing spend falling to 2.3% of GMV by 2026 [27].
- **Room to raise the take still exists.** Advertising was 4.46% of estimated domestic GMV in 2025, against 4.55% in 2024 [10]. The auction can lift the take without any posted price rise, as merchants compete for a fixed pool of buyer attention [14].
- **Merchants have nowhere better to go.** In the survey, PDD is used by half of merchants. One producer said PDD's margin is about 3 points lower than rivals' but it is still the biggest profit pool because of volume [12].

**Bear case.**

- **The market grows about 5%.** Online sales of physical goods grew 5.2% in 2025 [24]. PDD's domestic GMV ran about 1 point ahead of the market in Q3 2025, by Goldman's estimate [27]. Its user base is saturated.
- **The take has little room left.**
    - A self-selected survey of 1,056 merchants (all platforms) found more than 63% spending over 10% of sales on paid traffic, 17.8% over 30%, and 30.1% making a loss in 2025 [15].
    - Over 80% said the tax-reporting rules from October 2025 raised their costs [15][49].
    - Merchants losing money cannot keep raising bids.
- **The rules now limit the levers.**
    - Since 10 April 2026 platforms must justify fee changes by cost and give seven days' public notice [51].
    - Since October 2025, forcing merchants to sell below cost is illegal [50].
    - PDD dropped its lowest-price clause in March 2026 and its one-click refund rulings in August [16][17]. It can no longer force low prices, so it must buy them with subsidies.
- **The best analogue grew slower.** Alibaba's like-for-like customer management revenue grew 7% in its fiscal 2026, driven by take rate, and only 1% as reported in the June 2026 quarter [22][23]. That is a mature, saturated platform that also pushed full-site promotion and a 0.6% software fee.
- **Consensus already expects less than 8%.** Visible Alpha consensus has online marketing growing 6.6% in 2026E and 2027E, and 7.3% in 2028E [8]. For the nine months of 2026 it grew 6.1% (e) [2].
- **Q3 may be a base effect.** The +12% compares with Q3 2025, when online marketing grew only about 8% after the fee cuts [9]. Q1 and Q2 2026 grew 2.5% and 3.5%.

**Weighing the evidence.**

- **What is verified:** the quarterly growth figures, the rules, and Alibaba's like-for-like growth. Merchant distress comes from one self-selected survey across all platforms, so it is directionally useful but not a measure. No merchant posting first-hand from 2025–26 was reached [12].
- **What is not disclosed:** domestic profit itself. The 8–10% test rests on an estimate that strips out consensus Temu losses.
- **The analogue points one way.** Every large Chinese marketplace that saturated (Taobao from about 2021) settled into mid-single-digit revenue growth, with take-rate gains of 1–3 points a year.
- **Where the bull case can still win: costs, not revenue.** If domestic incentives and marketing fall as the support programme matures, profit can grow 8% on revenue growing 6%. Q3 is consistent with that, but the split between domestic and Temu costs is not disclosed.
- **Balance:** the bear case has more independent evidence (market growth, consensus, the analogue, the rules). The bull case has the latest quarter. Confidence is Low because neither domestic profit nor domestic costs are disclosed.

**Verdict: Bear more likely — ~58%; confidence: Low.**

**What would change the verdict.**

- Online marketing growth of 8% or more in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027, without the merchant support programme being cut back (release and call, March and May 2027).
- Merchant calls showing stable or falling ad cost per order alongside growing PDD budgets (scuttlebutt interviews).
- A broad merchant survey (Paidai's 2026 edition, January 2027) showing lower loss rates.

**Implication for the thesis.** The revenue-growth leg probably runs at mid-single digits domestically. The margin leg has to come from costs and from Temu. On its own, the domestic business looks like a bond with modest growth, not a compounder.

### Q2. Does the investment phase peak in 2026?

**Why it matters.** Since 2024 PDD has spent heavily on three fronts:

- merchant fee cuts and the RMB 100bn+ support programme;
- Temu's losses;
- New Pinmu, its first-party brand company.

Consensus has group non-GAAP net income rising from RMB 104.6bn (2026E) to RMB 139.0bn (2028E). More than half of that rise is Temu swinging from a loss of RMB 10.9bn to a profit of RMB 7.6bn [8][10]. If spending peaks in 2026, earnings re-accelerate within your window even with modest domestic growth. If it does not, earnings stay flat.

**What would have to be true.**

- *Bull:* Temu's local model earns a margin, seller fees rise, overseas marketing falls, New Pinmu stays within its RMB 100bn over three years, and the support programme shifts from subsidies to cheaper upstream help.
- *Bear:* each programme is followed by another, Temu's EU business needs fresh investment after the duty, and New Pinmu grows into a large, low-margin first-party retailer.
- *What separates them:* group operating margin in 2027 against 2026; transaction-services profit (not disclosed); property and equipment spending; how New Pinmu is booked.

**Bull case.**

- **The margin turned.** The Q3 2026 non-GAAP operating margin was 30.1%, 5 points higher than a year earlier, with sales and marketing down 17% [2][19].
- **Temu is cutting costs.** It cut US advertising sharply in January–May 2026 (X −95%, YouTube and TikTok −74%) while US monthly users rose 21% (Sensor Tower) [41].
- **Temu is taking more from sellers.** It reportedly ended zero commission for fully managed sellers in May 2026, charging 5–11% on the price after shipping, with sellers covering negative settlements [37][38]. Promotion fees rose from about 6% to 8–10% [40].
- **Moving to local warehouses shifts cost and stock risk to sellers** and raises margin per order [13].
- **Consensus expects Temu to turn.** Losses go from RMB 15.8bn in 2025 to RMB 0.6bn in 2027E [8]. A year ago Morgan Stanley already saw the turn coming in 2026–27 [27].
- **There is a precedent.** Alibaba's international arm narrowed its losses to near breakeven in mid-2025 once management chose efficiency over growth [25]. Its loss narrowed 96% in fiscal 2026 [22]. Cross-border marketplaces can break even when owners decide they must.
- **New Pinmu is bounded.** The plan is RMB 15bn initially and RMB 100bn over three years [5]. Its terms make factories take the deposit and settle on actual sales, which limits PDD's stock risk [42][43].

**Bear case.**

- **Management says otherwise.** On the Q3 2026 call it repeated that PDD "remains in the strategic investment phase" and said expense ratios fluctuate with the pace of rollouts [3][20].
- **The support programme is being widened, not wound down.** It now extends into research, production and fulfilment [20].
- **New Pinmu adds a heavy, uncertain build.**
    - It means outright purchase, platform-set prices and branded export lines [42].
    - Property and equipment rose from RMB 1.3bn to RMB 6.4bn in nine months [2].
    - Its rollout "has taken longer than we originally expected" [4].
- **The breakeven date keeps slipping.** Temu was expected to be profitable from 2026 (Morgan Stanley, October 2023), then to break even quarterly by end-2026 (UBS, September 2025). Consensus now has a small loss in 2027 [10].
- **Europe needs rebuilding.** The €3 duty cut Temu's share of EU online transactions from 16% to 8% [39]. A DSA decision could fine it up to 6% of global turnover [44]. Rebuilding Europe around local warehouses takes money.
- **The base rate for Chinese platforms is repeated reinvestment.** Alibaba itself re-entered a heavy investment phase in 2025–26: adjusted EBITA fell 78% in the September 2025 quarter as it spent on quick commerce [26]. PDD's own record runs core marketplace, then Duoduo Maicai, then Temu, then New Pinmu [10].

**Weighing the evidence.**

- **Verified:** the Q3 margin step-up, the S&M cut, Temu's ad cuts, and New Pinmu's size and terms.
- **Reported but unconfirmed:** Temu's seller fee increases, from trade press [37][38].
- **Assertion:** management's "investment phase" language. It has used the same language since 2024 while margins moved both ways, so it predicts little about direction.
- **The deciding fact is not disclosed:** Temu's loss.
- **Our reading of the two strands:** spending on Temu is probably peaking (higher fees, less ad spend, local model). Spending on New Pinmu and the supply chain is rising, but from a smaller base.
- **Balance:** the group margin is more likely higher in 2028 than in 2026, but not by much, and the record of new programmes argues against confidence.

**Verdict: Bull more likely — ~58%; confidence: Low.**

**What would change the verdict.**

- **Group non-GAAP operating margin** at 27% or more for FY2026 and holding in 1H 2027: Q4 release (March 2027) and Q1–Q2 2027.
- **Transaction-services revenue stabilising with no margin drop:** the first sign that Temu's local model is scaling (quarterly releases).
- **How New Pinmu is booked:** as gross merchandise revenue in the FY2026 20-F, and whether property and equipment keeps climbing.

**Implication for the thesis.** This is the most likely source of earnings growth in your window. It is also the one management controls and could reverse at the next opportunity.

## Industry structure and the state

### Q3. Will the state take a material slice through tax, fines or fee limits?

**Why it matters.** A "material slice" here means more than about 10% of earnings:

- **Tax.** The 15% tax rate saved RMB 9.6bn in 2025, about 10% of net income [1].
- **Fines.** SAMR's April 2026 penalty was RMB 1.52bn [47]. A DSA fine could reach 6% of global turnover [44].
- **Fee rules.** The pricing rules limit how fast fees can rise [51].

Together these decide whether the domestic toll keeps its margin.

**Bull case.**

- **Renewal has been routine.** The 15% rate has been renewed twice. The end date rolled forward from 2020 to 2023 to 2026 in successive 20-Fs, as each three-year certificate was renewed [1][7].
- **PDD clears the R&D test at group level.** Group R&D was 4.4%, 3.2% and 3.8% of revenue in 2023–25, above the rules' 3% bar [1][14].
- **The penalty was small and is resolved.** RMB 1.52bn is under 2% of annual net income, and PDD accepted it [47].
- **PDD complied ahead of the rules.** It dropped the lowest-price clause before they took effect [16]. It was cited by Xinhua as an early filer under the new tax-reporting regime [49][63].
- **The state needs PDD.** It brings cheap goods to deflation-hit households, a channel for millions of small merchants, and farm sales (inference) [14].
- **Fines stay small next to earnings.** The EU has so far fined Temu €200m [45]. A US state settled for US$3m [46].

**Bear case.**

- **The regulator now treats PDD as a problem case.** SAMR found PDD obstructed its inspection, using "violence and soft resistance", and fined it at the top per-store rate [47][48]. Co-CEO Zhao was fined personally.
- **The tax renewal has a weak point.** It depends on R&D at the two subsidiaries holding the status, which is not disclosed. A refusal would be a quiet way to tax a disfavoured company (inference). It would cost about RMB 9.6bn a year [1].
- **The pricing rules make fee rises public and contestable** [51].
- **Two EU cases are open.** One more DSA case is live (Article 35) [44].
- **US exposure is spreading.** Several more US states have sued [9].
- **Enforcement can reach anywhere.** Inspectors in December 2025 asked for highly sensitive transaction data [48].

**Weighing the evidence.**

- **Verified:** the facts on both sides.
- **The tax renewal:** routine for qualifying firms, and PDD has qualified twice. The new factor is the relationship.
- **The pricing rules:** they slow the take rate rather than cap it. They work against Q1 more than they cut margins directly.
- **Fines:** so far small next to earnings.
- **The biggest single risk** is a tax refusal, which is about a 10% hit, not a collapse.

**Verdict: Bull more likely (no slice above about 10% of earnings) — ~60%; confidence: Medium.**

**What would change the verdict.**

- Tax rate renewed or not: FY2026 20-F (April 2027).
- Any new SAMR or NDRC case naming PDD: SAMR website, state media.
- The EU's decision on Article 35, and the fine: European Commission press releases, 2027.

**Implication for the thesis.** This mainly affects the margin leg through the tax rate. Model the conservative case at a 22–25% tax rate from 2027.

### Q5. Will Douyin stall Pinduoduo's domestic growth?

**Why it matters.** Douyin is the only rival still taking share fast. If it pulls lower-tier buyers and merchants' ad budgets from PDD, domestic GMV growth falls below market and Q1 fails outright.

**Bull case.**

- **Douyin is slowing.** It grew about 20% in the first half of 2026 against more than 30% in 2025 (36Kr; Douyin disputes the figure) [28][29].
- **It is turning to monetisation.** It merged its ad platform into e-commerce to raise the take per order [31].
- **Its model is different.** Content and live-stream selling needs margin for hosts and brands. That limits how far it can match white-label prices. Its 2024 attempt to lead on price slowed its growth and was dropped within months [36]. Taobao's own 2023–24 low-price push left its revenue growing only about 5% [35].
- **The search share is small.** Former staff say purchases from searching Douyin's mall were only about 10% of its GMV by Q3 2025 [28].
- **PDD's ad revenue is still growing.** It grew 12% in Q3 2026 while Douyin grew [2][18].
- **Different shopping occasions.** Shoppers use Douyin for browsing and discovery, and PDD for planned purchases of cheap daily goods [10].

**Bear case.**

- **Douyin is now close in size.** About RMB 4.3tn of GMV in 2025 against PDD's estimated RMB 5.1–5.5tn [30].
- **It has the users.** Douyin passed 1bn monthly users in March 2026, up 14% [31]. On 11 November 2026 it had 713m app users against Pinduoduo's 439m [34].
- **Its shelf business is growing.** Citi's Double 11 checks put Douyin's shelf (search and mall) GMV above 60% of its total, growing faster than live streaming [32].
- **Merchants' ad budgets overlap.** Half of surveyed merchants sell on both [15].
- **PDD's own user counts have stalled.** Its daily app users (404m) now trail Taobao's (422m) [33].

**Weighing the evidence.**

- **Verified:** the user counts. The GMV and growth figures are from press estimates.
- **Douyin's maths.** Growth of 20% on RMB 4.3tn adds about RMB 0.9tn a year. That is more than the whole market's growth (about 5% of RMB 13tn, or RMB 0.65tn). So Douyin is taking share from someone.
- **Who is losing share.** PDD's estimated domestic GMV grew about 7.5% in Q3 2026, ahead of the market [10]. So far Douyin's share gains come more from Taobao and JD than from PDD.
- **The analogue.** Taobao lost share to PDD for years without its revenue collapsing. Share loss in a saturated market erodes growth; it does not cause a cliff.

**Verdict: Bull more likely (no stall) — ~60%; confidence: Medium.**

**What would change the verdict.**

- PDD's domestic GMV growth falling below the market's for two quarters (sell-side estimates, NBS online retail data).
- QuestMobile showing Pinduoduo daily users falling year on year while Douyin's mall users grow (monthly).
- Merchant calls reporting budgets moving from PDD to Douyin (scuttlebutt).

**Implication for the thesis.** Douyin caps the upside on Q1 more than it threatens the base. It is part of why domestic growth looks like 5–7% rather than 8–10%.

# Risks

The bear cases that remain plausible, ranked by impact on value:

1. **Domestic growth settles at about 5% (Q1, 58% likely).** Earnings stall unless Temu turns. At 8x, the return is roughly the earnings yield, with no compounding.
2. **The investment phase never ends (Q2, 42% likely).** New Pinmu, upstream supply-chain support and Europe absorb what Temu stops losing. The margin stays at 24–26% and cash keeps piling up unused.
3. **Cash stays idle or goes into low-return projects (Q4, 65% likely).** The shares stay cheap because the cash never counts.
4. **The state takes a slice (Q3, 40% likely).** The tax rate goes to 25% (about 10% of earnings), or a new enforcement case follows the April 2026 obstruction finding.
5. **Douyin takes buyers (Q5, 40% likely).** PDD's domestic GMV growth falls below the market's, and Q1 fails outright.

**Risks outside the debated questions:**

- **US-China rupture:** for example, delisting of US-listed Chinese companies, or sanctions affecting Temu's US business.
- **The structure itself:** PDD controls its main Chinese operating company through contracts rather than ownership (a VIE) [1].
- **Concentration in one foreign holder base:** the ADR holders who might press for change are mostly passive.
- **Key-person risk:** decision-making rests with two co-CEOs.
