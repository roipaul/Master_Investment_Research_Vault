**Zone.**
- Neither the conservative case (0–4%) nor either base path without a re-rating (8–13%) earns 15%.
- The hurdle is reached only if the discount narrows: a higher multiple or cash reaching shareholders.
- That makes it a **Discount** idea. If capital is never returned and the multiple holds, it is Pass on price.

**Value-trap check.** Not triggered: the conservative return is below 10%. The cash test decides the idea, so it is recorded anyway:
- **Cash reaching shareholders: Fail.**
  - No dividend or buyback since the 2018 IPO.
  - Retained cash went into an RMB 66bn listed-equity book (RMB 11.5bn added in 2025) and two RMB 100bn programmes.
  - About 57% of liquid funds sit offshore, so a return is feasible.
- **Earnings quality: Pass.** Free cash flow at or above net income; capex light; share-based pay about RMB 6bn a year.
- **Why it is cheap:** the market does not believe the cash or the profit reaches holders. The cash test confirms that is how things stand today.

### Macro overlay (requested)

**China: weak demand, policy tilting to consumption.**
- Retail sales grew 0.4% year on year in October 2026 and 1.0% for January–October. Online goods sales grew 2.9% year to date (NBS). By our derivation, online goods were about −3.6% in October.
- ING calls 2026 one of the weakest years for domestic demand in recent times (via TrendForce).
- CPI was +0.7% in October: mild, not deflationary.
- Policy makes consumption the top priority of the 15th Five-Year Plan, with a household-income plan and continued trade-in subsidies. The subsidies favour appliances and electronics, which helps JD and Tmall more than PDD.
- On 2 Nov Beijing widened its swap facility for stock buyers.

**What China's macro means for PDD.**
- **Positive:** trade-down keeps traffic and share flowing to the cheapest platform. In Q3 PDD's ad revenue grew 12% while Alibaba's merchant fees fell 5% (TIKR).
- **Negative:** merchant margins are thin and nominal prices flat, so the take rate has a ceiling. A successful consumption push that lifts upper-tier spending would favour Taobao and JD.
- **Net:** macro weakness hurts PDD's GMV less than its peers' but caps its pricing power. It supports V2's share argument and limits V2's size.

**Global: higher rates, a firm yuan, a trade truce.**
- **Fed:** raised rates in September 2026 to 3.75–4.00% and held in October. Markets price more hikes, and the 10-year Treasury yield is about 5%.
- **What higher rates mean:**
  - (+) More interest income on PDD's offshore cash.
  - (−) A higher required return for long-duration emerging-market equities, which keeps the China-ADR discount wide.
- **Yuan:** near a four-year high at about 6.66–6.71 per US dollar, with the central bank leaning against faster gains. For a USD investor, a firm yuan supports translated earnings and cash. A reversal is the main FX risk; model returns are at a constant 6.67.
- **US–China:** the September 2026 Washington summit cut tariffs on US$30bn of goods each way, including small appliances, toys and decorations. Those are Temu categories, so this helps at the margin. The Busan truce was extended to January. US de minimis remains closed and the EU duty stays.
- **The AI trade:** China internet has been a funding source for the AI-hardware trade (Citi). A rotation back, or an AI-trade correction, could narrow sector discounts regardless of PDD-specific news.

**Macro conclusion: no separate macro variant view.** Weak Chinese consumption, high US rates and geopolitics are risks Paul is paid for and accepts (Section 4). Macro enters V2 as the mechanism: trade-down share gains. The return's sensitivity to macro runs mainly through the required return, so a sector re-rating is an upside path that needs no PDD-specific news.

### What the thesis rests on

**Covered share:**
- 5 years of conservative operating earnings (5 × RMB 88bn = RMB 440bn) ÷ market value = **53%** with cash at 0%.
- **73%** with cash at 30%.

**How the return arrives:**
- With no change in the multiple, the business pays about 8–13% a year: 6–11% growth at 30% cash credit.
- The rest, up to 15% and beyond, needs the discount to narrow.
- **The event:** a capital-return announcement or payout policy. Next chance is the Q4 2026 call (~late March 2027); the evidence deadline is the FY2027 results (~March 2028). Segment disclosure or an end to new programmes would also help.
- A sector-wide re-rating is a second, non-company route.

**Label: Discount (main), Duration (secondary).** The low price is a markdown for cash that may not reach holders, governance and country risk, while the earnings path is only moderately disputed. The covered share sits between half and two-thirds, hence the second label.

### V1 (candidate) — Capital is returned by the FY2027 results

**Statement.**
- **Variable:** the probability that PDD announces a buyback, a dividend or a payout policy by the FY2027 results (~March 2028).
- **Gap:** candidate ~35–40%, against ~20% implied at the central path (Price slider).
- **Reason:**
  - Large China internet peers have adopted buybacks and hold authorisations (Citi list).
  - PDD's surplus cash heads toward ~RMB 900bn by 2029, about 57% of it offshore.
  - Temu's need for funding shrinks as it approaches breakeven.
- **Resolution:** an announcement, or a payout policy in a filing, by ~March 2028.

**Why the market is wrong.**
- **Mechanism: Extrapolation.** The 2024 refusal and seven calls without capital-return language are priced as permanent. Secondary mechanism: Constrained sellers, with growth and EM funds exiting and China internet used as a funding source.
- **Evidence the mechanism is at work:**
  - −28.5% on the August 2024 no-buyback day;
  - the cash-credit math (~20–30%);
  - Baillie Gifford's 56% cut;
  - Citi's funding-source observation.
- **What ends it:** any payout announcement. The return needs this to happen to clear 15%.
- **The case that the market is right:**
  - Huang's reinvest-first philosophy (本分) and a "strategic investment phase" with no end date;
  - two RMB 100bn programmes, plus New Pinmu framed as "another Pinduoduo";
  - RMB 11.5bn put into listed equities in 2025;
  - no activist lever: Huang ~25%, Tencent ~14%, the Partnership.

  Observables that would show it: a new programme, a larger equity book in the FY2026 20-F, or "investment phase" extended.

### V2 (candidate) — Operating earnings grow ~5–7% a year through share gains in a weak economy

**Statement.**
- **Variable:** after-tax operating earnings, 2027–29.
- **Gap:** candidate ~6% a year against ~0–2% implied. This sits below consensus (~10–11%), so it carries the **cuts-ahead flag**.
- **Reason:**
  - PDD keeps taking share as China trades down: Q3 ad revenue +12% against Alibaba's merchant fees −5%.
  - Duoduo Maicai keeps gaining density.
  - Temu's loss per order is narrowing.
  - These add growth even with a flat domestic take rate.
- **Resolution:** FY2027 non-GAAP operating profit up ≥5% on FY2026, *and* Q3 2027 online marketing growth ≥ GMV growth once the refund step laps (~Nov 2027 to Mar 2028).

**Why the market is wrong.**
- **Mechanism: Extrapolation.** Six quarters of a falling take rate and the 2024–25 fee relief are priced as permanent. Secondary mechanism: Uncertainty, since there is no segment or GMV disclosure.
- **Evidence the mechanism is at work:**
  - −10.4% on the Q1 2026 miss;
  - −39% over 12 months;
  - a multiple near its five-year low (6.8x before Q3);
  - a high required return implied (~19% if consensus is right).
- **What ends it:** two or more years of reported operating-profit growth after the lap. A re-rating helps but is not required: the business path alone pays about 8.5% a year.
- **The case that the market is right:**
  - The Q3 step is mostly fewer refunds: one-off, lapping in Q3 2027.
  - The 15% tax-deduction cap limits merchants' ad intensity.
  - ~30% of surveyed merchants lost money in 2025.
  - Domestic demand is the weakest in years.
  - Temu faces the EU and US.

  Observable: online marketing growth at or below GMV growth from Q3 2027, or a falling take rate.
