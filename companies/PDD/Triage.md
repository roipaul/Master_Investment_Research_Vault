# PDD Holdings (PDD) - Triage

**Verdict: Dig (proposed; awaiting Paul's confirmation)**

No pre-primer gate was run: the primer was written first, at Paul's request. The pipeline has room. No name sits at debate, evidence, views or writeup against `max_active: 3`. The 25 names at `frame` are screen output and are not counted as deep research.

## 1. Business quality: Pass

Three facts decide it.

- **Returns.** Returns on capital are effectively unbounded. Merchants fund the working capital: RMB 131bn of merchant payables and deposits at 30 September 2026. Capex is 0.3% of revenue. Free cash flow was 108% of net income in FY2024 and FY2025. The operating margin has stayed between 22% and 30% through 2023-26 despite the Temu build-out and RMB 100bn+ of merchant relief (Primer 1.1, 4.1-4.2).
- **The reason for those returns.** PDD has the industry's lowest cost to serve: about 25,000 staff, no logistics assets, revenue booked as agent. Its supply base of factories and farms selling direct has been hard to copy; Alibaba and JD tried price-led campaigns in 2023-24 and retreated (Primer 1.1, 3.2).
- **Runway.** The runway is real but moderate. The domestic advertising line (RMB 218bn) is growing again (+12% in Q3 2026). Temu is shrinking in the EU after the July 2026 duty, and its overseas entities lost RMB 6.1bn pre-tax in FY2025 (Primer 2.3, earnings/FY2026-Q3).

The quality caveat, for the debate rather than a knockout, is governance and regulatory standing:
- an altercation with regulators in December 2025;
- an RMB 1.5bn SAMR fine in 2026;
- an EU DSA fine of €200m, with a second finding pending;
- no capital return and the thinnest disclosure in the peer group.

## 2. Edge

**Edge hypothesis (candidate, Claude's framing): framing and duration.** At about 8.5x trailing earnings, and about 4.5x after-tax operating profit once surplus cash is excluded, the market treats the 2024-26 merchant-relief and investment programmes as a permanent margin reset and the cash as permanently trapped. Two pieces of evidence point the other way:
- the advertising line is re-accelerating as the relief annualises (+12% in Q3 against +2-3% in H1 2026);
- the Q3 non-GAAP operating margin was 30%, back to FY2024 levels.

Coverage is heavy, with 35 analysts (TIKR; MarketScreener/Reuters, Nov 2026), so the bar is higher. The specific assumption to dispute is that 2026 non-GAAP earnings of about RMB 100bn are a ceiling for several years.

**Who is on the other side:**
- holders who fear US-China ADR risk and avoid PDD on governance;
- funds that need capital return (Morgan Stanley downgraded to Equal Weight on 9 November, citing structurally low industry growth);
- holders who own it for Temu growth and are leaving as Temu shrinks in Europe.

No forced seller is named. **Type:** framing (investment read as a permanent reset), with a duration element (how long the investment phase lasts). `/variant-view` can pick this up.

## 5. Plan (Dig)

**High-materiality questions for the debate.** The primer is descriptive and has no Section 5, so these are drafted here.

1. **Advertising runway.** Is the H1 2026 slowdown to 2-3% a relief-driven pause, or share loss to Douyin plus a regulatory cap on fees? What is a sustainable growth rate for online marketing in 2027-29? *Material:* the advertising line is the earnings base.
2. **Investment phase.** Is the investment phase time-bound? What steady-state non-GAAP operating margin results once the RMB 100bn+ support programme ends (about April 2028) and the first-party brand business is running? *Material:* each 5 points of margin is about RMB 22bn of operating profit.
3. **Temu.** Can Temu earn an acceptable return in a localised model, and what is it worth? Or is it a permanent drain (overseas pre-tax loss of RMB 6.1bn in FY2025)?
4. **Tax and regulation.** How large is the downside from HNTE expiry (up to about 8 points of tax rate), the SAMR and DSA actions, and the unexplained RMB 9.5bn of "other" losses in 2026?
5. **Cash.** How much of the RMB 440bn surplus is accessible? RMB 126bn of net assets were restricted at the end of 2025, and 43% of liquid assets are onshore. Is there any path to capital return? This decides whether the cash is part of the value or not.
