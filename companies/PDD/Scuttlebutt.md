# PDD — Scuttlebutt (2026-11-22)

Two groups were mined in parallel: Pinduoduo merchants (A) and Temu sellers (B). Evidence files: [[files/scuttlebutt/evidence_merchants_A.csv]] (65 rows) and [[files/scuttlebutt/evidence_temu_sellers_B.csv]] (73 items). The project copies are `pdd/PDD_Holdings_PDD_Merchants_Voice.md` and `pdd/PDD_Holdings_PDD_TemuSellers_Voice.md`. This is Claude's read of public evidence, not Paul's view.

**Main limit.** Neither run reached first-hand seller posts from 2025–26 in volume. Merchants: none. Temu: 2 of 73. Most of the evidence is trade press and consultants. Reddit and the main Chinese seller forums (知乎, 贴吧, 小红书, 知无不言, 雨果网) could not be reached with these tools. The interviews are the way to close that gap.

**Across both groups (Claude's read).** At home, PDD has eased price enforcement: it dropped the lowest-price clause in March 2026 and ended one-click refund-only rulings in August. Abroad, Temu is taking more from sellers: a reported 5–11% commission from May 2026 and promotion fees of 8–10%. In both markets PDD appears to be moving from "lowest price at any cost" toward taking more per order. That fits the Q3 2026 profit beat. It also bears on Q1 (how much more merchants can pay) and Q3 (whether Temu's losses narrow at the cost of seller depth).

## PDD Holdings (PDD) — what Pinduoduo merchants say (scuttlebutt, 22 Nov 2026)

Public posts and press, mapped to the debate questions. This is evidence, not Paul's view. The evidence file has 65 rows (A01–A65) from 39 distinct sources. (e) = Claude's inference.

### 1. Scope and bias
- **What was read:**
  - Press and trade media: 虎嗅, 36氪, 亿邦动力, DoNews, 新京报, 新华网, 21世纪, 搜狐/东方财富 accounts.
  - Merchant-training creators on 抖音 (topic pages), 人人都是产品经理, 数英.
  - 黑猫投诉 (merchant complaints), 雪球 (call transcript).
- **Blocked or never reached:** 知乎 (metadata only); 贴吧, 小红书, B站, 快手, 电商派 and 卖家之家 never appeared in search; three pages errored. Nothing was worked around.
- **How the sample skews:**
  - **No first-hand merchant post from 2025–26 was reached.** First-hand rows are 4 黑猫投诉 complaints from 2022–24.
  - The rest: press 44 rows (19 quoting merchants), consultants and training creators 16 (most sell courses or agency services), official 1.
  - The positive merchant stories are mostly PR-style features. The negative numbers come mostly from one self-selected survey of 1,056 merchants across all platforms.
  - Apparel is over-represented.
  - Rows cluster in 2026H1 (33), so before/after comparisons are weak.
- **The silent majority:** the survey over-weights distressed merchants who choose to answer; consultants over-weight ad complexity they are paid to solve. Typical merchants are probably less negative than the survey and less sophisticated than the trainers. (e)

### 2. By debate question

#### Q1 — Advertising runway (take rate vs volume)
- **No Pinduoduo-only measure of cost per order or ROI for 2024–26 was found.** The question cannot be settled from public posts.
- **Merchant economics are stretched across platforms.** In Paidai's survey (fieldwork 20–30 Jan 2026, 1,056 self-selected merchants, 51% of whom sell on Pinduoduo):
  - over 63% put more than 10% of sales into paid traffic, and 17.8% more than 30%;
  - 30.1% had a negative net margin in 2025, and another 30%+ earned 0–5%;
  - over 80% say the e-commerce tax regime fully in force from October 2025 raised their costs, 60% of them by more than 6% of revenue (A04, A64).
- **Auto-bidding:** 2 of 5 consultant rows say 全站推广 auto-bidding lowers ROI or makes it unstable (A03, A06). One analyst estimates Pinduoduo's ad monetisation rate was still slightly down year on year in Q2 2026, with the decline narrowing as the fee-cut drag laps (A07).
- **Selling on several platforms is normal; there is no exodus.**
  - Survey: Taobao/Tmall 70%, Pinduoduo 51%, Douyin 51% (A51).
  - An insider says about 100k Pinduoduo merchants added Taobao's 淘工厂 for margin (A47).
  - One producer says Pinduoduo's margin is about 3 points lower but it is still its biggest profit pool because of volume (A48).
  - The only "budget moving to Douyin" row is unsourced commentary (A52). Nothing on 微信小店 or Kuaishou.
- **Implication (e):** the Q3 2026 ad recovery looks more like the fee-cut drag lapping plus higher bids than merchants getting richer. With a third of surveyed merchants losing money and new tax costs, room to pay more per order in 2027–29 looks limited. That leans **bear on the take-rate runway**. Low confidence, given the sample.

#### Q2 — Investment phase
- **Fee relief is visible and has not been reversed.** 3 rows show merchants receiving refunds and cuts, all PR-style (A12–A14); none show a cut reversed. On the Q2 2026 call management described the RMB 100bn programme as entering an "efficiency-release" stage, with no end date (A46).
- **新拼姆 (first-party brands) is so far an overseas-led programme for larger factories.** 10 rows from 5 sources:
  - Terms (A57, A61): an outright buy at a price the platform sets, with no sales to third parties; mainly apparel. Bulk orders of up to about 10k units per SKU with a 20–30% deposit, and entry bars of 5+ years in business and general-VAT status.
  - Five factory recruitment events were held in September 2026 (A61).
  - Settlement is on actual sales and slower than Temu's fully managed model, and a clause lets the platform deduct or freeze funds, which worries suppliers (A58).
  - Rollout is slower than planned (A62). Two factories welcome it (A54, A59); a trader without a factory fears being cut out (A60).
  - No domestic Pinduoduo merchant voice on channel conflict was found.
- **Implication (e):** support is levelling off rather than being withdrawn (**neutral**). 新拼姆 looks like a Temu supply-chain play, not yet a threat to domestic merchants.

#### Q4 — Regulation (pricing rules from 10 April 2026)
- **Pinduoduo unwound the targeted practices step by step, mostly before the rules took effect:**
  - Nov 2024: merchants can leave automatic price-following (自动跟价) at any time (A24).
  - Oct 2025: service metrics for 百亿补贴 loosened (A26).
  - 13 Mar 2026: the 百亿补贴 requirement changed from "商品价格不超过参与平台其他活动的同款价格" to "具备较强的价格竞争力" (A31; checked by Claude against DoNews, 12 Mar 2026). Violators can still be demoted or delisted (A32).
  - 1 Aug 2026: post-delivery refund-only requests no longer get a one-click compensation ruling; merchants get 36 hours to settle with the buyer (A37, A38; checked).
  - Nov 2026: the 申诉优赔 appeal scheme compensates merchants the same day (A39).
- **Counts:** after 10 April, 4 rows show easing and 1 shows pressure. No post after April mentions 自动跟价. The last forced-pricing complaints are consultant rows from 2025H2 (A25, A27).
- **Friction has moved to compliance.** Penalty and compliance rows: 2 in 2024, 5 in 2026 (A15–A17, A19–A21). One example: un-issued invoices trigger a 30% restriction on withdrawals (A21).
- **Regulatory read-across:** a three-agency meeting on 30 Apr 2026 ordered unnamed platforms to fix problems. State media said Taobao was removing forced price-cut rules and making promotions opt-in, and Meituan was splitting fees; Pinduoduo was not named (A34, A35). Precedent: 多多福利社 closed in 2023 after merchant objections.
- **Implication (e):** much of the domestic regulatory risk to the merchant relationship may already be absorbed. That leans **bull on regulatory risk**. But the same change removes the lever Pinduoduo used to force low prices, so the low-price edge now has to come from ads and subsidies, not rules. That leans **bear on the moat**. Watch for any SAMR or NDRC action naming Pinduoduo on pricing.

### 3. Other constituencies
- Temu sellers (see the Temu sellers section below) show the mirror image. Temu is adding a 5–11% commission and higher promotion fees for sellers, while Pinduoduo eases price rules at home. **In both markets PDD appears to be moving from enforcing the lowest price toward taking more per order.** That fits the Q3 2026 profit beat. ## PDD Holdings (PDD) — what Temu sellers say (scuttlebutt, 22 Nov 2026)

Public posts and press, mapped to the debate questions. This is evidence, not Paul's view. The evidence file has 73 items (E01–E73). (e) = Claude's inference.

### 1. Scope and bias
- **What was read:** Chinese trade press (AMZ123 columns; 亿邦动力 and WeChat accounts republished on 虎嗅; 界面), Tech Buzz China's "Temu Watch", and English trade press and vendor blogs (Modern Retail, Digital Commerce 360, Lengow, EcomCrew, Value Added Resource, The Register).
- **Blocked or never reached:**
  - Reddit: the search tool rejected the domain, and no Reddit links surfaced.
  - 知无不言, 雨果网, 小红书, 知乎, B站, 抖音 and YouTube never surfaced.
  - Errors on several Chinese portals; RetailDetail paywalled; Trustpilot had consumer reviews only.
  - Nothing was worked around.
- **How the sample skews:**
  - Only 2 of 73 items are sellers writing first-hand.
  - 47 are press, about 20 of them distress stories retold by trade media, which tilts bear.
  - 13 of 17 creator items come from one paid newsletter based on expert interviews.
  - 6 items are from vendors selling the fix they describe; 1 is sponsored by Temu (tilts bull).
  - By seller base: China 46, EU/UK 6, US 6, platform-level 15. The US evidence is mostly from 2025.
- **Tally (bull / bear / neutral for PDD):** 16 / 35 / 22 overall. Q3: 11 / 15 / 14. Q4: 5 / 20 / 8.

### 2. By debate question

#### Q3 — Temu's returns in a localised model
- **Temu is taking more from sellers.** 7 items:
  - From May 2026 fully managed sellers reportedly pay a 5–11% commission on price after shipping (category rates given by one source: home goods about 5%, apparel about 8%, electronics about 11%). A "negative commission" rule makes a seller whose settlement is below shipping cost bear the loss plus 5% of goods value (E06, E46).
    - Checked by Claude against both sources: AMZ123 (11 Sep 2026) says Temu **had not confirmed** the change; the Huxiu repost (19 Aug 2026) says it was announced. Treat as reported.
  - Promotion fees rose from about 6% in 2025 to 8–10% in Q4 2025, and deposits from RMB 10k to 30k (E28).
  - A logistics mark-up for semi-managed sellers and ad bidding moved to store level (E34, E39, E50, E55).
- **Seller margins are thin.** Fully managed margins are about 3–5% after 3–6 months on sale, and semi-managed margins fell from above 15% to 8–12%. 5 items, mostly one newsletter (E29 and others).
- **Churn.** 5 items on exits or churn. One vendor survey says only 35% of fully managed sellers are active (E47). In the US only about a third of planned local sellers were recruited in 2025 (E31).
- **The local-warehouse model moves capital and inventory risk onto sellers and favours big ones.** 5 bear items:
  - Storage costs and break-even thresholds (E09); cash cycles longer than a month (E17); listing lag (E16); unclear EU returns (E18).
  - Per-item logistics about 20 times the fully managed cost (E42).
  - Small sellers being dropped (E07, E44). SHEIN offers free warehouses to win the same sellers (E64).
- **The US shows the local model can restore volume.** Sales were back to April 2025 levels by July 2025 (E36), and US monthly users rose 21% while US ad spend was cut in January–May 2026 (E56). A newsletter claims US semi-managed reached break-even (E35; date unreliable).
- **Implication (e):** Temu is improving its own unit economics at sellers' expense (commission, fees, mark-ups) and moving stock risk to sellers. That is **bull for Temu's losses narrowing in 2027**, as consensus assumes. It is **bear for the depth and loyalty of its seller base**, which now skews to larger factories that also sell on SHEIN, AliExpress and Amazon. The fully managed model, Temu's original edge, is winding down; one seller calls it "全托管本就是过渡模式" (fully managed was always a transitional model).

#### Q4 — EU and US rules
- **The EU's €3 duty from 1 July 2026 broke direct shipping of cheap goods.** 10 bear items in 2026H2:
  - Temu's sales in France were down about 50% month on month in July (Joko data, E02).
  - Temu's share of EU top-100 online transactions fell from 16% to 8%, while UK share held. Average order value rose 37% (ECDB, E03).
  - Direct-ship volume was estimated down 60–67% (E10). One seller went from over 100 orders a day to under 10 (E22).
  - Margins on items under €10–20 were wiped out (E11, E23).
- **Goods already held in EU warehouses gained.** 3 bull items: pre-stocked items up about 14% in daily sales per a Temu buyer (E14); one German warehouse went from about 2,000 to 6,000 orders a day (E08, E15). Listing is slow: about 60% of one seller's units were listed a month after shipping (E16).
- **Enforcement is shifting to bans on the trader and demands for paperwork.**
  - The Texas judgment (Oct 2026) requires delisting within 2 working days and banning the merchant plus all linked stores (E57).
  - The EU's 17 Nov Article 35 finding targets action against single stores, and test reports that are rarely required and often unchecked (E62). A warehouse vendor expects stricter test-report checks (E45).
  - No evidence either way on whether seller fines rose or eased in 2026.
- **Regulatory read-across (e):** the Texas settlement is the likely template for an EU remedy: bans at trader level and required testing in high-risk categories. That would raise per-product compliance costs and speed consolidation toward large sellers.
- **Implication (e):** the EU is now a local-warehouse business with a smaller seller base, higher order values and lower volumes. That bears on Q3 as much as Q4.

### 3. Other constituencies
- Pinduoduo merchants (see the merchants section above) show PDD **easing** price enforcement at home after the April 2026 rules, while Temu **raises** what it takes from sellers abroad. In both, PDD appears to be moving from "lowest price at any cost" toward taking more per order. (e)

### 5. Questions for a 20-minute seller call (deciding question first)
1. On your best-selling Temu product, what is your all-in margin per unit now, against a year ago and against Amazon?
2. How have your EU orders split since 1 July between direct shipping, Temu's EU warehouses, your own overseas warehouse and Y2? Did the €2 handling fee from 1 Nov change anything?
3. What does Temu take from you in total now (commission, promotion, logistics mark-up, ads), and what changed in May 2026?
4. How many days from shipping to cash, by model? How much stock is more than 90 days old?
5. What share of revenue goes to fines and deductions, is it rising or easing, and have any of your stores been closed?
6. What new compliance documents has Temu asked for since the EU finding, at what cost per product, and how many products have you delisted?
7. Are you profitable on Temu US after tariffs, and who pays the tariff?
8. Where else are you growing (SHEIN, AliExpress, TikTok Shop, Amazon), and what would make you cut Temu?
