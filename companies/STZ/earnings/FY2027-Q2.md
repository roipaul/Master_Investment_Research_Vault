## 3. Structural changes: puts and takes

**A. Margin for volume: a new pricing and marketing posture.**
- What changed: beer marketing rose to 9.9% of sales in Q2 (8.1%) and 9.5% in H1 (8.5%), and is guided above 10% for H2 (10-Q; commentary). Net price and mix was zero, with price of US$12.8m offset by mix of US$14.4m (10-Q); the CFO said the plan was always "the lower end of that range" of 1-2%. Beer margin is guided to 37-38% for FY27 against 38.0% in FY26, 39.7% in FY25 and the withdrawn 39-40% target. Veracruz adds about US$75m of depreciation from FY2028.
- Puts: share gains widened from 0.6 to over 0.8 points; in the four weeks to 7 November Constellation's dollars were +1.5% against a category at -1.5% (NielsenIQ, via trade press); management says it had underspent before and that no further step-up is implied (claim); tariff relief funds part of it.
- Takes: the gross-margin gain is partly timing (absorption on the inventory rebuild) and partly a tariff change that helps every importer; G&A is rising too; zero price against beer inflation of about 2% (Citi, via trade press) gives up the easiest profit in the business; 37-38% less 90bp for Veracruz is 36.1-37.1% before offsets (computed), close to the proposed 36% line.
- Net effect: against the withdrawn 39-40% target, a margin of 37.5% is about 2 points lower, about US$166m of operating income and about US$0.78 a share (computed from the US$0.39 per point). One point of forgone price on US$8.35bn of beer sales is about US$83m a year. The base case in section 6 holds the margin at 37.5% through FY2030.
- Updates: legs 1 and 2. Confidence: high that the posture is deliberate; low on the return. Confirms: depletions turning positive with marketing held near 10%. Reverses: marketing above 10% for FY2028 with volumes still falling.

**B. The portfolio inside beer is shifting from the two large brands to the next two.**
- What changed: Modelo Especial and Corona Extra fell about 2% and 5% for a second quarter; Pacifico and Victoria grew 19% and 15%. Mix by package type cost US$17.8m of gross profit in Q2.
- Puts: management says Modelo Especial's distribution is about 20% below domestic competitors and Pacifico's and Victoria's about 80% below comparable brands (commentary; claims); Pacifico is now a top-ten brand.
- Takes: the two falling brands are the bulk of the volume (their share is not in the documents read); package shifts lower the mix; Corona Extra has fallen in every quarter on file (about -7% in FY26).
- Net effect: not sized. Each point of beer volume is about US$83m of net sales.
- Updates: leg 2. Confidence: medium. Confirms: Modelo Especial at flat or better.

**C. Capital allocation: the build is ending and the cash is going to shareholders.**
- What changed: capex US$354m in H1 and about US$800m for FY27 (US$875m in FY26; US$1,214m in FY25). Veracruz is 85% complete; the 10-Q says "We now expect commercial production at the Veracruz Brewery to commence at the beginning of Fiscal 2028." Buybacks of US$530m to September; diluted shares -3.0%.
- Puts: free cash flow of US$1.6-1.7bn covers about US$0.7bn of dividends 2.4 times (computed) and leaves about US$0.95bn a year, about 4% of the market value; September purchases were at US$123.48.
- Takes: no FY2028 capex figure; using the authorisation by February 2028 needs about US$150m a month against about US$76m so far (computed); the Sands family sold 0.27% of the company at US$145 and has filed to sell more (press); net debt is about 3 times, with US$1.8bn due in FY2028, US$600m of it already rolled into commercial paper.
- Net effect: buybacks of about US$1.0bn a year retire about 4% of the shares at today's price; this is the whole of H1's EPS growth.
- Updates: legs 4, 5 and 6. Confidence: high on direction; medium on pace.

**D. Portfolio and cost.** SpikedAde takes the company into spirits-based ready-to-drink products for a small initial price and a large earn-out; Nelson's Green Brier and the New Zealand brands leave. The chief executive said most value will come from beer. A multi-year cost programme is promised without a target. Net effect: small. Updates: leg 3. Confidence: medium.

- They agree with Constellation that the category is soft and price is thin. They disagree on timing: each saw Q3 worse than the first half, while Constellation describes September as better. The spirits companies show the weakness is in all alcohol, not beer alone.

**Phase: Trough** for Constellation's own volumes, with the US beer industry still in a Downturn that has eased in the latest four-week data. Direction: flat to slightly up. No earlier phase is on file to compare with.
- For Trough: depletions have run between +0.6% and -0.6% for three quarters after about -3% for three. Distributor stock has been rebuilt. Scanner data show the category's volume decline easing from -5.9% to -3.2% and Constellation's dollars turning positive.
- Against: the stable readings sit on comparisons of -2.6% to -3.0%; the two largest brands are still falling; Mexican imports fell 14% in September; distributors' buying intentions for imports are lower than a year ago; every peer got worse through the quarter.

**Earnings against mid-cycle.** Margin is slightly below the five-year range in FY27, by choice. Whether volumes are below mid-cycle is the unresolved question.
- Structural if: the Hispanic consumer does not come back (Bank of America puts Hispanic consumers at about 40% of Constellation's US beer business and ties the weakness to immigration enforcement; the company stopped giving its zip-code comparison this quarter); moderation is the driver (Gallup's 54% of Americans who drink is the lowest since 1939; US beer shipments are heading for a fifth annual fall, per Citi); or spirits-based drinks keep taking occasions (AB InBev's "beyond beer" revenue is up low-teens, and Constellation has bought its way in). The documents read hold nothing on hemp drinks.
- Cyclical if volumes follow the consumer: the 10-Q blames sentiment and value-seeking; management tied the spring slowdown to petrol prices up more than 50%; NielsenIQ puts 92% of the early-November decline down to weaker shopper demand, not lost distribution; on-premise purchasing is up.
- What separates them: depletions growing against a normal comparison once fuel prices and sentiment ease, with Modelo Especial positive. If the consumer improves and beer does not, it is structural.

**What the price assumes.** At 12 times FY27 earnings and a free-cash-flow yield of about 7%, the dividend (2.9%) plus buybacks (about 4% a year) give about 7% a year with no growth and no re-rating. So the price assumes roughly flat volumes at today's margin. A higher return needs volume and price each growing 1-2%, the margin holding after Veracruz, and a multiple above 12.

**Next period (Q3, September-November).** Confirms: depletions positive against the -3.0% comparison; shipments below depletions by several points; Modelo Especial flat or better; net sales per case not negative; the beer margin inside 34.5-35.5%. Phase wrong (still Downturn): depletions negative against that comparison; Modelo Especial worse than -2%; negative price and mix; scanner volume back near -5%. Phase wrong the other way (Early recovery): depletions +2% or better with the guide raised.

**Read-across.** The Midwest aluminium premium was 112 c/lb on 4 November against about 88-90 a year ago (trade press), in line with [[aluminium-cycle]]. Since April 2026 imported beer has been outside the Section 232 scope, which cut Constellation's tariff cost by US$24.1m in Q2, while Molson Coors, a domestic can buyer, faces about US$130m more premium cost in 2026. The premium that supports [[AA]]'s US earnings now falls on domestic brewers and not on importers; a lower Canadian rate would narrow that gap. On the consumer, [[us-housing-channel]] records Home Depot growing 2.2% on ticket with transactions down 0.5%. Beer shows the same pattern with less pricing power: volumes down 3-5% and Constellation's price and mix at zero. The US macro note scores the economy Hot on growth and holds no consumer-confidence or fuel series, so it cannot separate a squeezed low-income consumer from a shrinking category.

The price implies an outcome between the conservative and the base case on earnings, with no re-rating: at an unchanged 12 times the base case returns about 8% a year and the conservative case about 4% (computed).

**Fresh money.** Someone meeting Constellation today gets the largest share gainer in US beer and its largest brand by dollars, a first-half beer margin of 39%, a brewery build that is nearly finished, and about 7% of the market value in free cash each year, at 12 times earnings. They also get volumes that are flat on easy comparisons in a category falling 3-5%, a new chief executive spending margin and price to hold them, and net debt of about 3 times. What has to be true: volumes at least flat; the margin holding near 37-38% after Veracruz; the cash going to buybacks at sensible prices; the multiple at least holding. The evidence supports the third and, so far, the second. On the first it supports no worse, not yet growing: depletions are stable only against falls of about 3%, and the second half is guided down. On the fourth, the multiple has risen two turns in seven weeks on a change in sentiment. The harsh case costs about 22%, and in it leverage would rise above the 3.0 times target and put the buyback at risk. The base case is about four points short of the hurdle.

What would move it to Don't own: a falsifier above; depletions negative against the -3.0% comparison; a FY2028 guide below US$11.
