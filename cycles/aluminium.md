# Aluminium cycle note

Covers [[000807-SZ]] (Yunnan Aluminium) and [[AA]] (Alcoa). The cycle is a spread: the metal price against alumina and power. The fixed indicators are in `cycles/index.md`. The running evidence log for the theme is [[aluminium-cycle]]; this note scores the fixed indicators against their own history and rolls up the names. Everything here is an observation or Claude's read. Nothing is a forecast, and nothing here is Paul's view.

## Phase log

- 2026-11-26: **Rolling over** for aluminium (first cycle note; the same phase as [[AA]]'s Q3 earnings update, which dated the Peak to Q2 2026). Alumina: **Trough** inside China, **Early recovery** outside it. Claude's read, proposed.

## Read of 2026-11-26 (first build)

### Bottom line

- **Phase: Rolling over.** The metal price has fallen about 13% in three months and futures are below a year ago, but producers are still earning far above any earlier level on file: SMM's average profit was RMB 7,557 a tonne in October against about RMB 5,000 a year before.
- **The indicators disagree, and that is the main finding.** Price says the cycle has turned. Inventories say the physical market is still short: China's social stocks halved in three months to a five-year seasonal low. The two have moved in opposite directions since August.
- **Both names are over-earning by more than a quarter.** Alcoa by about 58% on EBITDA against its 2018-2025 average. Yunnan Aluminium by about 140% against the only three years on file; that base is short and may understate normal.
- **Alumina is two markets.** Inside China the price is about RMB 100 below the industry's average full cost and stocks are building. Outside China the price has risen 16% in three months. Yunnan Aluminium buys the first; Alcoa sells the second.
- **Not read:** Yunnan dry-season power orders, China operating capacity for October, and any ten-year range for these series.

### 2. Phase: Rolling over

Previous phase: none in a cycle note. [[AA]]'s Q3 earnings update (written 2026-11-26) read Rolling over from a Peak in Q2 2026, and this note agrees.

For Rolling over:
- The metal is down 13% from the August high in Shanghai and 23% from the Q2 average in London; London three-month is now below a year ago (rows 1 and 1a).
- China's average profit per tonne has come off the August record (row 4), and costs are rising into the dry season [11].
- Supply outside China is returning: Gulf output rose from 306-325 kt in September to 352 kt in October (row 6), and a 480 kt Indonesian smelter has been registered for London delivery [2].
- Demand readings in China are soft: the aluminium-processing PMI was 48.9 in October and exports of unwrought aluminium and semis fell 9.5% from September [6]. SMM puts part of the recent fall down to macro: markets pricing a US rate rise in December and a ten-year Treasury yield above 5.1% [20].
- Alumina outside China is rising (row 2), which narrows the spread for smelters that buy it at the international price.

Against (signs that it is still at the Peak):
- Inventories. China's stocks have halved since August to a five-year seasonal low, and London stocks are down 56% this year (row 7). Spot aluminium trades RMB 500 to 900 above the futures in China [2] and cash was far above three months in London. A market in surplus does not usually look like this.
- Every tonne of Chinese capacity was profitable in October, and average profit is still about half as high again as a year ago (row 4).
- China cannot add tonnes: output is flat at the cap (row 5).
- Chinese alumina is priced below its average full cost with stocks building (row 2), so the cost side of a Chinese smelter's spread is not under pressure.

**The disagreement.** Price and inventories have gone opposite ways for three months. Either the price is looking ahead to supply that has not arrived, or stocks are being drawn for a reason that does not last. This note does not say which. It is the thing to watch.

**Alumina.** Inside China: Trough. The index is below average full cost, total stocks rose from 7.27 Mt on 3 September to 7.84 Mt on 26 November, and refineries run at 76% of 119.6 Mt of built capacity [7][8][10]. Outside China: Early recovery, as [[AA]]'s earnings file reads it, with Western Australia FOB at US$406 against US$350 three months ago. The gap between the two is now about RMB 700 a tonne at Chinese ports, on SMM's figures of 12 November [21].
