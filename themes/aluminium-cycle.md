# Aluminium cycle

Primary aluminium and its inputs: the London metal price and regional premiums (US Midwest, Rotterdam, Japan), the alumina price and the spread between the two, exchange stocks, China's 45 Mt capacity cap and output against it, new capacity outside China (Indonesia, the Gulf restarts), and trade policy (the US Section 232 tariff). Bears on [[AA]] and [[000807-SZ]]; peers read are Norsk Hydro, Rio Tinto, Century Aluminum, South32 and Chalco.

Phase as read on 2026-11-26 (Claude's read, proposed): **Rolling over** for aluminium, from a Peak in Q2 2026; **Early recovery** for alumina, from a Trough in Q2 2026.

## Evidence log

- 2026-11-26 — Prices, read for [[AA]] Q3 2026. **The metal peaked in the second quarter of 2026 and the forward price has fallen faster than the spot price.** London aluminium averaged US$3,571 in Q2 and US$3,226 in Q3 (Rio Tinto's quarterly reviews), against US$2,618 in Q3 2025. On 24 November 2026 the three-month price closed at about US$2,780 while cash was about US$3,190; the gap reached US$405 on 23 November, reported as the widest since 1988, and the exchange tightened its lending rules the next day (trade press). London stocks were 220 thousand tonnes on 18 November against 546 thousand a year earlier, and Alcoa puts global cover at 42 days. So the physical market is still short while the curve prices a surplus.
- 2026-11-26 — **Forecasters see the 2026 deficit turning into a 2027 surplus.** 2026: deficits of 0.7-1.0 Mt (CRU, Wood Mackenzie, StoneX, S&P, Norsk Hydro). 2027: from a 0.2 Mt deficit (S&P) to surpluses of 0.2-1.4 Mt (Bank of America, Wood Mackenzie, StoneX, CRU, Hydro). The swing is Gulf smelters restarting after the 2026 conflict (EGA had half its cells back by late October and expects full output in Q1 2027; CRU has Middle East output rising from 4.8 Mt to 6.4 Mt) and Indonesia (about 1.5 Mt of new capacity in 2027 on Alcoa's figure; CRU 3.2 Mt of output). Alcoa's chief executive would not say whether the Gulf restarts can be supplied through Hormuz on time.
- 2026-11-26 — **China is producing above the cap.** Output is tracking 45.8-46.5 Mt in 2026 against the 45 Mt cap (NBS data via trade press; Alcoa: about 45.8 Mt). Alcoa's chief executive: the cap is "more of a policy tool to set a limit than a firm cap". This bears on [[000807-SZ]], whose primer treats the cap as binding and names its enforcement as a thing to watch.
- 2026-11-26 — **Alumina has turned up from a low, outside China only.** FOB Australia averaged US$307 in Q2 and US$348 in Q3 and was US$398 on 18 November (S&P Global; trade press). Outside China supply is constrained (Rio's Yarwun down 40% from Q4 2026; Hydro's Alunorte cut to half in August, since restored; Alcoa's Western Australia mine approvals slipping into 2027). Inside China refining is "near break-even" (UBS on Chalco, via trade press), China became a net exporter in August, and forecasters see a 2026 surplus of 1.3-1.5 Mt. For [[000807-SZ]], which buys about three-quarters of its alumina, the spread that made 2026 is narrowing from the alumina side; for [[AA]], which is long alumina, the Alumina segment lost money in each of the first three quarters of 2026 at realised prices of US$324-353 and should turn positive at US$390-400.
- 2026-11-26 — **The US tariff is a large part of Western smelters' 2026 profit and is under negotiation.** Section 232 has been 50% since June 2025. The Midwest duty-paid premium averaged US$2,420 a tonne in Q3 2026 (US$1,731 in the second half of 2025) and peaked at US$1.19 a pound in May. Rio Tinto paid US$430m of tariff in Q3 on its Canadian metal; Alcoa paid US$571m in 2025 and has not given a 2026 figure. Talks to cut the rate on Canadian metal to 25% collapsed on 21 August and revived after the November midterms (press). A July proclamation gives half-rate import allowances to companies with approved US smelter plans; Century (Oklahoma, with EGA, hot metal by end-2029) qualifies. Premiums elsewhere are falling: Japan's Q4 premium was set 35% lower at US$255; European duty-paid fell to US$470-500 in mid-November.
- 2026-11-26 — **Producers' results agree: smelting earnings at or just past a high, Q4 guided lower on price.** Calendar Q3 2026: Alcoa's Aluminum segment EBITDA US$862m (Q2 a record US$1,073m; a year earlier US$307m) and Alumina segment -US$1m; Norsk Hydro Aluminium Metal NOK 7.0bn (a year earlier NOK 2.7bn) and Bauxite & Alumina NOK 0.3bn (NOK 1.3bn); Century adjusted EBITDA US$332m, Q4 guided to US$275-300m; Chalco Q3 profit +59% (trade press). [[000807-SZ]] earned almost twice its 2025 profit in the first nine months of 2026 (its primer).
- 2026-11-26 — [[AA]] Q3 2026 (results of 2026-10-15). Adjusted EBITDA US$789m (Q2 US$901m); metal prices took US$235m off the quarter and production costs gave back US$119m. Alcoa is buying South32's Worsley, Hillside and Brazilian stakes for US$4.1bn up front plus up to US$750m, closing in the first half of 2027, financed with US$2.6bn of notes at 6.6-6.9%: a large producer adding capacity and debt at what looks like the top of the cycle, while South32 exits. On Alcoa's own sensitivities each US$100 on the London price is US$237m of EBITDA a year and each US$10 on alumina US$43m net. Alcoa's shares, at US$35.07 on 25 November, are 58% below their June high.
- 2026-11-26 — Cycle note built: [[aluminium]] (`cycles/aluminium.md`) scores the eight fixed indicators against three months and a year ago. Phase unchanged at Rolling over. New readings: China's social inventories 428 kt on 26 November, a five-year seasonal low and half the level of late August, while Shanghai futures are 13% below the August high; SMM's average smelting profit RMB 7,557 a tonne in October against a record RMB 8,208 in August (Mysteel) and about RMB 5,000 a year earlier; the SMM alumina index (RMB 2,640) is below October's average full cost of RMB 2,764 while FOB Western Australia is US$406.
- 2026-11-27 — [[STZ]] Q2 FY2027 (results of 2026-10-06), read from the buyer's side of the can. Imported beer left the Section 232 aluminium scope in April 2026, which cut Constellation's beer tariff cost by US$24.1m in the quarter to August (10-Q). Domestic brewers still pay the Midwest premium: Molson Coors puts it at about a US$130m headwind for 2026 (Q3 release and call, 2026-11-05), and trade press had the premium at 112 c/lb on 4 November against about 88-90 a year earlier. So the premium that lifts US smelting earnings at [[AA]] now lands on domestic can buyers and not on importers; a lower rate on Canadian metal would narrow that gap. Constellation hedges at least 90% of its commodity exposure for the year (CFO on the call, third-party transcript).
