## 2. "Help me compare the Cloud revenue run rate between the three hyperscalers and also compare their margin profile" (approx. 2026-09-03)

Here's the comparison on a segment-reported basis (June 2026 quarter for all three), with the caveats that matter for apples-to-apples.

| | **AWS** | **Microsoft (Azure / Intelligent Cloud)** | **Google Cloud** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Latest qtr revenue | $42.2B | Intelligent Cloud $39.3B; Azure not disclosed (est. ~$28–30B) | $24.8B |
| YoY growth | +36.7% | IC +32%; Azure +43% | +82% |
| Annualized run rate | $169B (company-stated) | Azure ~$115–120B (est.); IC ~$157B; Microsoft Cloud ~$237B | ~$99B |
| Growth trajectory | Accelerating 5 straight qtrs: 17→20→24→28→37% | Azure 40→39→40→43%, guided ~45% cc next qtr | 63% → 82% |
| Segment op income | $16.6B | IC $16.0B | $8.8B |
| Segment op margin | ~39% | IC 40.6% | 35.6% |
| Margin change YoY | +650bps | Flat (40.6% vs 40.6%) | ~+15pts (from ~21%) |
| Gross margin | n/d | IC ~57% (from ~60%); Microsoft Cloud 66% for FY26, down | n/d |
| Backlog | $496B | Commercial RPO $678B (+84%; +25% ex-OpenAI) | $514B; just over half converts within 24 months |
| Capex / FCF context | ~$220B capex; trailing FCF -$7.6B | ~$175B FY27 capex; FCF +$19.6B in qtr | $195–205B capex; FCF -$5.9B in qtr |

Sources for the key figures: AWS did $42.23B with $16.62B of operating income and an annualized run rate of $169B, with AI and chips each above $25B run rate; AWS margin expanded to 39%, up 650bps YoY, and now supplies 21% of Amazon's revenue but 60.5% of its operating income. Google Cloud's operating income rose from $2.8B to $8.8B while margin went from ~21% to ~36%, with growth of 82% following 63% in Q1. Microsoft's Intelligent Cloud posted $39.3B revenue and $15.96B operating income in Q4 vs $29.9B and $12.1B a year earlier; Azure grew 43% and surpassed $100B for the year, up 41%, with ~45% cc guided for Q1. Microsoft Cloud gross margin fell to 66% on AI infrastructure investment and product usage, partly offset by efficiency gains.

**How to read it**

1. **Scale vs. slope.** AWS is still ~1.5x Azure and ~1.7x Google Cloud in dollars, and it's adding roughly the same sequential dollars as Google Cloud (~$4.7B vs ~$4.8B q/q). Google's slope is steeper because of the base; Evercore flagged that nobody is matching Google's pace of sequential dollar growth, raising share-shift questions. On a two-year view Google closes the gap fastest; on a one-year view AWS's re-acceleration is the bigger surprise.

2. **Margins converged in the high-30s/low-40s — but the composition differs.** AWS's expansion is operating leverage plus efficiency on an installed base. Google's is the most dramatic — a business that spent years near breakeven is now at 36%, helped by the TPU supplier margin staying in-house. Microsoft is the outlier: gross margin is compressing (Intelligent Cloud ~57% from ~60%, Microsoft Cloud 66% from ~69%) as depreciation mounts, but opex discipline held segment op margin flat. That's the cleanest signal that AI-infra depreciation is a real drag, and Microsoft's 15→25-year useful-life extension from FY27 will flatter it going forward.

3. **Comparability caveats.** Google Cloud includes Workspace (SaaS margin, ~15–20% of segment). Microsoft's Intelligent Cloud includes on-prem server products and GitHub/Nuance, so Azure's standalone margin is unobservable; the ~$28–30B Azure quarterly figure is my inference from "$100B+, +41%" with Q4 at 43%. Server depreciation lives differ (Amazon 5–6 years; Google extended to 6 in 2023; Microsoft moving DC shells to 25). And company-level op margin varies: Amazon 13.7%, Microsoft 45%.

4. **Quality of the run rate.** The concentration is the thing to underwrite: Barclays estimates Anthropic and OpenAI are ~73% of Amazon's AI revenue in 2026–27, and UBS has Anthropic at 21% of 2026 and 44% of 2027 Google Cloud revenue. Microsoft's RPO growth is 84% with OpenAI and 25% without. So the marginal growth at all three is disproportionately two counterparties; Microsoft has the most diversified installed base and the most FCF, Google the most concentrated incremental growth.

5. **Where the margin risk sits next.** Amazon and Meta both raised capex on memory cost, and Microsoft guided FY27 op margins down slightly. The depreciation wall hits in 2027–28 as 2025–26 GPU vintages roll through; whoever has the most pricing power (all three are supply-constrained, so all currently have some) and the most non-lab demand will defend margin best. On that screen: Microsoft > AWS > Google Cloud on durability, Google Cloud > AWS > Azure on incremental slope.

Net: if you want the cleanest margin *level* with the least concentration, it's Microsoft; the cleanest margin *trajectory*, Google; the best combination of scale, re-acceleration, and margin expansion, AWS.

## 3. "What are some system of record companies that worth a deeper dive? And then, should we do a deep dive on Google?" (approx. 2026-09-09)

### Systems of record worth a deeper dive

The screen I'd use: owns canonical data *and* governed workflow, has disclosed real AI/agent revenue, and either (a) trades at a reset multiple or (b) has proven it can re-price from seats to agents. Ranked by how much a deep dive could change the answer:

| Name | Why now | What the dive has to settle |
|---|---|---|
| **Adobe** | ~12x forward, cheapest large-cap software; AI-first ARR tripled to >$500M; -31% YTD; CFO departed | Is the creative-asset SoR defensible when generative models attack the core function itself? This is the only SoR where the model *is* the substitute, not the agent. Highest asymmetry, highest risk of being right for the wrong reasons. |
| **Intuit** | -50% YTD; FY27 guide only +9–10%; 17% workforce cut; Enterprise Suite ARR $145M (4x) | Does an agent do the bookkeeping *inside* QuickBooks (Intuit wins) or *instead of* it (Intuit loses)? Small-business ledgers plus tax filing are about as canonical as data gets; the market is pricing displacement. |

| **SAP** | ERP is the deepest SoR; cloud +24%, current cloud backlog +26%; trimmed op-profit on M&A; S/4HANA end-of-maintenance forces migrations through 2027 | Least seat-exposed (transactional, not per-user) and lowest multiple of the majors. Salesforce is the reference case (Agentforce >$1.5B ARR, Claudeforce, cRPO +14%), but after the +40% August move it's less contrarian — I'd do it last, focused on cRPO-to-revenue conversion and Agentforce unit economics.

If you want a sequence: Adobe and Intuit first (cheapest, most hated, clearest tests), Workday third, then SAP and ServiceNow as quality anchors, Veeva as the control. That gives you both tails of the seat-vs-agent debate with real numbers on each.

### Google: yes — and it's the best single deep dive on the board

Google is the one name where every thread we've pulled converges, and where the bull and bear cases are both strong and quantifiable:

- **Bull:** Cloud +82% with $514B backlog; the only hyperscaler with owned custom silicon at scale (Broadcom's TPU business is Google, with Anthropic going 5GW in 2027); 3 quadrillion tokens a month, 7x YoY, and a TPU cadence now at two platforms a year; Search still +19%; Waymo at 500k rides/week; the AdX breakup remedy rejected. Lohmeyer's "co-designed stack from model to silicon" is the structural claim, and the CPU/Axion piece is a second leg.
- **Bear:** Negative FCF for the first time since IPO, an $84.75B equity raise, capex $195–205B and rising again in 2027, and the most concentrated incremental growth of the three (UBS: Anthropic 21% of 2026 and 44% of 2027 Cloud revenue). Token growth of 7x against revenue growth of 82% implies steep unit deflation. And the market's message on July 22 was clear: it's charging Google for the funding gap.

That makes it the cleanest test of the question this whole exercise has circled: does a co-designed, vertically integrated AI stack earn a return on $200B a year, or is Google financing Anthropic's compute with its own equity?

I'd scope it in your pitch format:

1. **Thesis** — valuation gap (SOTP with Cloud as the swing) plus compounding via Cloud margin (36% and rising) and TPU economics.
2. **Business** — Search/YouTube cash engine, Cloud (GCP vs Workspace split), TPU as merchant-adjacent supplier, Waymo; the co-design flywheel across cycles.
3. **Management** — capital allocation under Pichai/Ashkenazi: the equity raise decision, useful-life policy, buyback vs capex.
4. **Situation** — FCF crossover, customer concentration, AI Mode/agentic commerce and search monetization, antitrust residue.
5. **Return drivers** — Cloud share gains, TPU external sales, per-token cost curve vs pricing, Waymo optionality.
6. **Open questions** — Anthropic dependency and IPO effects; TPU margin vs Broadcom's take; what happens to Search when agents transact.
7. **Risks / what to track / what breaks it** — Cloud backlog conversion, Anthropic share of Cloud revenue, FCF path, token price realization.

## 4. "Given what we learned, how would you list the priority of each of the companies to dive deeper into?" (approx. 2026-09-27)

Ranking logic: expected value of the dive = how likely the market is mispricing it × the payoff if you're right × how much a dive can actually resolve — with a bonus for a dated catalyst. Friday's tape (10-year at 4.96%, Micron/Lam/Nvidia down 2–6% on record fundamentals) pushes the self-funded, contracted names up the list because the derating is creating the entry, not destroying the thesis.

### Tier 1 — do now, in this order

1. **Micron** — Dated catalyst (Sept 30), expectations reset (24% off highs, 10x), and three suppliers now saying 2027 is tighter than 2026. The dive must settle whether the moderating rate of price increases is a peak signal or a plateau, whether the $100B SCAs de-commoditize the cycle, and what >$45B FY27 capex does to 2028. SK Hynix is the cross-check, not a separate dive.
2. **Alphabet** — Every thread converges here: TPU/Broadcom, Anthropic concentration (21%→44% of Cloud), 7x token growth against 82% revenue growth, negative FCF and an equity raise, Search intact. The one name where a SOTP plus Cloud unit economics could move you from "hate the capex" to "own the stack." Highest complexity, highest payoff.
3. **Broadcom** — Lagging leader (+6% YTD vs sector +15%), six labs on custom XPUs, FY27/FY28 contracted and power-constrained rather than fab-constrained. Dive resolves customer concentration, Marvell's share of Google, and whether $230B in FY28 is a revenue plan or a supply plan.
4. **Lam Research** — Twenty-year-high GM with a path to mid-50s, share gains to >36% of WFE, DRAM capex $60B→$100B+, clean rooms limiting the pace (which stretches the cycle). Cheapest way to own the memory tightness without the memory-price risk. AMAT is the value alternative if you want the lower multiple; do Lam first for the share-gain story.
5. **Microsoft** — The FCF-positive hyperscaler with the most disciplined capex framing and the only large SoR that's articulated a seat-plus-consumption pricing model. Dive: Copilot gross margin trajectory, Azure supply-driven guide, and whether the 15→25-year useful-life change flatters FY27.

### Tier 2 — next, the contrarian and consumption set

6. **Adobe** — Cheapest large-cap SoR (~12x), AI ARR tripled, CFO gone, and the only SoR where the model itself is the substitute. Binary; worth resolving.
7. **Intuit** — -50% YTD, a public strategic reset, and canonical small-business data. Does the agent do bookkeeping inside QuickBooks or instead of it?
8. **Datadog** — Non-AI base re-accelerated to high-20s, AI-native cohort at 750, GRR high-90s, build-vs-buy churn-and-return. The August concentration scare is now the entry, if the breadth holds.
9. **Workday** — The purest seat-compression test; $600M AI agent revenue, >25% of new ACV. Resolve whether digital-workforce pricing can replace per-employee fast enough.
10. **Marvell** — Oct 6 investor day is the catalyst; FY27+FY28 outlook $30B, connectivity +70%, custom GM dilution, CXL as DRAM substitute. Cheaper than Broadcom on growth, riskier on share.
11. **Snowflake** — The consumption winner that just admitted AI weakens lock-in. Dive: NRR durability at 126% and what "moving up the stack" costs.
12. **TSMC** — Highest certainty, least controversy; a confirmatory dive that sets the baseline for everything above. Mid-October Q3 is the checkpoint.

### Tier 3 — conditional or watch

13. **ServiceNow** — Quality benchmark; AI ACV $1.5B target, CRM doubling. Dive only if Tier 2 makes you want a quality anchor at a full multiple.
14. **AMD** — $70B DC in 2027, CPU TAM re-sized to $220B, but 160x+ and an explicit MI450 margin caveat. Wait for the margin print.
15. **Amazon** — AWS re-acceleration and margin expansion are real; FCF negative and Quick is a new app business. Second-priority hyperscaler after Microsoft and Alphabet.
