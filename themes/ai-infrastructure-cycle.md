# AI infrastructure cycle

Where the AI data-center build stands: accelerators, memory, foundry, hyperscaler capex and its funding. Bears on [[NVDA]] and on the memory names (SK Hynix, and through it SK Square on the watchlist).

## Evidence log

- 2026-11-18 — [[NVDA]] FY27 Q3 read: phase **Late cycle**. Demand is still supply-bound: Q4 guide $130B, FY28 about +75%, TSMC and SK Hynix both short of capacity, GPU rental rates rising. Funding is where the strain shows: Big-4 capex was $201.9B in Q3 CY26 (+108% y/y), Alphabet's and Amazon's FCF went negative, and NVIDIA carries $118B of guarantees, $102B of cloud and lease commitments, and $32B of financing-contingent neocloud commitments. Memory is taking margin: SK Hynix ran an 80% operating margin, and NVIDIA's gross margin goes from 74.9% to a 71-72% trough. Full note: [[earnings/FY2027-Q3]].
- 2026-11-19 — [[0700-HK]] Q3 2026 read: a China-side confirmation of the same phase. Tencent's cash capex RMB 65.1bn (+225% YoY) is "at peak levels" and stays there through 2027, declining from 2028; accrual capex RMB 81.4bn; FCF −69.1bn on compute prepayments; first net debt (RMB 13.7bn) in a decade. Memory is taking margin here too: management cited DRAM up ~400% YoY in early 2026 as a core-profit pressure "for the next few quarters", now flattening. Compute pricing "elevated" in 2026-27, expected to fall "significantly" by 2028. New US restrictions on remote access to advanced chips slow training, not inference (domestic chips suffice). Cloud revenue +35% YoY, GPU rental +30% QoQ. Full note: [[earnings/FY2026-Q3]] under 0700-HK.
- 2026-11-19 — [[JD]] Q3 2026 read: memory inflation is reaching consumer electronics prices. JD's CEO cited mobile-phone growth "amidst a broader industry price inflation" in Q3 and "upstream price pressure in consumer electronics" in Q2; JD's inventory days have risen five quarters running (35.8 to 41.2), which may include stocking ahead of price rises (unresolved). Demand-side cost of the memory cycle, to set against [[0700-HK]]'s DRAM complaint. Full note: [[earnings/FY2026-Q3]] under JD.
- 2026-11-24 — [[105560-KS]] Q3 2026 read: the chip boom's second-order effect on Korean credit. The Bank of Korea has raised its base rate twice (2.50% to 3.00%, July and August 2026) on growth it now expects above 3.3% for 2026, with a further rise expected on 2026-11-26. Outside the chip sector, credit is worsening: banks' delinquency ratio was 0.59% in September, the highest for that month in a decade, and the four big banks' non-accrual corporate loans rose 44% in nine months (Korea JoongAng Daily, 2026-11-19). KB's brokerage fees, which rose 150% in nine months on the stock-market rally, halved from Q2 to Q3. A Korean bank's earnings now carry AI-cycle exposure through fees and through the rate path. Full note: [[earnings/FY2026-Q3]] under 105560-KS.
