# China platform base rates: five books, applied to PDD (2026-11-23)

These are base rates from the five books in the claude.ai Project "China Internet". Each is a pattern from what happened before to Alibaba, Tencent, Meituan, JD and the regulators. The note gives summaries and very short quotes only, with no book text reproduced. Two press sources were added to check the 2022 Meituan fee episode. The first part can be reused for any China platform: [[JD]], [[3690-HK]], [[0700-HK]]. The second part applies it to [[PDD]].

## Base rates

**1. Posted fee rises on Chinese marketplaces get reversed. Auction ad prices rise slowly and stick.**
- In May 2006 Taobao launched its first paid ranking product (招财进宝), breaking its promise of three free years. Sellers planned a mass strike. Paipai answered that week with "free for three more years". Taobao put the product to a seller vote; 63% voted against, and it was dropped (腾讯传, Paipai chapter).
- Taobao Mall's 2011 fee rise brought a protest by about 40,000 vendors. The commerce ministry sided with the merchants, and Alibaba gave about RMB 1.8bn of concessions (Clark).
- eBay's listing fees lost China to a free Taobao (Clark).
- Pinduoduo's 2018 penalty for counterfeits froze ten times the order value. Almost 1,000 merchants protested at its Shanghai office, and the protest turned violent (High Wire 7.2).
- **The state can cap a take rate directly.** On 18 February 2022 the NDRC and other departments told food-delivery platforms to cut restaurant fees. Meituan fell 14.9% that day. In March it capped commission at 5% for struggling merchants, and halved it (at most RMB 1 an order) in outbreak areas (NBD; 36Kr).
- **The scrutiny then moves to hidden take.** After Meituan published its fees in 2021, merchants pointed to promotion costs. Average commission per order rose from RMB 5.23 to RMB 5.32 even as headline rates fell (China News).
- **What worked.** Alibaba's monetisation climbed for a decade through auction ads, where merchants bid against each other. It did so in what High Wire calls the light-touch era, roughly 2008–2020 (High Wire ch. 4).

**2. Firms that defy regulators are punished beyond the original issue; firms that yield get leniency.**
- High Wire (ch. 2): a firm seen as uncooperative "could face retaliation" through higher penalties, while firms that give way get lower penalties or immunity.
- Regulators under-act until the leadership sends a signal, then over-act. This is the "volatility" in Zhang's pyramid model.
- Ant is the example: its IPO was halted after Jack Ma's speech.
- So is Didi. It listed against regulators' wishes, a cybersecurity review began two days later, its apps were removed, and it delisted.
- Clark's word for the state is a "multiheaded hydra": many agencies, each with its own rents and turf.

**3. Repair is possible. It takes visible deference and helping the regulator do its job.**
- **Alibaba and the SAIC, 2015.** The SAIC found only 37% of sampled Taobao goods genuine. Alibaba attacked the official by name, and its shares fell about 4% and then 8.8%. Within days Jack Ma flew to Beijing, buried the hatchet in public and promised more people and money against fakes. Anti-counterfeit staff went from 150 to 450. Later that year Beijing became a second headquarters, and Singles' Day showcased Belt and Road countries (Clark). Clark's line: firms cannot remove arbitrary-regulation risk, but they can shield themselves by "helping the government do its job".
- **Tencent and the 3Q war, November 2010.** Tencent forced users to choose between QQ and 360, and the industry already called it the "public enemy". Ma Huateng's letter to staff said that in the past the firm had thought about what was right; now it must think about what others can accept (什么是能被认同的).
  - Tencent held ten "Diagnose Tencent" sessions with 72 critics.
  - It opened its platform within seven months, paying about RMB 4bn of its RMB 20bn 2010 revenue to partners.
  - It turned capital into openness through minority stakes (腾讯传).
- **PDD itself, 2018–19.** When PDD policed fakes, the state backed it. Shanghai courts upheld its 10x penalty clause in 10 of 11 merchant suits, and the Supreme People's Court listed one of those cases among the top ten internet cases of 2019 (High Wire 7.2).

**4. A strategic asset moved outside the listed company stays outside.** In 2009–10 Alipay was moved to a domestic company controlled by Jack Ma for RMB 330m. The reason given was the PBOC's licensing rules. Yahoo and SoftBank said they learnt afterwards, and Yahoo lost about US$3bn of market value in two days. The settlement let the transfer stand and capped compensation at US$2–6bn from a future Alipay IPO. Joe Tsai's defence was that owning all of a business that cannot operate is owning all of nothing (Clark).

**5. Founders keep investing longer than analysts expect, and leave losing ventures through deals rather than write-offs.**
- Tencent ran its own e-commerce against Taobao for nine years, from Paipai in 2005 to QQ Wanggou. More than 60% of Paipai's users came from QQ, and it still lost. In March 2014 Tencent folded the business into JD for a 15% stake, which within months was worth about 18 times the money on paper (腾讯传).
- Alibaba pushed its messaging app Laiwang with everything it had, even making each employee sign up 100 users to qualify for the annual bonus. It lost to WeChat and faded rather than being shut (Clark; 腾讯传).
- Meituan's 2011 winter: it kept its cash while rivals spent on billboards, and showed reporters its bank balance (US$62m). When funding dried up it launched "50 days of all-out fighting" and was number one by November (九败一胜). The disciplined player with cash invests more, not less, when rivals retreat.

**6. Platform versus own inventory is a "competition of ideas", and platforms that bolt on their own inventory have a poor record.**
- Clark frames JD (own stock, own logistics: quality and speed) against Alibaba (asset-light: scale).
- Tencent's own retail arms were folded into JD.
- Zeng Ming's 点线面体 says the platform's job is to make the sellers (the "points") succeed. The scalable form of C2B, in his S2b2c model, is a platform that supplies demand data, design, traffic and finance to makers who own the goods. In his account, Tmall's C2B production-line deals stayed small.

**7. Traffic alone does not make e-commerce.**
- Paipai had QQ's traffic and lost.
- Laiwang had Alibaba's push and lost to a network that was already there.
- Merchants sell on several platforms but keep their store on the incumbent, because buyers trust it more (Clark).

**8. A foreign platform in a hostile host state localises or leaves.** eBay ran China from headquarters and lost; Yahoo China was handed to Alibaba (Clark). Uber China was sold to Didi. In each case host regulators and local rivals set the terms.

With these changes the business verdict stays Inconclusive under the debate rules. The books mainly fatten the tails rather than move the centre.

### Q1. Can domestic profit grow 8–10% a year?
- **The posted-fee route is shut.** Four precedents (Taobao 2006, Taobao Mall 2011, eBay, PDD 2018) show merchants reverse visible fee rises. Since April 2026 the pricing rules also require cost justification and seven days' public notice ([[Layers]]). Growth has to come from auction ad yield, the form that worked for Alibaba.
- **But Alibaba did that under light-touch regulation.** PDD is doing it under an explicit anti-involution signal. High Wire notes the 2022 draft amendment to the anti-unfair-competition law added a "relatively advantageous position" test, which is far easier to prove than dominance. *Check:* whether the revised law in force since October 2025 kept that test.
- **The Meituan precedent.** One line in a multi-ministry document cut a platform's take and took 15% off its shares in a day. Once headline fees were public, attention moved to the promotion take, which is PDD's take.
- **To watch:** any ministry document that steers platforms to cut merchant service, ad or promotion fees.

### Q2. Does the investment phase peak in 2026?
- **Management is unlikely to choose to stop now.** Base rate 5 says founders persist. Meituan's winter says the player with cash presses when rivals pull back. Alibaba and JD are spending on quick commerce and Douyin is turning to monetisation, so from PDD's side this is the time to press. That is what "strategic investment phase" and "build another Pinduoduo" say.
- **So the bull case rests on Temu alone.** Temu's losses would have to close for structural reasons: local warehouses and higher seller fees. The [[Library]] finding that Temu's breakeven date has slid about a year every year fits this base rate.
- **When PDD does exit something,** expect a deal or a quiet fade, not a write-off.

### Q3. Will the state take a material slice?
- **PDD is now the uncooperative firm.** It got the top per-store fine rate and a finding of "violence and soft resistance" (April 2026). High Wire's rule says defiance makes later cases dearer and spreads them across agencies. Ant and Didi mark the tail.
- **On the other side:**
  - The leadership needs private-sector growth, and the climate has eased since 2023 (High Wire ch. 4).
  - PDD fits stated goals: cheap goods in a weak economy, rural supply and exports.
  - The courts backed PDD when it policed fakes.
- **The repair template** is visible deference, help with the regulator's own priorities (food safety, fakes) and symbolism. PDD's 180-plus governance measures and co-chair structure are a start.
- **One contrast (fact; the reading is inference).** After its 2011 B2B fraud, Alibaba removed its CEO and COO. PDD kept co-CEO Zhao Jiazhen in both his roles after the April 2026 decision fined him personally; he has been co-chairman since December 2025.
- **To watch:**
  - State-media tone.
  - Whether Huang or the co-chairs appear at state-convened entrepreneur meetings.
  - The next SAMR case naming PDD, and whether the obstruction language recurs.

### Q4. Cash and governance
- **Fufeitong is the Alipay structure in miniature.** PDD's payment licence sits with two executives; it is small today, with 2025 revenue of RMB 1.06bn and profit of RMB 0.3m. The Alipay case shows how such arrangements end once a licensing rule is cited: in the insiders' favour, with outside holders holding little leverage. The question for management is why it has not been bought in at cost.
- **New Pinmu partners.** Treat any new related-party structure around them the same way. Clark's warning on deals tied to Jack Ma's Yunfeng fund is that each one adds opacity.
- **Tencent's 2011–14 route** (minority stakes, JD at about 18 times in months) shows cash can create value without buybacks. PDD's creed is focus rather than diversification, so that route is not expected.
- **Why founders hoard cash.** Wang Xing's Amundsen speech: carry three tons of supplies for a trip that needs one, because plans go wrong. Expect PDD's management to see its more than RMB 500bn the same way. This supports the bear verdict.

### Own brands (New Pinmu)
- **Two models.**
  - (a) S2b2c / C2B: PDD supplies demand data, traffic, design and finance, while factories own the brand and the stock. That fits Zeng Ming and PDD's 2018 New Brand Plan.
  - (b) PDD owns the brands and the inventory. That is JD's model and Tencent's failed one, and it competes with the merchants PDD is asking to pay more for ads (Q1).
- **The deciding fact is on the balance sheet.** Inventory and prepayments sit either on PDD's books or on its partners'. The model's conservative case already has RMB 55bn of stock and deposits earning nothing.
- **To check in the FY2026 20-F** (about April 2027): the inventory line, any segment disclosure, and related-party partners.

### Temu
- **Temu is now the foreign platform** (base rate 8). Moving to local warehouses and local sellers is the move that record says works. It also monetises lower and later.
- **eBay's fees lost to a free Taobao.** Temu's rising seller fees invite a cheaper rival if one is willing to subsidise. That rival is TikTok Shop. The reported 5–11% commission is unconfirmed by Temu ([[Scuttlebutt]]).
- **Conduct travels.** Under the EU's DSA process, non-cooperation escalates the penalty. The December 2025 conduct at home is the worrying sign for how PDD handles the EU decision due in 2027.

### Q5. Will Douyin stall Pinduoduo?
Traffic alone is not enough (Paipai, Laiwang), and merchants keep their main store where buyers trust it. That supports "no stall". The limit is that Douyin is not a late entrant on borrowed traffic; it has its own content-to-purchase loop. Keep ~60%.

### Culture: the read-across from Tencent
- **The parallel.** In 2006–10 Tencent was called the industry's public enemy for copying rivals and keeping its platform closed. The crisis came from a rival using public opinion, not from regulators. The fix was a change in what management optimised for.
- **PDD's equivalent is the merchant relationship.** The 2024 fee cuts, the RMB 100bn programme and the 申诉优赔 appeals scheme are its "open and share" move. The test is whether merchant economics measurably improve, not the announcements.
- **Meituan's merchant lesson.** In the 2011 winter, fast, semi-automatic payouts won merchants while rivals paid late. PDD's equivalents are deposit levels and payout speed: questions for the merchant calls.
